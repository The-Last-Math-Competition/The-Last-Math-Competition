\section{Exact statement and definitions}
The repository's English statement is:
\begin{quote}
Conjecture: There exists a bijection $\pi$ of $\NN$ such that for every $n$,
$|\pi(n)-n|\in\mathbb{P}\cup\{0\}$ (every displacement is a prime or zero).
\end{quote}
The Chinese statement has the same quantifiers and also permits zero
displacement. Neither version excludes the identity or requires infinitely
many moved points. The assertion is elementary: the identity already proves
it. Below we also give a nonidentity witness; no mathematical novelty is claimed.

We use the usual definition: $p$ is prime if $2\leq p$ and every positive
divisor $d$ of $p$ is either 1 or $p$. In particular, 2 is prime: if
$2=dk$ with $d>0$, then $k\ne0$, so $1\leq d\leq2$ and $d=1$ or $d=2$.
This is the definition used by the Lean predicate \texttt{Prime}, not a
predicate restricted to the witness.

Lean's natural subtraction is truncated. We therefore define
\[
  \operatorname{absDiff}(a,b)=(a\mathbin{\dot-}b)+(b\mathbin{\dot-}a),
  \qquad a\mathbin{\dot-}b=\max(a-b,0).
\]
For $a\leq b$ the sum is $b-a$; otherwise it is $a-b$. Thus it is exactly
$|a-b|$. The formal theorem \texttt{absDiff\_piecewise} proves this distinction.
One direction of truncated subtraction alone would not suffice.

\section{Proof and nonidentity strengthening}
\begin{theorem}
The original conjecture holds. Moreover, it has a nonidentity involutive
solution that moves exactly 1 and 3.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the original assertion alone, the identity $\pi(n)=n$ is a bijection
and has displacement zero at every input. This is a complete proof as written.

For the strengthening, set
\[
 s(n)=\begin{cases}3,&n=1,\\1,&n=3,\\n,&n\notin\{1,3\}.\end{cases}
\]
The three cases show that $s(s(n))=n$ for every natural number $n$.
If $s(a)=s(b)$, applying $s$ gives $a=b$, proving injectivity.
For any target $b$, its preimage is $s(b)$, proving surjectivity.
The displacements at 1 and 3 are $|3-1|=|1-3|=2$, which is prime;
all other displacements are zero. Finally $s(1)=3\ne1$ and $s(3)=1\ne3$,
while every other element is fixed. This proves all claimed properties.
\end{proof}

\section{Whether natural numbers include zero}
Lean's \texttt{Nat} contains zero, and our witness satisfies $s(0)=0$.
For every $n>0$, also $s(n)>0$: the moved points have positive images,
and every other positive point is fixed. The restriction to $\NN_{>0}$
is therefore well defined and remains self-inverse, hence bijective.
Its nonidentity property and displacements are unchanged. A separate Lean
theorem establishes the existential statement on the subtype
\texttt{PositiveNat = \{n : Nat // 0 < n\}}, so both conventions are covered.
