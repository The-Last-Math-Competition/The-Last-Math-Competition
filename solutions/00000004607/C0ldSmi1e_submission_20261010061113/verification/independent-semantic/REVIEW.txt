2. Faithful extraction from both language versions

The original English and Chinese agree that the proposed correction is a
nonnegative function of volume, convex and symmetric, with zeros at exactly
zero volume and total volume. Chinese "两点" and the double-zero parentheticals
support requiring both endpoints to be zeros, rather than merely allowing zeros
there. The report's biconditional for the zero set is therefore faithful. It
does not weaken the zero-set clause to permit other zeros, reverse the sign,
replace convexity with concavity, or assume strict convexity.

For finite positive total volume V, the extracted requirements are a real
function on [0,V], ordinary convexity there, nonnegativity there, and the exact
zero set {0,V}. The final ShapeRequirements declaration adds the usual symmetry
under v -> V-v. The earlier nonexistence theorem does not assume symmetry.
Consequently, the choice of symmetry convention is not a logical dependency
of the contradiction. The word "explicit" is likewise unnecessary to refute
existence in the larger class of all real-valued functions.

The source supplies no formula for "concavity correction." The package does
not invent one or identify it with the profile, a deficit, or a derivative.
ShapeRequirements is openly a name for necessary output properties, not a
purported definition of the missing operation. This is legitimate for a
disproof: any output meeting the original assertion must in particular meet
the extracted properties. Proving that no function meets those necessary
properties refutes the stronger assertion, whatever correction operator might
have been intended. This is not a substitute claim whose truth leaves the
original conclusion possible.

3. Domain and quantifier audit

The final nonexistence theorems quantify over every real V with 0 < V and every
real function f. They impose all relevant conditions only on Set.Icc 0 V.
They do not assume a special curvature, dimension, profile formula, regularity,
minimizer, or normalization. Since V is real, it is finite. Positivity is
explicit and is needed for distinct endpoints and an interior volume.

The source does not expressly specify an ambient space or volume-domain class.
The report correctly identifies its scope rather than claiming that every
isoperimetric space has finite volume. Its flat unit square torus example
provides a standard permitted finite positive setting: total area is 1, the
periodic strips have every area v in [0,1], and their interfaces have finite
length. For interior v the two boundary circles each have length 1. At the
endpoints the empty and full regions are available. These elementary facts
are correct. They establish the availability of all relevant volumes without
requiring a computation of the isoperimetric profile or an attained minimizer.
The report explicitly uses the usual infimum formulation when discussing
profile finiteness; no attainment assertion is smuggled into the formal proof.

The report also correctly explains that ordinary convexity uses a convex
domain. Independently of that convention, the torus example ensures that
discarding interior volumes would change the stated geometric setting. There
is thus a legitimate ordinary setting in which the complete interval is the
volume domain. The finite-positive setting is sufficient to refute the
unqualified assertion and is not an extra hypothesis that makes a purported
proof narrower than the source.

The case V=0 is explicitly distinguished and is not falsely declared
impossible. Negative total volume is not asserted to be geometric. Infinite

in [0,V] when x does. No domain endpoint is omitted or silently identified.

4. Independent mathematical verification

The report's Theorem 1 is correct. For V>0 and v in [0,V], t=v/V lies in
[0,1]. The convex combination (1-t)0+tV equals v. Ordinary convexity bounds
c(v) above by the corresponding endpoint-value combination, which is zero.
Nonnegativity bounds it below by zero. For V=0 the domain is the singleton
{0}, already handled by the endpoint hypothesis. No continuity, differentiability,
or smoothness result is needed.

For V>0, V/2 is strictly between 0 and V. The resulting zero there violates
the prescribed exact zero set. The corollary therefore follows. The displayed
midpoint restatement is mathematically equivalent, and the assertion that
symmetry or explicitness cannot restore existence follows because adding
properties only restricts the class already proved empty.
