Abstract
For every positive exponent parameter, the exponent-badly-approximable set in the conjecture has
full Lebesgue measure in the real line. Its Hausdorﬀ dimension, and that of the intersection at parameters
1/3 and 1/2, is therefore one. The complete argument is formalized in Lean 4.19.0 with Mathlib v4.19.0.

3 One strict constant for every denominator
Fix α > 0 and set s = 2 + α. Choose an irrational x satisfying Lemma 1; this holds almost everywhere
because the rationals are countable. Applying the lemma at C = 1 gives N ∈ N such that
|x − p/q | ≥ q−s for every q ≥ max(1, N ) and every p ∈ Z.
For each ﬁxed q > 0, the closed lattice q−1Z does not contain x, so its distance from x is positive. There are
only ﬁnitely many denominators 1 ≤ q ≤ N . Hence some ε > 0 satisﬁes
|x − p/q | ≥ ε (1 ≤ q ≤ N, p ∈ Z).

These two cases prove the required strict inequality for all numerators and all positive denominators. Thus
IIα has full Lebesgue measure.
4 Hausdorﬀ dimension and the conjecture
A full-measure subset S ⊆ R agrees almost everywhere with R and has inﬁnite Lebesgue measure. In the
normalization used by Mathlib, one-dimensional Hausdorﬀ measure on R equals Lebesgue measure. It follows
that dimH S ≥ 1. Monotonicity and dimH R = 1 give the reverse inequality.
The intersection of two full-measure sets has full measure: its complement is a union of two null sets.
Apply the preceding paragraph ﬁrst to IIβ and then to IIα ∩ IIβ. This proves Theorem 1; substituting
α = 1/ 3 and β = 1/ 2 proves the original equality.
5 Formal correspondence and reproduction
The ﬁle lean/Conjecture302.lean deﬁnes Conjecture302.II exactly as in Section 1.
The theorem mem_II_of_not_liouvilleWith proves the strict uniform-constant argument. The full-
measure statement is ae_mem_II; dimH_II and dimH_inter_II prove the dimension results. The ﬁnal
theorem Conjecture302.conjecture has precisely the requested equality as its type, with no additional
hypotheses.
The measure input is Mathlib’s theorem ae_not_liouvilleWith, whose deﬁnition uses actual integer
numerators, natural denominators tending to inﬁnity, and real approximation errors. Mathlib proves it
from convergent power-series bounds and Borel–Cantelli. Its bounded-denominator separation theorem for
irrational numbers supplies the ﬁnite-denominator step. Dimension is Mathlib’s genuine dimH, valued in
[0, ∞ ], and the proof obtains the exact ﬁnite value one.
