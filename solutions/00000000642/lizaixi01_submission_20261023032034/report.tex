\section{The necessary determinant condition}
The source says the LKB image is ``Markov-invariant at the determinant
level.'' The ordinary, unnormalized determinant already fails this
necessary condition. Work over the actual rational function field
$K=\mathbf Q(q,t)$, with $q,t$ independent indeterminates. Under positive
stabilization from two to three strands, the identity braid becomes
$\sigma_2$. Their representation determinants are respectively
\[
 1 \qquad\hbox{and}\qquad tq^3\ne1.
\]
Thus the stated conjunction is false under its literal determinant
condition. No additional writhe correction or normalization is specified
in the source. We make no claim about what an extra normalization could
achieve, and do not separately settle its Jones-value or kernel assertions.

\section{The standard fraction-field matrix model}
Bigelow, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0204057}
{\emph{The Lawrence--Krammer representation}}, Section 4.2, p.13,
gives the Krammer generator action on the basis $F_{ij}$.
He explicitly states that over $\mathbf Q(q,t)$ this model and the
homological Lawrence--Krammer representation are isomorphic, hence
conjugate as matrix representations. He also warns that their integral
lattices need not be isomorphic. We stay over the generic fraction field
throughout; we do not specialize the representation at numerical parameters.
We use Bigelow's sign convention for $t$.

In the ordered basis $(F_{12},F_{13},F_{23})$, his displayed action gives
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}-tq^2&0&q^2-q\\0&0&q\\0&1&1-q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&q&0\\1&1-q&0\\0&(1-q)qt&-tq^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For two strands, the single generator acts on $F_{12}$ by the
$1\times1$ matrix $(-tq^2)$. Direct calculations, all checked in Lean,
give
\[
 \det A=\det B=tq^3,\qquad ABA=BAB,\qquad \det(-tq^2)=-tq^2.
\]
Since $q,t\ne0$ in $K$, these matrices are invertible.
Lean uses the genuine Artin presentations $B_2$ (one free generator)
and $B_3=\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2\mid
\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2\rangle$.
It proves the relation before constructing actual homomorphisms
$\rho_2:B_2\to\mathrm{GL}_1(K)$ and
$\rho_3:B_3\to\mathrm{GL}_3(K)$ by their universal properties.

\section{A genuine elementary Markov move}
The inclusion $\iota:B_2\to B_3$ adds a straight third strand, sending
the first generator to $\sigma_1$. Positive stabilization is
\[
 b\longmapsto\iota(b)\sigma_2.
\]
This is the standard elementary Markov move: see Bigelow, Ramos and Yi,
\href{https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~bigelow/publications/15.pdf}
{\emph{The Alexander and Jones polynomials through representations of
Rook algebras}}, printed p.1250114-4, moves (2)--(3) preceding Theorem 1.5.
Lean constructs this inclusion as a group homomorphism, defines the
positive move by this very product, and proves that $1$ stabilizes to
$\sigma_2$. Consequently,
\[
 \det\rho_2(1)=1,\qquad
 \det\rho_3(\iota(1)\sigma_2)=\det B=tq^3.
\]
The closed theorem \texttt{not\_positive\_markov\_invariant} negates
invariance over all pairs related by this actual elementary move.
Failure of one allowed move suffices; no full topological Markov theorem
or construction of link closures is needed for this necessary condition.

\section{Independent variables and arbitrary bases}
The formal field is exactly
\[
 K=\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbf Q[X_0,X_1]),\qquad q=X_0,\quad t=X_1.
\]
Injectivity into the fraction field proves $q,t\ne0$. If $tq^3=1$
in $K$, injectivity would imply the polynomial identity
$X_1X_0^3=1$. Applying the polynomial evaluation homomorphism
$X_0=X_1=0$ gives $0=1$, a contradiction. This evaluation is applied
only to an alleged polynomial identity. It is not a specialization of
an LKB representation, and no evaluation on the whole fraction field
is asserted.

For every invertible matrix $P$, Lean proves
\[
 \det(PMP^{-1})=\det(M).
\]
The theorem \texttt{determinant\_changes\_in\_any\_bases} therefore
gives the same unequal determinants after arbitrary and independent
changes of basis in dimensions one and three. This also explains why
the generic conjugate LKB model has the same obstruction. The report
uses the standard matrix model as defined by the primary source;
Lean does not reconstruct configuration-space homology or reprove
Bigelow's topological identification theorem.
