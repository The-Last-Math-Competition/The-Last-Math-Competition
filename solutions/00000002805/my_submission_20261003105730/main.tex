\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} Cameron--Martin rigidity of the Schilder rate:
the rate function vanishes if and only if the path lies in the
Cameron--Martin unit ball, and the spectral distribution of the rate
on the sphere is an explicit chi-square-type law.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Schilder's rate on the Cameron--Martin space
$H_0 = \{w: w(0) = 0 \text{ abs.\ cont.}\}$ is
$I(w) = \tfrac12\int_0^1 |\dot w(t)|^2\,dt = \tfrac12 \|w\|_H^2$.
It vanishes exactly when $\dot w = 0$ a.e., i.e.\ when $w \equiv 0$
--- a single point, \emph{not} the unit ball.  In the coordinate
model $w_c(t) = c\,t$ ($\|w_c\|_H = |c|$, $I = c^2/2$): the path
$c = \tfrac12$ lies \emph{in} the unit ball with $I = \tfrac18 \ne 0$
--- the ``if'' direction fails --- while every $c \ne 0$ has
$I = c^2/2 > 0$ (kernel-certified positivity), so the true vanishing
set is $\{0\}$.

On the sphere $\|w\|_H = 1$ the rate is $I = \tfrac12$ for
\emph{every} point: in 2D Cameron--Martin coordinates
$w = a_1 e_1 + a_2 e_2$ with $a_1^2 + a_2^2 = 1$,
$I = (a_1^2 + a_2^2)/2 = \tfrac12$ identically.  The ``spectral
distribution of the rate on the sphere'' is the point mass
$\delta_{1/2}$ --- not a chi-square-type law, which is
non-degenerate.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies the positivity off the zero path, the in-ball
counterexample, and the constancy on the sphere; the identification
$I(w) = \tfrac12\|w\|_H^2$ (Schilder's theorem proper) is classical,
cited in prose and instantiated by the script.  Both the rigidity
clause and the spectral clause are refuted.
