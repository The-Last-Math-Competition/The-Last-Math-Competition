\begin{center}{\Large Incompatible formulae for circular-ensemble overlaps}\\[5pt]{\large Conjecture 00000001865}\\[5pt]zbw123456 \quad 14 October 2026\end{center}

The conjecture simultaneously asserts a mean
\[
 M(N,\beta,i)=N^{-2+2\beta}\{i(N-i)\}^{\beta-1}
\]
and its specialization $M(N,2,i)=N^{-2}$. These two assertions already contradict each other on their stated domain.
\begin{theorem}
No mean function can satisfy both formulae for every $N\ge2$, $\beta>0$, and $1\le i<N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $N=2$, $\beta=2$, and $i=1$. The first formula gives $M=2^2\cdot1=4$, whereas the claimed specialization gives $M=2^{-2}=1/4$. Since $4\ne1/4$, their conjunction is false. This argument needs no assumptions about the distribution of the matrices.
\end{proof}
There is a separate problem with the stated statistic. Distinct members $v_i,v_j$ of an orthonormal eigenbasis have $\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=0$. Consequently the expectation of $|\langle v_i,v_j\rangle|^2$ is zero under any probability measure on such families. In particular, the overlap of two vectors of the same orthonormal eigenbasis is not the overlap of two independent eigenbases. The circular unitary ensemble at $\beta=2$ consists of unitary matrices and admits orthonormal eigenbases.

No probability model is substituted for the ensemble in the main contradiction.
