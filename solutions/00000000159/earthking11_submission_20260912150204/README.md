# Disproof of conjecture `00000000159`

**Verdict: FALSE.** For `p = 11` the complete graph `K_11` cannot be decomposed
into cycles of pairwise distinct prime lengths: the available prime lengths
`3, 5, 7, 11` total only `26`, while `K_11` has `C(11,2) = 55` edges.

## The conjecture

> **Conjecture.** Decompositions of complete graphs of prime order into distinct
> prime cycles: for every prime `p ≥ 11`, `K_p` can be decomposed into a union
> of cycles of pairwise distinct prime lengths.

Since the conjecture asserts the claim for **every** prime `p ≥ 11`, a single
counterexample refutes it. We exhibit `p = 11`.

## Why it is false

**Lemma (cycle lengths in `K_p`).** A cycle of a simple graph has length equal to
its number of distinct vertices; hence a cycle of `K_p` has length at least `3`
and at most `p`.

*Proof.* A simple graph has no loops or parallel edges, so a cycle uses at least
three distinct vertices. A cycle of `K_p` uses pairwise distinct vertices of the
`p`-element vertex set, so it uses at most `p`. The edges join consecutive
vertices, and distinctness makes edges correspond bijectively to vertices, so
the length equals the number of distinct vertices. ∎

It follows that a prime cycle length in `K_11` lies in the set
`{3, 5, 7, 11}` of primes in `[3, 11]`.

Now let the purported cycles have pairwise distinct prime lengths `L`. Since
`K_11` has

`C(11,2) = 11·10/2 = 55`

edges:

- if the decomposition is **edge-disjoint**, the lengths sum to exactly `55`;
- if the cycles merely **cover** every edge, the lengths sum to at least `55`,
  because the union of the cycles has at most `Σ_{q∈L} q` edges.

So in both readings `Σ_{q∈L} q ≥ 55`. But `L` is a set of pairwise distinct
primes contained in `{3,5,7,11}`, so

`Σ_{q∈L} q ≤ 3 + 5 + 7 + 11 = 26 < 55`.

Contradiction. No decomposition of `K_11` exists, and the conjecture is false.

**The `p = 11` witness, explicitly:** the only available cycle lengths are
`3, 5, 7, 11`, whose sum `26` falls `29` edges short of `C(11,2) = 55`. Note
that the prime `2` is never available, since every cycle has length at least `3`.

## Small primes

`C(p,2)` required vs. the maximal total `Σ_{3 ≤ q ≤ p, q prime} q`:

| `p` | `C(p,2)` | available total | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 55 | 26 | impossible |
| 13 | 78 | 39 | impossible |
| 17 | 136 | 56 | impossible |
| 19 | 171 | 75 | impossible |
| 23 | 253 | 98 | impossible |

`reproduce.py` checks that the inequality fails for **every** prime
`11 ≤ p ≤ 2000` (299 primes, 299 failures, 0 passes). It only gets worse:
`Σ_{q≤p} q ~ p²/(2 ln p)` while `C(p,2) ~ p²/2`, so the ratio tends to `0` and
the obstruction is asymptotic.

## The strongest objection, and its rebuttal

The one way to evade the count is to **weaken the meaning of "cycle"** to
*closed trail* (edges distinct, vertices allowed to repeat). A closed trail in
`K_p` can be longer than `p`, so the bound `|C| ≤ p` would dissolve and the edge
count alone would no longer obstruct the claim.

This escape is not available. In graph theory a *cycle* is a simple cycle; a
closed walk with repeated vertices is a closed trail, not a cycle, and the
conjecture says "cycles". Under the standard reading the lemma applies and the
obstruction is decisive. We state the reading we use explicitly, and we also
note that `2` is never an available cycle length, which only strengthens the
obstruction.

```bash

```

`3 + 5 + 7 + 11 = 26 < 55`; an assertion that the bound fails for all 299

## Scope of the refutation

The graph-theoretic reduction from a decomposition of `K_11` to the arithmetic
statement "there is a list of pairwise distinct members of `{3,5,7,11}` with sum
`≥ 55`" is argued in full in `main.tex`. The Lean development formalises the
arithmetic core of that reduction (the list has sum `≤ 26 < 55`) and documents
the un-formalised bridge in `lean4/README.md`. The formalised statement is not
vacuous: it genuinely excludes every such list from reaching the required total.
