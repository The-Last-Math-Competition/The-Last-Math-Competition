\begin{center}{\Large A zero invariant satisfies the stated existence claim}\\[6pt]
{\large Conjecture 00000001404}\\[5pt]
zbw123456 \quad 18 October 2026\end{center}

Let $\mathcal S$ denote the smooth homology four-spheres in the question.
Define $\theta:\mathcal S\to\mathbb Z$ by $\theta(M)=0$ for every $M$.
The additive group $\mathbb Z$ is countable and abelian.
Diffeomorphic objects have equal values, so $\theta$ is an invariant, and
\[
\theta(M\#N)=0=0+0=\theta(M)+\theta(N),\qquad \theta(S^4)=0.
\]
Thus the requested invariant exists.

The final implication is valid for any invariant with the stated normalization.
If $\theta(M)\ne0$ and $M$ were diffeomorphic to $S^4$, invariance would imply
$\theta(M)=\theta(S^4)=0$, a contradiction. Hence a nonzero value would refute
the universal diffeomorphism assertion printed in the problem.

\textbf{Scope.} The conjecture asks for an invariant and a conditional
consequence of a nonzero value. It does not require such a value to occur.
The zero invariant proves the stated existence claim; it does not settle
the smooth Poincare problem or construct an exotic sphere. No identification
of homology spheres with homotopy spheres is used.

\textbf{Lean correspondence.} The theorem is stronger: for every type $S$,
distinguished element $e$, binary operation, and relation, the constant map
to $\mathbb Z$ satisfies the displayed requirements. These are universally
quantified inputs without assumptions. Substitution of the objects,
connected sum, and diffeomorphism relation requires no geometric theorem.
The separate theorem \texttt{nonzero\_obstructs} proves the conditional for
every invariant. Countability and commutativity of $\mathbb Z$ are also checked.
