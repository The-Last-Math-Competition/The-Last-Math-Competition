\begin{center}{\Large A reversible splitting with nonpalindromic coefficients}\\[6pt]
{\large Conjecture 00000006563}\\[5pt]
zbw123456 \quad 18 October 2026\end{center}

On $\mathbb R^2$, take the two translation flows
\[
A_t(x,y)=(x+t,y),\qquad B_t(x,y)=(x,y+t).
\]
They obey the flow laws and have identity at $t=0$. They are distinct:
$A_1(0,0)=(1,0)\ne(0,1)=B_1(0,0)$.
Consider the alternating splitting
\[
S_h=A_{h/3}\circ B_h\circ A_{2h/3}.
\]
Its coefficient list $(1/3,1,2/3)$ is not palindromic.
Nevertheless direct substitution gives
\[
S_h(x,y)=(x+h,y+h),\qquad
S_{-h}\circ S_h=S_h\circ S_{-h}=\operatorname{id}.
\]
Thus the actual map is time reversible for every real step size while
coefficient mirroring fails. This refutes necessity, hence the asserted
necessary-and-sufficient criterion.

\textbf{Scope.} Reversibility means the standard self-adjoint identity
$S_{-h}=S_h^{-1}$. The problem imposes no noncommutativity or free-generator
assumption and does not require reversibility uniformly over all possible
underlying flows. In a free formal composition algebra, or under that
additional universal requirement, the palindrome criterion is a different
assertion. The witness here uses two distinct commuting flows, nonzero
coefficients, and the actual specified splitting; no zero steps are inserted.
