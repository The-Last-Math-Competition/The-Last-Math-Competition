\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} $\tau(G)$ for random 3-regular graphs is
asymptotically $c_3^n$ with
$c_3 = \left(\frac{\sqrt3-1}{2}\right)^2 \exp\left(\frac{\int
\log(3-2\cos\theta)\,d\theta}{4\pi}\right)$; the numerical limit is
$1.175\ldots$, and the closed form is computable.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

The integral is classical: $(1/2\pi)\int_0^{2\pi}\log(a-b\cos
t)\,dt = \log\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{2}$, so with $a=3$, $b=2$ the
exponential part is $\sqrt{(3+\sqrt5)/2} < 2$ ($\sqrt5 < 5$). The
prefactor $\left(\frac{\sqrt3-1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{2-\sqrt3}{2} <
\frac14$ ($\sqrt3 > 3/2$). Hence the closed form is
\[
c_3 < \tfrac14 \cdot 2 = \tfrac12 < 1,
\]
while the conjecture's own claimed numerical limit is $1.175 > 1$:
writing $c_2 = 2000\,c_3$, the bounds give $c_2 < 1000$ but the claim
gives $c_2 = 2350$ --- absurd. The closed form evaluates to $\approx
0.2168$ (numerically confirmed); the true McKay-type constant is
$\approx 1.216$, so the claimed $1.175$ is also off versus the
literature.
