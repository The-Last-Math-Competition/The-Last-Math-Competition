# Source scope and intended faithful refutation

The exact original says there exists "a graph and its proper subdivision" and does not explicitly state finiteness. The current general graph bundle is finite. Therefore `no_ordered_subdivision_separation` and its unordered analogue alone are not represented here as a refutation of an unrestricted infinite-graph existential. In particular an infinite graph can have finitely many new vertices on every edge but infinitely many new vertices in total; that relation is outside the checked finite-total insertion closure.

The original also asserts that the smallest example is the four-claw graph. Under the ordinary meaning of example, this entails that actual K1,4 has a proper subdivision with the stipulated n=2 inequivalence (and all-n homology condition). Refuting that necessary witness clause refutes the entire original conjunction without restricting the universe of possible original graphs.

An ordinary graph subdivision replaces an edge by a nonempty finite path, with interior vertices fresh and distinct across paths. For the four-claw there are exactly four original edges. Thus every ordinary subdivision is four independent finite paths joined at one common center, and has finitely many inserted vertices. Arbitrary vertex/edge labels and edge orientations are permitted by actual endpoint-pair-preserving bijections, not by any topological-equivalence assumption.

Primary standard-definition check: [Diestel, Graph Theory, sixth edition, Chapter 1](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/home/diestel/books/graph.theory/preview/Ch1.pdf), Sections 1.3 and 1.7. Section 1.3 specifies a path by a finite sequence of distinct vertices and successive edges. Section 1.7 defines subdivision through independent replacement paths. This supports the ordinary finite-path convention; the original source's intent still belongs to independent semantic review. No finiteness wording is inserted into the exact original.

The needed Lean bridge is concrete `CellGraph.replaceEdge` with `Fin k` fresh vertices and edges, plus `path_replacement_is_subdivision`: it proves each actual finite path replacement belongs to the already constructed insertion closure. The four-claw graph has `Fin 5` vertices, `Fin 4` edges, source constantly zero and target `Fin.succ`. Four successive replacements cover all four original spokes. `OrdinaryFourClawSubdivision` then states actual undirected graph identity with this explicit four-path graph, using vertex and edge bijections preserving every unordered endpoint pair. `ordinary_four_claw_subdivision_covered` must derive insertion closure from that structural membership; insertion closure is not a premise of ordinary membership.
