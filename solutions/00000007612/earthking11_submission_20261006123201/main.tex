\paragraph{Claim under test.}
The conjecture asserts, without a parity restriction, that the dimension of
an irreducible Clifford-algebra representation is exactly $2^{n/2}$.

\paragraph{Counterexample.}
Take the complex Clifford algebra on one generator with positive square:
\[
  \mathrm{Cl}_1(\mathbb C)=\mathbb C[e]/(e^2-1).
\]
The map
\[
  \rho(a+be)=a+b
\]
is a unital algebra homomorphism to $\mathbb C$, because
$\rho(e)^2=1$.  Thus $\mathbb C$ is a nonzero one-dimensional
$\mathrm{Cl}_1(\mathbb C)$-module.  A one-dimensional nonzero module has no
proper nonzero subspace, so it is irreducible.

For $n=1$, however, the proposed formula is
\[
  2^{n/2}=2^{1/2}=\sqrt 2\ne 1.
\]
Indeed no natural-number dimension $d$ can satisfy $d^2=2$.  Hence the
claimed dimension law is false.  (The familiar correct complex formula is
$2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ for an irreducible module, with the usual odd/even
qualification on the algebra.)

\paragraph{Formal verification.}
The Lean file encodes $a+be$ as an integer pair, its multiplication modulo
$e^2=1$, and the character $a+be\mapsto a+b$.  It proves multiplicativity,
nontriviality, and the decisive numerical mismatch $1^2\ne2$ for the
explicit one-dimensional representation.  This is the exact finite
algebraic core of the counterexample.
