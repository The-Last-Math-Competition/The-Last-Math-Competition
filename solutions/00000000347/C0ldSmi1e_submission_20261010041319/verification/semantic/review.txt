Source statement and meaning

The two language versions agree: there exists a transcendental alpha whose partial quotient sequence is Sturmian, explicitly with factor complexity n+1. Neither version specifies the starting index. The initial theorem proves the ordinary regular continued-fraction fractional word a1,a2,..., while a0=0. This convention is common and is explicitly disclosed in the report. Nevertheless its full sequence a0,a1,... has three distinct letters, so it does not have complexity 2 at length 1. I raised this as an indexing ambiguity, not as a defect in the fractional-word theorem. RESOLVED: The final sources add a reciprocal witness beta=1/alpha whose complete quotient at index n is exactly alpha's complete quotient at index n+1. Consequently the full partial-quotient sequence of beta is 1+b_t(n), including its integer part, and its whole factor set equals the fractional-word factor set of alpha. Its remainders remain positive by the same shift identity. If beta were algebraic, stock IsAlgebraic.inv would imply alpha is algebraic, contradicting the selected witness. The full report states both conventions and correctly allows the two witnesses to differ. This removes the source ambiguity without an unsupported assertion that deleting an arbitrary first letter preserves factor complexity.

Finite-word argument

WordCombinatorics.lean defines a length-n block as i |-> w(j+i), with i in Fin n, and Factor as the range over all natural starting positions j. Its complexity is the finite cardinality of this actual factor set. No prefixes-only or sample-count definition is substituted. Deleting the last letter is a surjection from (n+1)-factors to n-factors. Equality of the two finite cardinalities makes this map injective, so every n-factor has a unique right extension. Two equal blocks exist at distinct positions by the infinite pigeonhole principle; iterating unique extension makes the suffixes equal. This contradicts Aperiodic, whose meaning here is precisely that no two distinct suffixes agree. Thus complexity strictly increases at every n, including n=0. Starting from the unique empty block gives p(n)>=n+1. The Lean argument follows this reasoning and treats the empty-block case correctly.

Mechanical-word argument

Mechanical.lean uses irrational slopes t with 0<t<1 and letters floor((j+1)t)-floor(jt), proved to lie in {0,1}. Cumulative counts are floor(kt+frac(jt)). Subtracting floor(kt) produces coordinates in {0,1}, each nondecreasing in the fractional starting point. Their sum is an integer rank between 0 and n. Two equal ranks force all coordinates equal because the two coordinate vectors are comparable; hence the cumulative counts and successive letters agree. Choosing a representative occurrence for each factor yields an injection into Fin(n+1), and therefore p(n)<=n+1. The chosen representative introduces no assumption that the rank is well-defined independently of representatives: equal chosen ranks already suffice for injectivity.

If two distinct suffixes at j<l agreed, with q=l-j and integer c=floor(lt)-floor(jt), their cumulative counts imply floor((j+mq)t)=floor(jt)+mc for every natural m. The floor inequalities bound m(qt-c) independently of m. The Archimedean property forces qt=c, contradicting irrationality. Together with the finite-word lower bound this proves p(n)=n+1 for every natural n, not merely asymptotically or on finitely many tested lengths. If two slopes produced the same word, telescoping would give floor(kt)=floor(ks) for all k. Boundedness of k(t-s) then forces t=s. The mechanical-word map is therefore injective.

Continued-fraction realization

CFRealization.lean constructs a real for every infinite binary word. It uses the interval I=[1/3,3/4] and bounded continuous functions from the discrete natural numbers to I, with the uniform metric. This is a nonempty complete metric space; all I-valued sequences are bounded and continuity is automatic on the discrete domain. The map (T r)(n)=1/(d(n)+r(n+1)), where d(n)=1+w(n), preserves I. Indeed 4/3<=d+x<=11/4, so its reciprocal lies in [4/11,3/4], a subset of I. The difference of two reciprocals is bounded by (9/16)|x-y|, so the map is a strict contraction. Stock Mathlib's fixed-point theorem yields tails satisfying the displayed recursion. There is no unproved continued-fraction realization axiom.

The custom completeQuotient definition is exactly the ordinary real floor-and-invert algorithm: x0=x and x(n+1)=1/frac(xn). The partialQuotient is floor(xn). The construction proves floor(alpha)=0 and completeQuotient(alpha,n+1)=1/r(n)=d(n)+r(n+1). Since each tail lies between 1/3 and 3/4, every remainder is strictly positive and below 1, and every subsequent digit is the prescribed d(n). In particular Lean's totalized inverse at zero is never used along these expansions. Recovery of every letter from the deterministic algorithm proves that the realization map is injective. All these facts concern the same constructed real.

Cardinality and transcendence

Proof.lean proves the irrational slopes in (0,1) are uncountable using the uncountability of the real interval and the countability of rational reals. The injection from slopes through their mechanical words to their realized continued-fraction reals is explicit. If every image were algebraic over Q, this would inject the uncountable slope type into the countable subtype of algebraic reals, a contradiction. The inspected stock definitions identify IsAlgebraic Q x with being a root of a nonzero polynomial over Q, and Transcendental Q x with its negation. The argument is an existence proof; no unjustified claim that every slope gives a transcendental real is made.
