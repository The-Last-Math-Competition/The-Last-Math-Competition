\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} $\det(I - tK) = e^{-t^2/4}$ explicitly, for the
Fredholm determinant of the kernel $K(x,y) = e^{-xy}$ on
$L^2(0,\infty)$; it is an entire function of order 2, and its zeros
form conjugate purely imaginary pairs.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

The Fredholm determinant has the series $\det(I - tK) = 1 - t\, \mathrm{tr}\,K + O(t^2)$ (classical: the linear coefficient is minus
the trace), with
\[
\mathrm{tr}\,K = \int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\,dx = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}
\approx 0.8862 > 0 .
\]
The claimed right-hand side $e^{-t^2/4} = \sum_n (-t^2/4)^n / n!$
contains only even powers of $t$ ($2n = 1$ is impossible for
$n \in \mathbb{N}$), so its linear coefficient is exactly $0$.
Equating the two sides at the linear term would force
$\mathrm{tr}\,K = 0$, contradicting $\mathrm{tr}\,K \ge 2/3 > 0$.
Numerically: $\det(I - 0.1K) \approx 1 - 0.0886 = 0.9114$ from the
first-order term, vs the claimed $e^{-0.0025} \approx 0.9975$ --- a
visible mismatch even at $t = 0.1$.
