# Conjecture 00000007621 — Disproof of the Zero-Divisor Metric Rank-Deficit Law

## Verdict: **DISPROVED**

Conjecture `00000007621` (*zero-divisor metric rank-deficit law*) asserts that the zero-divisor variety $Z(A)$ defined by multiplicative annihilation obeys an explicit dimension law whose constant is the metric rank deficit $n - \mathrm{rank}(B_Q)$, equal to the metric nullity count $r = \dim \ker(B_Q)$.

While the linear-algebraic identity $n - \mathrm{rank}(B_Q) = \dim \ker(B_Q) = r$ is tautological by rank-nullity, **the dimension of the zero-divisor variety is neither determined by $\bigl(n, r\bigr)$ nor governed by the metric rank deficit $r$** under any standard algebraic or geometric formulation:

1. **Exact Real Clifford Dimension Law (Radical Projection + Bott Periodicity):**
   For every real quadratic space of signature $\bigl(p, q, r\bigr)$ ($n = p + q + r$, metric rank $p + q$, metric rank deficit $r$), let $J \trianglelefteq \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q,r}(\mathbb{R})$ be the nilpotent ideal ($J^{r+1} = 0$) generated by $\ker(B_Q)$. Canonical projection $\pi : \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q,r}(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})$ satisfies $Z\bigl(\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q,r}(\mathbb{R})\bigr) = \pi^{-1}\bigl(Z(\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}))\bigr)$, yielding
   $$\dim_{\mathbb{R}} Z\bigl(\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q,r}(\mathbb{R})\bigr) = 2^n - c(p - q \bmod 8), \qquad c(k) = \begin{cases} 1, & k \equiv 0, 1, 2 \pmod 8, \\ 2, & k \equiv 3, 7 \pmod 8, \\ 4, & k \equiv 4, 5, 6 \pmod 8. \end{cases}$$
   The subtracted codimension constant $c \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ is $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{D}_{p-q \bmod 8}$ for the underlying division ring $\mathbb{D} \in \{\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{H}\}$ of the nondegenerate quotient—**completely independent of the metric rank deficit $r$**.

2. **Nondegenerate Counterexamples ($r = 0$):**
   Even when the metric rank deficit vanishes ($r = 0$), $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} Z$ fails to be single-valued at fixed $\bigl(n, r\bigr)$:
   - At $n = 1, r = 0$: $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{C}$ has $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} Z = 0$, whereas $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R} \oplus \mathbb{R}$ has $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} Z = 1$.
   - At $n = 2, r = 0$: $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,2}(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{H}$ has $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} Z = 0$, whereas $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}(\mathbb{R}) \cong M_2(\mathbb{R})$ has $Z = \{X : \det X = 0\}$ of dimension $3 = 2^2 - 1$.
   - At $n = 3, r = 0$: $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} Z$ takes three distinct values: $4$ on $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,3}(\mathbb{R})$, $6$ on $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,2}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$, and $7$ on $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R})$.

3. **Algebraically Closed Fields & Exterior Algebras ($r \gt 0$ vs. $r = 0$):**
   - Over $K = \mathbb{C}$ (or any algebraically closed field with $\mathrm{char}\,K \neq 2$), $\dim_K Z\bigl(\mathrm{Cl}(K^n, Q)\bigr) = 2^n - 1$ ($\mathrm{codim}_K Z = 1$) for **every** $r \in \{0, \dots, n\}$, exhibiting zero dependence on $r$.
   - In the purely degenerate exterior algebra $\Lambda(K^r) = \mathrm{Cl}_{0,0,r}(K)$ ($n = r \ge 1$), $x = x_0 + x_+ \in Z\bigl(\Lambda(K^r)\bigr) \iff x_0 = 0$, so $\dim_K Z\bigl(\Lambda(K^r)\bigr) = 2^r - 1$ ($\mathrm{codim}_K Z = 1$)—the exact same codimension $1$ and dimension $2^n - 1$ as split nondegenerate $\mathrm{Cl}_{m,m}(K)$ ($r = 0$).

4. **Grade-$1$ Vectors, Multiplicative Annihilators, and Finite-Field Point Counts:**
   - **Grade-$1$ vectors:** $Z\bigl(\mathrm{Cl}(V, Q)\bigr) \cap V = \{v \in V : Q(v) = 0\}$ is the isotropic cone, whose real dimension at $n = 2, r = 0$ is $0$ for $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,2}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}(\mathbb{R})$ versus $1$ for $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R})$.
   - **Multiplicative annihilator nullity:** In $M_2(K)$ ($r = 0$), $\dim_K \mathrm{Ann}_R(E_{11}) = 2 \neq 0$. In $\Lambda(K^r)$ ($r \ge 2$), $\dim_K \mathrm{Ann}_R(e_r) = 2^{r-1} \lt 2^r - 1 = \dim_K \mathrm{Ann}_R(e_1 \wedge \dots \wedge e_r)$, so annihilator nullity is non-constant across $Z \setminus \{0\}$.
   - **Finite-field point counts over $\mathbb{F}_q$:** At $n = 1, r = 0$ with $q \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, $\lvert Z(\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}(\mathbb{F}_q)) \rvert = 1$ (degree $0$) vs. $\lvert Z(\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}(\mathbb{F}_q)) \rvert = 2q - 1$ (degree $1$). At $n = 2$, $\lvert Z(M_2(\mathbb{F}_q)) \rvert = q^4 - (q^2 - 1)(q^2 - q) = q^3 + q^2 - q$ (degree $3$ at $r = 0$) vs. $\lvert Z(\Lambda(\mathbb{F}_q^r)) \rvert = q^{2^r - 1}$ (degree $2^r - 1$, equal to $3$ at $r = 2$).
