\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} the catenary degree $c(H)$ is the diameter of the
factorization graph. \textit{Conjecture:} the catenary degree of the
three-generated numerical semigroup $\langle a,b,c\rangle$ is an
explicit function of $\min(a,b,c)$.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

The catenary degree is $c(H)=\sup_n c(n)$, where $c(n)$ is the minimal
$t$ such that any two factorizations of $n$ are joined by a chain of
factorizations of $n$ with consecutive sup-norm distances $\le t$.
Direct exhaustive computation gives
\[
c(\langle 5,6,7\rangle) = 4 \ne 5 = c(\langle 5,6,25\rangle),
\qquad \min = 5 \text{ for both};
\]
\[
c(\langle 6,7,8\rangle) = 4 \ne 7 = c(\langle 6,7,35\rangle),
\qquad \min = 6 \text{ for both}.
\]
The witnesses: in $\langle 5,6,7\rangle$,
$Z(30)=\{(6,0,0),(2,1,2),(1,3,1),(0,5,0)\}$ with $(6,0,0)$ isolated at
threshold $3$ and chained at $4$; in $\langle 5,6,25\rangle$,
$Z(25)=\{(5,0,0),(0,0,1)\}$, a two-point set at sup-distance $5$.
Equal minima with unequal catenary degrees, twice: $c$ is not a
function of $\min(a,b,c)$.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies the finite factorization data; the transport to
catenary-degree values uses the classical results that $c(H)$ is
attained on Betti elements (Chapman--Garc\'ia-S\'anchez--Llena) and
that pairwise-coprime generated (generic) semigroups have Betti
elements exactly $\{g_i g_j\}$, cited in prose, with the
$\le$-direction carried by the exhaustive script's stability window.
The second counterexample pair is script-verified. The stated claim is
refuted in full through the first pair.
