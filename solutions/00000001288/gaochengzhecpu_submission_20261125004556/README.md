# Conjecture 00000001288 — Turning Turtles and Homomorphisms of $\mathbb{Z}_2[x]$

## Problem Statement

- **English:** Conjecture: The SG function of the Turning Turtles game is generated by a homomorphism of the polynomial ring over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (the algebraization of Turning Turtles).
- **中文：** 猜想：Turning Turtles 博弈的 SG 函数由 $\mathbb{Z}_2$-系数多项式环的同态生成(翻转乌龟的代数化)。

*(Verbatim statement preserved in `SOURCE.md`.)*

## Verdict: **DISPROVED (False)**

Under the mathematical definition of a homomorphism of the polynomial ring $\mathbb{Z}_2[x]$ — namely, a ring (or even non-unital rng) homomorphism preserving both polynomial addition and multiplication — **Conjecture `00000001288` is false** under both $0$-based and $1$-based coin-indexing conventions, across **all** possible ring structures on the codomain, and even under arbitrary set-theoretic readout factorizations through nontrivial quotient rings.

At the same time, our formalization proves the two positive algebraic properties that explain why the conjecture appears plausible on small boards:

1. **Surjective $\mathbb{F}_2$-Linearity on $\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_2[x], +\bigr)$:** Both the $0$-indexed Sprague–Grundy function $\mathcal{G}_0$ and the $1$-indexed Sprague–Grundy function $\mathcal{G}_1$ are surjective homomorphisms of the underlying additive group / $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space $\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_2[x], +, 0\bigr) \to \bigl(\mathbb{N}, \oplus, 0\bigr)$, satisfying $\mathcal{G}_t(P + Q) = \mathcal{G}_t(P) \oplus \mathcal{G}_t(Q)$. However, this uses only the infinite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_2$-module basis $\{1, x, x^2, \dots\}$ (as holds for every non-interfering coin-turning game), not the multiplication of the polynomial ring $\mathbb{Z}_2[x]$.
2. **Exact Degree-$\le 2$ Coincidence with Conway's Nimber Ring Homomorphism $\mathrm{ev}_2$:** Equipping $\mathbb{N}$ with Conway's nimber field operations $\bigl(\mathbb{N}, \oplus, \otimes\bigr)$, the unique unital ring homomorphism $\mathrm{ev}_2 : \mathbb{Z}_2[x] \to \bigl(\mathbb{N}, \oplus, \otimes\bigr)$ sending $x \mapsto 2$ satisfies $\mathrm{ev}_2(P) = \mathcal{G}_0(P)$ for **all $2^3 = 8$ polynomials of degree $\le 2$** ($1 \mapsto 1$, $x \mapsto 2$, $x^2 \mapsto 2 \otimes 2 = 3$), before breaking at the fourth coin:
   $$\mathcal{G}_0(x^3) = 4 \neq 1 = 2^{\otimes 3} = \mathrm{ev}_2(x^3).$$

## Summary of Mathematical Results

### 1. Algebraization and Exact Sprague–Grundy Evaluation

Identify a finite Heads set $S \subset \mathbb{N}$ with the polynomial $P_S(x) = \sum_{k \in S} x^k \in \mathbb{Z}_2[x]$. A legal move flips a Heads coin at $n$ to Tails (adding $x^n$) and optionally flips at most one coin at $i \lt n$ (adding $x^n + x^i$). Directly from the recursive $\mathrm{mex}$ definition on the well-founded game graph (decreasing binary valuation $\nu(P) = \sum_{k \in S} 2^k$), we prove:

$$\mathcal{G}_0\Bigl(\sum_{k \in S} x^k\Bigr) = \bigoplus_{k \in S} (k + 1), \qquad \mathcal{G}_1\Bigl(\sum_{k \in S} x^k\Bigr) = \bigoplus_{\substack{k \in S \\ k \ge 1}} k.$$

### 2. Three-Tier Refutation of Polynomial Ring Homomorphisms

- **Refutation in Conway's Nimber Field $\bigl(\mathbb{N}, \oplus, \otimes\bigr)$:**
  - For $0$-indexed Turning Turtles: $\mathcal{G}_0(x \cdot x^2) = \mathcal{G}_0(x^3) = 4 \neq 1 = 2 \otimes 3 = \mathcal{G}_0(x) \otimes \mathcal{G}_0(x^2)$.
  - For $1$-indexed Turning Turtles: $\mathcal{G}_1(x \cdot x) = \mathcal{G}_1(x^2) = 2 \neq 1 = 1 \otimes 1 = \mathcal{G}_1(x) \otimes \mathcal{G}_1(x)$.
  - Globally, every rng homomorphism $\Phi : \mathbb{Z}_2[x] \to \bigl(\mathbb{N}, \oplus, \otimes\bigr)$ has bounded image inside a finite Fermat subfield $[0, 2^{2^m}) \cong \mathbb{F}_{2^{2^m}}$, whereas $\mathcal{G}_0$ and $\mathcal{G}_1$ are unbounded surjections onto $\mathbb{N}$.
- **Universal Ring / Rng Obstruction (Arbitrary Codomain Ring $R$):**
  For **any** (not necessarily commutative, not necessarily unital) rng $\bigl(R, \boxplus, \boxtimes, 0_R\bigr)$ and **any** map $\iota : \mathbb{N} \to R$ with $\iota(n) = \iota(0) \Rightarrow n = 0$ (in particular, any injective encoding $\iota$), neither $\iota \circ \mathcal{G}_0$ on $\mathbb{Z}_2[x]$ nor $\iota \circ \mathcal{G}_1$ on the positive-degree sub-rng $x\mathbb{Z}_2[x]$ is a rng homomorphism, because the kernel of $\mathcal{G}$ is not an ideal:
  $$\mathcal{G}_0(1 + x + x^2) = 1 \oplus 2 \oplus 3 = 0, \quad \text{yet} \quad \mathcal{G}_0\bigl(x(1 + x + x^2)\bigr) = \mathcal{G}_0(x + x^2 + x^3) = 2 \oplus 3 \oplus 4 = 5 \neq 0,$$
  $$\mathcal{G}_1(x + x^2 + x^3) = 1 \oplus 2 \oplus 3 = 0, \quad \text{yet} \quad \mathcal{G}_1\bigl(x(x + x^2 + x^3)\bigr) = \mathcal{G}_1(x^2 + x^3 + x^4) = 2 \oplus 3 \oplus 4 = 5 \neq 0.$$
- **Zero-Conductor & Quotient-Factorization Obstruction:**
  For every nonzero polynomial $m(x) \in \mathbb{Z}_2[x]$ of degree $d$ (setting $r = d + 2$), shifting by $k_0 = 2^r - 1 - d$ and $k_1 = 2^r - d \ge 2$ isolates a unique summand with $r$-th binary bit equal to $1$, giving $\mathcal{G}_0(x^{k_0} m) \ge 2^r \gt 0$ and $\mathcal{G}_1(x^{k_1} m) \ge 2^r \gt 0$. Consequently, if $\mathcal{G}_t = \psi \circ \Phi$ for **any** rng homomorphism $\Phi : \mathbb{Z}_2[x] \to R$ and **any** set map $\psi : R \to \mathbb{N}$, then $\ker(\Phi) = \{0\}$ and $\Phi$ is injective.
