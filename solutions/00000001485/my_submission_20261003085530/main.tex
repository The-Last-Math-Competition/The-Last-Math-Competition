\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} the zeta function of a suspension of an
invertible map is the generating function
$\zeta(z) = \prod (1 - z^p)^{-1}$ of its periodic counts.
\textit{Conjecture:} for every hyperbolic suspension, $\zeta$ has
radius of convergence $1$, and its singular set on the unit circle is
exactly the set of roots of unity; no hyperbolic suspension has
radius of convergence $> 1$.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

The Arnold cat map $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
is hyperbolic with eigenvalues $\lambda_{1,2} = (3 \pm \sqrt{5})/2$.
A suspension of $A$ has the same periodic-orbit counts
$N_k = |\det(A^k - I)| = \lambda_1^k + \lambda_2^k - 2 = L_{2k} - 2$
($L_{2k}$ = even Lucas): $N_1 = 1$, $N_2 = 5$, $N_3 = 16$,
$N_4 = 45$.  The zeta function is the rational function
\[
\zeta(z) = \exp\Big(\sum_k \frac{N_k z^k}{k}\Big)
= \frac{(1-z)^2}{(1 - \lambda_1 z)(1 - \lambda_2 z)}
= \frac{(1-z)^2}{1 - 3z + z^2},
\]
with poles at $z = 1/\lambda_1 = (3-\sqrt5)/2 \approx 0.382$ and
$z = 1/\lambda_2 \approx 2.618$.  The radius of convergence is
\[
r = \frac{3 - \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 0.382 < 1,
\]
NOT $1$; and the singular set $\{0.382, 2.618\}$ contains no root of
unity (no unit-circle singularity at all).  Both clauses of the
conjecture fail for this hyperbolic suspension.
