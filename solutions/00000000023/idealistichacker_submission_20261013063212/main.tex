\begin{abstract}
The asserted absolute bound $C|S|^2$ for the number of solutions of $x+y=1$
in rational $S$-units is false. For every $N$ we use coprime pairs in the square
$[1,N]^2$ to obtain distinct solutions $(a/(a+b),b/(a+b))$. At least $N^2/4$
of these pairs are coprime. Taking $N=2^{2m}$ and using only an elementary
primorial bound gives $m|S_N|\leq6N$, so the solution-to-support-square ratio
is at least $m^2/144$. This tends to infinity, contradicting every absolute
constant. The Lean project checks the actual rational support bridge, not an
opaque surrogate predicate.
\end{abstract}

\section{The source assertion}
The source names the $S$-unit equation $x+y=1$ and asserts the existence
of an absolute constant $C$ such that the number of solutions is at most
$C|S|^2$. We use the standard rational interpretation: a nonzero reduced rational
number is an $S$-unit when every prime dividing its numerator or denominator lies
in the finite prime set $S$. Supports may be proper subsets of $S$.

\section{The explicit family}
For $N\geq1$ define
\[
 A_N=\{(a,b):1\leq a,b\leq N,\ \gcd(a,b)=1\},\qquad
 S_N=\{p\text{ prime}:p\leq2N\}.
\]
Map $(a,b)$ to
\[
 F_N(a,b)=\left(\frac{a}{a+b},\frac{b}{a+b}\right).
\]
Both coordinates are nonzero, add to one, and are reduced because
$\gcd(a,a+b)=\gcd(b,a+b)=1$. All prime divisors of the numerator and denominator
are at most $2N$, so the coordinates are genuine rational $S_N$-units.
Uniqueness of reduced positive numerator/denominator representations proves that
$F_N$ is injective.

\begin{lemma}
For every $N$, $|A_N|\geq N^2/4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every non-coprime pair has a common divisor $d$ with $2\leq d\leq N$.
The pairs divisible by a fixed $d$ are at most $\lfloor N/d\rfloor^2$.
Therefore the bad pairs are bounded by
\[
N^2\sum_{d=2}^{N}\frac1{d^2}\leq
N^2\left(\frac14+\sum_{d=3}^{N}\left(\frac1{d-1}-\frac1d\right)\right)\leq\frac34N^2.
\]
The finite telescoping inequality gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Support size and contradiction}
For $m\geq1$, set $N_m=2^{2m}$, $X_m=2N_m$, and $S_m=S_{N_m}$.
Partition primes up to $X_m$
into those at most $2^m$ and the rest. There are at most $2^m+1$ small primes.
For the large primes, their product is at least $(2^m)^r$ if there are $r$ of them.
The elementary primorial estimate $\prod_{p\leq X_m}p\leq4^{X_m}$ follows by
partitioning primes into dyadic intervals and bounding each interval product by
a central binomial coefficient. Thus $mr\leq2X_m$. Since $m\leq2^m$,
\[
m|S_m|\leq m(2^m+1)+2X_m\leq2N_m+4N_m=6N_m.
\]
Here $m(2^m+1)\leq 2(2^m)^2=2N_m$, since $m\leq2^m$ and $1\leq2^m$.
These are precisely the definitions and bounds in the Lean development.

Combining this with the injected family gives
\[
\frac{|F_{N_m}(A_{N_m})|}{|S_m|^2}
\geq \frac{N_m^2/4}{(6N_m/m)^2}=\frac{m^2}{144}.
\]
For any proposed absolute $C$, choose $m$ with $m^2>144\max(C,0)$.
The finite set $F_{N_m}(A_{N_m})$ then contains more than $C|S_m|^2$ actual
solutions, so the universal quadratic bound is impossible.
