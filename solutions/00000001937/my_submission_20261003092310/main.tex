\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} the subgroup growth zeta function is the
Dirichlet series of subgroup-index counts. \textit{Conjecture:} the
abscissa of convergence of the zeta function of an arithmetic linear
group is $\dim G / (\dim G + 1)$, uniquely determined by the
algebraic dimension of the group.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Take the arithmetic linear group $G = \mathbb{Z}$ (dimension $1$).
The subgroups of index $n$ are exactly the $n\mathbb{Z}$ --- one per
$n$ --- so $a_n = 1$ identically and
\[
\zeta_{\mathbb{Z}}(s) = \sum_{n \ge 1} \frac{1}{n^s}
= \text{the Riemann zeta function},
\]
whose abscissa of convergence is $1$: the harmonic series diverges at
$s = 1$ ($H_{20000} > 10$ and growing; classical), and convergence
holds for $s > 1$.  The conjecture's formula gives
$\dim G / (\dim G + 1) = 1/2$ at $\dim G = 1$.  Since
$1 \ne 1/2$, the abscissa of convergence is not determined by the
algebraic dimension: refuted at the very first arithmetic linear
group.  (For comparison, $\mathbb{Z}^d$ with $d \ge 2$ has abscissa
$d$, also not $d/(d+1)$.)

The kernel certifies the arithmetic core (formula value, harmonic
