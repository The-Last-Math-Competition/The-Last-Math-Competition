\section{The finite-isomorphism-quotient clause is false}
For every prime $p$ take the actual localization and additive group
\[
 R_p=\mathbf Z[1/p],\qquad G_p=R_p\oplus R_p\oplus R_p.
\]
These are rank-three Butler groups, and $G_p\not\cong G_q$ whenever
$p\ne q$. Since there are infinitely many primes, the set of
isomorphism classes of all rank-three Butler groups is infinite.
This disproves the source's necessary claim that the quotient is finite
up to isomorphism. It does not deny a classification with an infinite
parameter space or decide a modified problem fixing the rank-one types.

We follow the precise English definition in the source: a finite-rank
pure subgroup of a direct sum of rank-one groups. The standard
torsion-free formulation is recalled in Fuchs and Rangaswamy,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1017/S144678870001404X}
{\emph{Valuated Butler groups of finite rank}},
J. Aust. Math. Soc. 80 (2006), Introduction, printed p.336, item (1),
before the authors introduce the valued version.
Our examples are already completely decomposable; their realization
as pure subgroups uses the whole ambient direct sum, not a requirement
that the subgroup be proper.

\section{Actual localization, rank, and purity}
Lean uses Mathlib's actual localization
\texttt{Localization.Away} of $\mathbf Z$ at $p$.
The rank-one assertion is the genuine module rank
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf Z}R_p=1,
\]
obtained from the rank-of-localization theorem. The canonical map
$R_p\to\mathbf Q$ is constructed by the localization universal property
and proved injective, so each summand is torsion free.

The group $G_p$ is represented first by the finite function space
$\mathrm{Fin}\,3\to R_p$. Its rank is not assigned by definition.
The actual injective $\mathbf Z$-linear maps
\[
 \mathbf Z^3\longrightarrow G_p\longrightarrow\mathbf Q^3
\]
give lower and upper bounds of three on its module rank. The upper
bound uses the rank-preserving passage from $\mathbf Z$ to its fraction
field $\mathbf Q$. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf Z}G_p=3.
\]
No freeness of $R_p$ as a $\mathbf Z$-module is assumed. Injectivity of
the rational map also proves torsion freeness of $G_p$.

\newpage
Lean explicitly supplies the linear equivalence between this finite
function space and the actual direct sum
$D_p=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathrm{Fin}\,3}R_p$.
For an actual integer submodule $H\le D_p$, the purity condition is
encoded by the usual scalar-equation criterion: for every natural
$n$ and $h\in H$, if $nb=h$ has a solution $b\in D_p$, then it has
a solution in $H$. This is exactly $nD_p\cap H=nH$.
The full submodule $H=D_p$ satisfies that criterion immediately.
Thus the formal witness consists of actual rank-one torsion-free
summands, an actual finite direct sum, an actual pure submodule and
a linear isomorphism from the actual rank-three torsion-free group.
The theorem \texttt{family\_qualifies} collects these properties.

\section{An additive-group invariant separates the primes}
Multiplication by $p$ is surjective on $R_p$ because $p$ is invertible
in this localization. It is therefore surjective on all three
coordinates of $G_p$.

If $q\ne p$ is another prime, the image of $p$ is a unit in
$\mathbf Z/q\mathbf Z$. The universal property gives a genuine ring
homomorphism
\[
 \rho_{p,q}:R_p\longrightarrow\mathbf Z/q\mathbf Z.
\]
If multiplication by $q$ were surjective on $G_p$, apply it to the
vector $(1,1,1)$. Its first coordinate would give $qx=1$ in $R_p$.
Applying $\rho_{p,q}$ gives $0=1$ in the nonzero field
$\mathbf Z/q\mathbf Z$, a contradiction. Hence
\[
 G_p\text{ is $p$-divisible, but is not $q$-divisible for }q\ne p.
\]
Surjectivity of multiplication by a fixed natural number is preserved
by every additive-group isomorphism. It follows that no additive
isomorphism $G_p\cong G_q$ exists for distinct primes. This obstruction applies to all additive-group isomorphisms without
requiring preservation of the chosen ring presentation.

\section{The real isomorphism quotient is infinite}
On the actual type of primes Lean defines the relation
\[
 p\sim q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \operatorname{Nonempty}(G_p\simeq_+G_q).
\]
It checks reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity using actual additive
equivalences. Pairwise nonisomorphism makes the map from primes to
this quotient injective, so the quotient is infinite. A separate
theorem excludes every finite complete catalog: any assignment of
labels to these groups for which equal labels imply isomorphism
would inject the infinite set of primes into a finite type.
The relation is not defined as equality of the prime labels.
