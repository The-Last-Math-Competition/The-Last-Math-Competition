\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} the number of bistellar equivalence classes of
$d$-dimensional combinatorial manifolds is at most two for $d \ge 4$,
decided by the parity of middle Betti numbers.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

In $d = 4$, the manifolds $S^4$, $\mathbb{C}P^2$, $S^2 \times S^2$ have
Euler characteristics $2$, $3$, $4$ (from the standard cell
decompositions), which are pairwise distinct. Since the Euler
characteristic is invariant under bistellar moves (Pachner: bistellar
equivalence is PL homeomorphism; $\chi$ is a PL invariant), these are
three distinct bistellar classes --- at least $3 > 2$ in dimension $4$.
Moreover the parity criterion fails: $\beta_2(S^4) = 0$ and
$\beta_2(S^2 \times S^2) = 2$ are both even, yet the manifolds are
inequivalent ($\chi = 2 \ne 4$).

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} recomputes the three characteristics and checks
distinctness and the parity failure. The Lean package (core Lean~4,
v4.33.1) certifies the seven arithmetic statements, all \texttt{does not
depend on any axioms}; the characteristics and PL invariance are classical
cited facts.

\section{Boundary}

Only the $d \ge 4$ clause is refuted; the $d = 3$ logarithmic bound is not
addressed.
