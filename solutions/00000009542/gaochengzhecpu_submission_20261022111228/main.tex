\begin{abstract}
Consider three genuine quantum channels from a qutrit
to a classical bit: channel $i$ reports whether the
input basis index equals $i$. Each individually has
maximized quantum mutual information $\log2$.
Their common-input max--min is instead $h(1/3)<\log2$,
where $h$ is binary entropy. The compound
entanglement-assisted coding theorem identifies the
operational capacity with this common-input quantity,
not the min--max asserted in the source. The calculation
holds for every density matrix, including coherent inputs.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Three actual completely positive trace-preserving maps}
Use input $A=\mathbb C^3$ with basis indexed by $0,1,2$,
output $B=\mathbb C^2$, and environment $E=\mathbb C^3$.
For $i\in\{0,1,2\}$ define $b_i(j)=0$ if $j=i$,
and $b_i(j)=1$ otherwise. Set
\[
 K_{ij}=|b_i(j)\rangle\langle j|,
 \qquad \Phi_i(X)=\sum_{j=0}^2 K_{ij}XK_{ij}^*.
\]
This is a complex-linear map. Every matrix amplification
is a sum of positive conjugations by $I\otimes K_{ij}$,
so it is completely positive. Moreover
$\sum_jK_{ij}^*K_{ij}=I_3$, proving trace preservation.
Directly,
\[
           \Phi_i(X)=\operatorname{diag}
                         (X_{ii},\operatorname{tr}X-X_{ii}).
\]
For a density matrix $\rho$, write $p_i=\rho_{ii}$.
Positivity makes these real and nonnegative, and
$p_0+p_1+p_2=1$. The output is the actual classical-bit
state $\operatorname{diag}(p_i,1-p_i)$.

The genuine Stinespring isometry
\[
                  V_i|j\rangle=|b_i(j)\rangle\otimes|j\rangle
\]
satisfies $V_i^*V_i=I_3$. Tracing its output over $E$
gives $\Phi_i$. Tracing over $B$ gives its complementary
channel
\[
       \Phi_i^c(\rho)=P_i\rho P_i+Q_i\rho Q_i,
       \quad P_i=|i\rangle\langle i|,\quad Q_i=I_3-P_i.
\]
This is a two-block pinching, not full dephasing.
Both output and complementary matrices are positive
and have trace one.

\section*{Entropy and coherent inputs}
Use natural logarithms, so capacities are in nats.
Define $\eta(x)=-x\log x$ for $x>0$ and $\eta(0)=0$,
$S(\rho)=\operatorname{tr}\eta(\rho)$, and
\[
                    h(p)=\eta(p)+\eta(1-p).
\]
The usual channel mutual information is
\[
 I(\rho,\Phi_i)=S(\rho)+S(\Phi_i(\rho))-S(\Phi_i^c(\rho)).
\]
To justify this standard entropy-exchange expression,
purify $\rho$ on a reference system $R$ and apply $V_i$.
The resulting state on $RBE$ is pure, so its reduced
states on $RB$ and $E$ have the same nonzero eigenvalues
by Schmidt decomposition. Substituting
$S(RB)=S(E)$ into the usual reference-output mutual
information gives the displayed formula.

Here is a direct proof of the needed pinching entropy
inequality, including coherent matrices. Let $\rho$ have
orthonormal eigenbasis $u_b$ and eigenvalues $\lambda_b$,
and let $\sigma=P\rho P+Q\rho Q$ have eigenbasis $v_a$
and eigenvalues $\mu_a$, where $P,Q$ are complementary
orthogonal projections. Set
\[
 w_{ab}=|\langle v_a,Pu_b\rangle|^2
                    +|\langle v_a,Qu_b\rangle|^2.
\]
These weights are nonnegative. Completeness of both
orthonormal bases and $P^2+Q^2=I$ give
\[
 \sum_b w_{ab}=1,\qquad \sum_a w_{ab}=1,
             \qquad \mu_a=\sum_b w_{ab}\lambda_b.
\]
Since $\eta$ is concave on $[0,\infty)$, finite Jensen
and the column sums imply
\[
 S(\sigma)=\sum_a\eta(\mu_a)
    \geq\sum_{a,b}w_{ab}\eta(\lambda_b)
    =\sum_b\eta(\lambda_b)=S(\rho).
\]
Applying this to $P_i,Q_i$ yields, for every actual
density matrix,
\[
          I(\rho,\Phi_i)\leq S(\Phi_i(\rho))=h(p_i).
\]
No restriction to diagonal or classical inputs was
used in this upper bound. If $\rho$ is diagonal,
the pinching leaves it unchanged, and equality holds.

\section*{Exact max--min and min--max values}
Binary entropy is at most $\log2$, with equality at
$p=1/2$. Thus for every $i$ and every state,
$I(\rho,\Phi_i)\leq\log2$. The diagonal state with
$p_i=1/2$ and the other two probabilities $1/4$
attains equality. Consequently
\[
              \sup_\rho I(\rho,\Phi_i)=\log2
                        \quad\text{for each }i,
\qquad
              \min_i\sup_\rho I(\rho,\Phi_i)=\log2.
\]

On the other hand, every state has some $p_i\leq1/3$,
because its three diagonal probabilities sum to one.
Binary entropy is increasing on $[0,1/2]$, so that
channel satisfies
\[
                 I(\rho,\Phi_i)\leq h(p_i)\leq h(1/3).
\]
This proves $\sup_\rho\min_i I(\rho,\Phi_i)\leq h(1/3)$.
The uniform diagonal state $\rho=I_3/3$ attains
$I(\rho,\Phi_i)=h(1/3)$ simultaneously for all three
channels. Hence
\[
        \sup_\rho\min_i I(\rho,\Phi_i)=h(1/3)<\log2
                             =\min_i\sup_\rho I(\rho,\Phi_i).
\]
The strict inequality is exact, not numerical:
$h(1/3)=\log3-\tfrac23\log2$, so its gap from
$\log2$ is $\tfrac13\log(32/27)>0$.

\section*{The operational capacity bridge}
For a compound memoryless quantum channel set
$\mathcal W$, the channel is fixed during transmission
but unknown within $\mathcal W$, and one code must be
reliable for every member. The entanglement-assisted
compound-channel coding theorem states
\[
 C_{\rm EA}(\mathcal W)
       =\sup_\rho\inf_{\Phi\in\mathcal W}I(\rho,\Phi).
\]
This holds under both average and maximal transmission
error; see \cite[Theorem 1, equation (7)]{bjk}.
The reference uses bits; multiplying by $\log2$
gives the same formula in the nats used here.

For our finite set $\mathcal W=\{\Phi_0,\Phi_1,\Phi_2\}$,
the preceding exact calculation therefore gives
\[
                   C_{\rm EA}(\mathcal W)=h(1/3),
\]
whereas the source's proposed formula gives $\log2$.
The two cannot be equal. The relevant difference is
the common encoder input needed for the unknown
channel; each individual maximizing state need not
work simultaneously for the whole set.

\section*{Conclusion and formalization scope}
\begin{theorem}
The entanglement-assisted capacity of a finite compound
quantum channel set need not equal
$\min_i\max_\rho I(\rho,\Phi_i)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The three explicitly constructed CPTP maps satisfy
$\sup_\rho\min_i I=h(1/3)<\log2=\min_i\sup_\rho I$.
The compound coding theorem identifies the first
quantity with their operational capacity.
\end{proof}
The first finite-set capacity formula is already false.
No claim about the separate continuity statement or
the additional error-exponent assertion is needed to
refute the source's conjunction.

The Lean project uses genuine complex matrices with
actual positive-semidefinite and trace-one conditions.
Entropy is the real trace of the actual continuous
functional calculus of $-x\log x$. Its spectral
eigenvalue formula is proved, and the pinching entropy
inequality is derived through the actual unitary
eigenbases and finite Jensen argument above. No
quantum entropy inequality is assumed as a custom axiom.

The channel is an actual complex-linear Kraus map.
Complete positivity is proved for every finite matrix
amplification, and trace preservation holds on every
input matrix. The explicit Stinespring matrix is proved
an isometry, and both actual partial-trace identities
are verified. Positive normalized output and environment
states are proved. Thus the mutual-information expression
uses genuine state and channel entropies, not a chosen
binary-entropy proxy. Its bound for all coherent states,
diagonal equality cases and exact optimizing states are
formal theorems.

The common-input optimization is an actual supremum
over all density matrices of their finite infimum over
the three channels. Individual optimizations are actual
suprema over all states. Their exact values and strict
gap are proved using global bounds and attaining states;
conditional-supremum defaults do not supply the result.

The formal project verifies this actual single-letter
gap. The operational coding theorem and the standard
purification justification of the entropy-exchange
formula are explicitly invoked in the mathematical
proof, not newly formalized in Lean. In particular,
the project does not define its optimization to be
an operational capacity by fiat.
