# Conjecture 00000007601 — Symmetric–Antisymmetric Clifford Product Decomposition

## Verdict: **Disproved** (False in general on $\mathrm{Cl}\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$; True on grade-$1$ Euclidean vectors)

### Source Conjecture (`SOURCE.md`)

> **Definition:** Clifford algebras as algebras generated by quadratic forms.  
> **Conjecture:** The product decomposes into symmetric plus antisymmetric parts and the combination of the decomposition is the commutator structure; the symmetric part is inner-product type; the antisymmetric part is outer-product type; and the orthogonality of the decomposition is given by the metric. *(symmetric-antisymmetric Clifford product decomposition)*

---

## Mathematical Resolution Summary

Let $\mathrm{Cl}\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$ be the Clifford algebra generated by an $n$-dimensional quadratic space $\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$ over a field $K$ with $\mathrm{char}\bigl(K\bigr) \neq 2$, with polar symmetric bilinear form $B_Q\bigl(u, v\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(Q\bigl(u+v\bigr) - Q\bigl(u\bigr) - Q\bigl(v\bigr)\bigr)$, canonical Chevalley vector-space grading $\mathrm{Cl}\bigl(V, Q\bigr) = \bigoplus_{k=0}^{n} \mathrm{Cl}^k\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$, reversion $X \mapsto \widetilde{X}$, exterior product $X_r \wedge Y_s = \langle X_r Y_s \rangle_{r+s}$, inner contractions ($X_r \lrcorner Y_s = \langle X_r Y_s \rangle_{s-r}$, $X_r \llcorner Y_s = \langle X_r Y_s \rangle_{r-s}$, Hestenes inner product $X_r \cdot Y_s = \langle X_r Y_s \rangle_{|r-s|}$), and canonical metric bilinear form $\langle X, Y \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = \langle \widetilde{X} Y \rangle_0$.

Every product $X Y$ in $\mathrm{Cl}\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$ uniquely splits into symmetric and antisymmetric parts via the anticommutator and commutator:

$$X Y = S\bigl(X, Y\bigr) + A\bigl(X, Y\bigr), \qquad S\bigl(X, Y\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\{X, Y\} = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(X Y + Y X\bigr), \qquad A\bigl(X, Y\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}[X, Y] = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(X Y - Y X\bigr).$$

### 1. Positive Non-Vacuity Theorem on Grade-$1$ Vectors

When restricted to **grade-$1$ generating vectors** $u, v \in V = \mathrm{Cl}^1\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$, Hestenes' identity holds universally:
- $S\bigl(u, v\bigr) = B_Q\bigl(u, v\bigr) \cdot 1 = u \lrcorner v = u \llcorner v = u \cdot v \in \mathrm{Cl}^0\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$ (inner-product type),
- $A\bigl(u, v\bigr) = u \wedge v \in \mathrm{Cl}^2\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$ (outer-product type),
- $\langle S\bigl(u, v\bigr), A\bigl(u, v\bigr) \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = 0$, with the Lagrange–Pythagoras norm identity $\langle u v, u v \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = \langle S, S \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} + \langle A, A \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = Q\bigl(u\bigr) Q\bigl(v\bigr)$, and $B_Q\bigl(u, v\bigr) = 0 \iff S\bigl(u, v\bigr) = 0 \iff u v = -v u$.

### 2. Exact Homogeneous Grade-Parity Law & Failure Beyond Grade $1$

For homogeneous multivectors $X_r \in \mathrm{Cl}^r\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$ and $Y_s \in \mathrm{Cl}^s\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$, reversion yields the exact commutation law at every output grade $m = r + s - 2j$ ($0 \le 2j \le r + s$):

$$\langle Y_s X_r \rangle_{r + s - 2j} = \bigl(-1\bigr)^{r s - j} \langle X_r Y_s \rangle_{r + s - 2j}.$$

Consequently, the conjecture **fails in general across $\mathrm{Cl}\bigl(V, Q\bigr)$ as soon as $\dim V \ge 2$**:

1. **Complete Parity Reversal in $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\bigr)$ ($\dim V = 2$, Vector $\times$ Bivector):**  
   For $A = e_1 \in \mathrm{Cl}^1$ and $B = e_1 e_2 \in \mathrm{Cl}^2$ ($e_1^2 = e_2^2 = 1$), $A B = e_2$ and $B A = -e_2$. Thus $S\bigl(A, B\bigr) = 0 \neq e_2 = A \lrcorner B = A \cdot B$, while the antisymmetric commutator $A_{\mathrm{anti}}\bigl(A, B\bigr) = e_2 = A \lrcorner B \neq 0 = A \wedge B$ is **pure inner contraction**.
2. **Symmetric Part = Pure Outer Product & Bivector Lie Bracket in $\mathrm{Cl}_{3,0}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\bigr)$ ($\dim V = 3$):**  
   - For $X = e_1 e_2 \in \mathrm{Cl}^2$ and $Y = e_3 \in \mathrm{Cl}^1$, $X Y = Y X = e_1 e_2 e_3$, so $S\bigl(X, Y\bigr) = e_1 e_2 e_3 = X \wedge Y \neq 0$ (**pure outer product**), $A\bigl(X, Y\bigr) = 0$, all inner contractions vanish, and $\langle X, Y \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = 0$ despite $S\bigl(X, Y\bigr) \neq 0$. More generally, for any $u \in \mathrm{Cl}^1$ and $B \in \mathrm{Cl}^2$, $\frac{1}{2}\{u, B\} = u \wedge B$ and $\frac{1}{2}[u, B] = u \lrcorner B$.
   - For $B_1 = e_1 e_2$ and $B_2 = e_2 e_3$, all inner contractions and $B_1 \wedge B_2$ vanish, yet $\frac{1}{2}[B_1, B_2] = e_1 e_3 \neq 0$.
3. **Irreducible Three-Part Bivector Product in $\mathrm{Cl}_{4,0}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\bigr)$ ($\dim V = 4$):**  
   For $B_1 = e_1 e_2$ and $B_2 = e_1 e_2 + e_2 e_3 + e_3 e_4$ in $\mathrm{Cl}^2$, $B_1 B_2 = -1 + e_1 e_3 + e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4$ spans three nonzero grades ($0, 2, 4$): the symmetric part $S\bigl(B_1, B_2\bigr) = -1 + e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4$ mixes **both** the grade-$0$ inner contraction and the grade-$4$ outer product, while the antisymmetric commutator $\frac{1}{2}[B_1, B_2] = e_1 e_3 \in \mathrm{Cl}^2$ is neither.
4. **Failures of Metric Orthogonality:**  
   - *Positive-definite Euclidean $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\bigr)$, inhomogeneous elements:* For $X = 1 + e_1$ and $Y = e_2 + e_1 e_2$, $X Y = 2\bigl(e_2 + e_1 e_2\bigr)$ and $Y X = 0$, so $S\bigl(X, Y\bigr) = A\bigl(X, Y\bigr) = e_2 + e_1 e_2 \neq 0$ are **identical** with $\langle S, A \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = 2 \neq 0$ and $\langle X Y, X Y \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = 8 \neq 4 = \langle S, S \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} + \langle A, A \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}}$.
   - *Indefinite and degenerate quadratic forms (even on grade-$1$ vectors):* In $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,1,1}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}\bigr)$ ($q = \bigl(1, -1, 1\bigr)$), $u' = e_1 + e_2$ and $v' = e_3$ give $A' = u' \wedge v' \neq 0$ with $\langle A', A' \rangle_{\widetilde{Q}} = \langle A' A' \rangle_0 = 0$ (self-orthogonal isotropic blade; likewise in $\mathrm{Cl}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}^2, 0\bigr)$).
