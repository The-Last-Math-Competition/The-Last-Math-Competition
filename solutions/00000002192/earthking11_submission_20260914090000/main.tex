\section*{Verdict: FALSE}

We disprove conjecture \texttt{00000002192} as stated. The refutation is
unconditional and completely elementary. The standard example $S_2$ is a
height-$2$ poset on $n=4$ elements whose order dimension is exactly $2$,
whereas the conjecture predicts $\lceil \log_2\log_2 4\rceil = 1$. The
conjecture claims that the bound ``follows from the deep nesting of standard
examples of random posets'', and it is precisely the standard example that
refutes it.

\section{The conjecture, quoted verbatim}

From \texttt{conjectures/00000002192.md}:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{English.} Definition: The dimension of a poset is the minimal number of
linear extensions whose intersection is the poset. Conjecture: The maximal
dimension of height-2 posets is $\lceil \log_2 \log_2 n\rceil$ (exact); the bound
follows from the deep nesting of standard examples of random posets.
\end{quote}

The conjecture is filed in both English and Chinese; the Chinese original is
reproduced verbatim (in Unicode) in \texttt{README.md}, and the two versions are
identical in content. The PDF font does not embed CJK glyphs, so the Chinese is
kept in the markdown copy rather than duplicated here.

Throughout, a \emph{linear extension} of a poset $P$ is a strict total order
extending the strict order of $P$, and the \emph{intersection} of linear
extensions $L_1,\dots,L_d$ is the relation
$x < y \iff (x <_{L_i} y \text{ for all } i)$. The \emph{dimension} $\dim(P)$ is
the least $d$ for which some $d$ linear extensions have intersection exactly
$P$.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$n$}{n} is never defined}


The statement is ill-posed before it is false: the symbol $n$ is never
introduced. We show that every natural reading fails.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.30\textwidth} p{0.36\textwidth} p{0.26\textwidth}}
\toprule
Reading of $n$ & Counterexample & Formula vs.\ truth \\
\midrule
number of elements $|P|$ & $S_2$ has $n=4$ elements & $\lceil \log_2\log_2 4\rceil = 1$ vs.\ $\dim = 2$ \\
number of minimal elements & $S_k$ has $k$ minimal elements & $\lceil \log_2\log_2 k\rceil < k$ vs.\ $\dim = k$ \\
width (largest antichain) & $S_k$ has width $k$ & $\lceil \log_2\log_2 k\rceil < k$ vs.\ $\dim = k$ \\
rank / height & every $S_k$ has height $2$ & $\lceil \log_2\log_2 2\rceil = 0$ vs.\ $\dim = k$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

So no choice of $n$ saves the conjecture. The primary witness is the first row,
the natural reading $n = |P|$.

\section{The standard example \texorpdfstring{$S_2$}{S2}}

\begin{definition}[Standard example]\label{def:Sk}
For $k \ge 1$ the \emph{standard example} $S_k$ is the height-$2$ poset with
$k$ minimal elements $a_0,\dots,a_{k-1}$ and $k$ maximal elements
$b_0,\dots,b_{k-1}$ and relations
\[
a_j < b_i \iff i \neq j .
\]
\end{definition}

For $k=2$ take the four elements $0,1$ (minimal) and $2,3$ (maximal), with the
two relations
\[
0 < 3 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 1 < 2 .
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:height}
$S_2$ has height $2$, i.e.\ it contains a $2$-element chain but no
$3$-element chain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The chain $0<3$ exists. The only relations are the disjoint pairs $0<3$ and
$1<2$. If $x<y<z$ were a $3$-chain, then $y$ would be above $x$ and below $z$;
but the top element of each relation ($3$ or $2$) is maximal and the bottom
element ($0$ or $1$) is minimal, and the two relations share no element, so no
such $y$ exists.
\end{proof}

\section{Two linear extensions whose intersection is \texorpdfstring{$S_2$}{S2}}

Define the two total orders
\[
L_1 = [\,0,3,1,2\,] \quad(\text{order } 0<3<1<2),
\qquad
L_2 = [\,1,2,0,3\,] \quad(\text{order } 1<2<0<3).
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:upper}
$L_1$ and $L_2$ are linear extensions of $S_2$ and their intersection is
exactly the relation of $S_2$. Hence $\dim(S_2) \le 2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both orders place $0$ before $3$ and $1$ before $2$, so both respect the two
relations. Conversely, in the intersection:
\begin{itemize}
\item the pair $\{0,1\}$ has $0<1$ in $L_1$ and $1<0$ in $L_2$, so neither
  order survives;
\item the pair $\{2,3\}$ has $3<2$ in $L_1$ and $2<3$ in $L_2$, so neither
  order survives;
\item $0<3$ and $1<2$ hold in both orders, so both survive.
\end{itemize}
Hence the intersection is exactly $\{(0,3),(1,2)\}$, the relation of $S_2$.
\end{proof}

\section{No single linear extension realises \texorpdfstring{$S_2$}{S2}}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower}
No single linear extension of $S_2$ has intersection equal to the relation of
$S_2$. Hence $\dim(S_2) \ge 2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $L$ be a single linear extension and suppose its intersection (which is
just its own total order) equals the relation of $S_2$. A total order must
compare the incomparable pair $\{0,1\}$: either $0 <_L 1$ or $1 <_L 0$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $0 <_L 1$, then $0<1$ lies in the intersection, but $0$ and $1$ are
  incomparable in $S_2$ (both are minimal, and $0<1$ is not one of the two
  relations) --- contradiction.
\item If $1 <_L 0$, then $1<0$ lies in the intersection, but again $0$ and $1$
  are incomparable --- contradiction.
\end{itemize}
So no single extension realises $S_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dim2}
$\dim(S_2) = 2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine $\dim(S_2) \le 2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:upper}) with $\dim(S_2) \ge 2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:lower}).
\end{proof}

\section{The contradiction}

$S_2$ has $n = 4$ elements, height $2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:height}), and dimension
$2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dim2}). The conjecture asserts that the maximal dimension
of a height-$2$ poset on $n$ elements is
\[
\left\lceil \log_2 \log_2 n \right\rceil
= \left\lceil \log_2 \log_2 4 \right\rceil
= \left\lceil \log_2 2 \right\rceil
= \lceil 1 \rceil = 1 .
\]
But $S_2$ is a height-$2$ poset on $4$ elements with dimension $2 \neq 1$.
Therefore the conjectured value is strictly too small and the conjecture is
false.

\begin{corollary}
There is a height-$2$ poset on $n=4$ elements whose dimension exceeds
$\lceil \log_2\log_2 4\rceil$. Hence the maximal dimension of height-$2$ posets
is not given by $\lceil \log_2\log_2 n\rceil$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The failure is not a rounding or boundary effect. The same witness family
refutes the claim for every $k \ge 2$; see Section~\ref{sec:family}.
\end{remark}

\section{The family \texorpdfstring{$S_k$}{Sk} and the true growth}\label{sec:family}

\begin{theorem}[Dushnik--Miller]\label{thm:dimSk}
For every $k \ge 1$, $\dim(S_k) = k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the lower bound]
In a realizer, for each $i$ the incomparable pair $\{a_i,b_i\}$ must be
reversed by some extension $L$ with $b_i <_L a_i$; otherwise $a_i < b_i$ would
belong to the intersection.

One extension cannot reverse two distinct pairs. Suppose $L$ satisfies
$b_i <_L a_i$ and $b_j <_L a_j$ with $i \neq j$. Since $i \neq j$, the
relations $a_i < b_j$ and $a_j < b_i$ hold in $S_k$, and every extension
respects them. Combining,
\[
a_i < b_j <_L a_j < b_i <_L a_i ,
\]
a cycle in the total order $L$, impossible.

Hence each of the $k$ pairs $\{a_i,b_i\}$ requires its own extension, so
$k$ extensions are necessary. Conversely, the $k$ extensions
\[
L_i = [\,a_j\ (j \neq i)\,]\,[\,b_i\,]\,[\,a_i\,]\,[\,b_j\ (j \neq i)\,]
\]
are linear extensions (every relation $a_j < b_l$ with $j \neq l$ is respected:
$a_j$ precedes $b_i$ in the leading block or $a_i$ precedes $b_l$ in the
trailing block), and they realise $S_k$. Therefore $\dim(S_k) = k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:allk}
For every $k \ge 2$, $S_k$ is a height-$2$ poset on $2k$ elements with
$\dim(S_k) = k > \lceil \log_2\log_2 (2k)\rceil$. Thus the conjecture fails for
every $k \ge 2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:dimSk}, $\dim(S_k)=k$. For $k=2,3,4$ one checks
$\lceil \log_2\log_2(2k)\rceil = 1,2,2 < k$; for $k \ge 5$ the doubly
logarithmic quantity grows far more slowly than $k$ (e.g.\
$\lceil\log_2\log_2(2k)\rceil < k$ already for all $k \ge 5$ by monotonicity and
the value at $k=5$). Hence $k > \lceil \log_2\log_2(2k)\rceil$ for all
$k \ge 2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hiraguchi; tight for standard examples]\label{thm:floor}
For every poset $P$ with $|P| \ge 2$, $\dim(P) \le \lfloor |P|/2\rfloor$.
Moreover, for $n \ge 4$ the maximum dimension of a height-$2$ poset on $n$
elements equals $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, attained by
$S_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Hiraguchi's inequality gives $\dim(P) \le \lfloor |P|/2\rfloor$. The standard
example $S_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ has $2\lfloor n/2\rfloor \le n$ elements and
dimension $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dimSk}); adding
$n-2\lfloor n/2\rfloor \in \{0,1\}$ isolated elements does not decrease the
dimension nor increase the height beyond $2$, so the bound is attained whenever
$n \ge 4$.
\end{proof}

The true growth is therefore linear, not doubly logarithmic.

\section{Formalisation}

The $n=4$ contradiction is formalised in the Lean~4 project under
\texttt{lean4/} (core Lean only, \texttt{import Std}, no Mathlib, no
\texttt{sorry}): $S_2$ is modelled on \texttt{Fin 4} by
\texttt{ltS2 x y := (x = 0 \(\wedge\) y = 3) \(\vee\) (x = 1 \(\wedge\) y = 2)}, a linear
extension is a list respecting it, and the intersection of a family is the
pointwise intersection of the induced orders. The main statements are
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
theorem dim\_le\_two : DimLe 2\\
theorem no\_single (L : List E) (h : IsLinExt L) :\\
\hspace*{2em}\(\neg\) (\(\forall\) x y, Intersection [L] x y \(\leftrightarrow\) ltS2 x y)\\
theorem dim\_eq\_two : DimEq 2\\
theorem height\_two : HeightTwo\\
theorem formula\_at\_four : Nat.log2 (Nat.log2 4) = 1\\
theorem conjecture\_00000002192\_false :\\
\hspace*{2em}HeightTwo \(\wedge\) DimEq 2 \(\wedge\) Nat.log2 (Nat.log2 4) = 1 \(\wedge\) 2 \(\ne\) 1
\end{quote}
Here \texttt{DimLe d} says a realizer of $d$ linear extensions exists and
\texttt{DimEq 2} says the dimension is exactly $2$. Core Lean has no real
logarithm, so $\lceil\log_2\log_2 4\rceil = 1$ is formalised as the exact
integer iterated logarithm \texttt{Nat.log2 (Nat.log2 4) = 1}; at $n=4$ the two
readings coincide. See \texttt{lean4/README.md} for the statement table, the
proof strategy, and the axiom audit.

\section{Reproducibility}

The exhaustive checks are carried out by \texttt{reproduce.py} using the
Python~3 standard library only:

\begin{quote}\ttfamily
\$ python3 reproduce.py\\
... \textit{(exact dimension of $S_2,S_3,S_4$; the $S_2$ realizer; the chain and
$V$ witnesses; exhaustive height-$\le2$ enumeration for $n \le 6$; the
asymptotic table)}\\
PASS: all checks verified; conjecture 00000002192 is FALSE.
\end{quote}

It prints \texttt{PASS} and exits $0$ exactly when every check holds, and exits
non-zero otherwise. The exact order dimension is computed by iterative
deepening over tuples of linear extensions encoded as bitmasks of the two
orientations of every incomparable pair, with branch and bound; for a given
length the search is exhaustive unless pruned by the valid counting bound, so
the reported dimensions are exact.
