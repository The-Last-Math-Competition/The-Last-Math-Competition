\begin{abstract}
We resolve Conjecture \texttt{00000007169} (\emph{antisymmetric Fiedler additive correction}), which asserts under the heading of ``Fiedler with the additive inequalities of eigenvalues'' that \emph{``the ordering correction of additive inequalities holds, and the optimum of the correction is antisymmetrization.''} The statement is a logical conjunction $P \wedge Q$. We prove that while Clause~$P$ holds in its classical Lidskii--Wielandt--Ky Fan--Weyl--Fiedler form, Clause~$Q$ is \textbf{false across every standard mathematical interpretation of antisymmetrization for all matrix dimensions $n \ge 2$}, rendering the conjunctive conjecture \textbf{false}.

Specifically, for Hermitian (or real symmetric) matrices $A, B \in \Herm_n$ with non-increasing eigenvalue vectors $\alpha^\downarrow, \beta^\downarrow \in \R^n$ and reverse-ordered vector $\beta^\uparrow = (\beta_n, \dots, \beta_1)$, the eigenvalue vector $\gamma^\downarrow = \lambda^\downarrow(A + B)$ satisfies two-sided majorization $(\alpha^\downarrow + \beta^\uparrow)^\downarrow \prec \gamma^\downarrow \prec \alpha^\downarrow + \beta^\downarrow$ and Fiedler's (1971) determinant inequality $\prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_i) \le \det(A + B) \le \prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_{n+1-i})$ (for $A, B \ge 0$). However, the ordering-corrected extremum $(\alpha^\downarrow + \beta^\uparrow)^\downarrow$ is realized by \textbf{commuting Hermitian/symmetric matrices aligned in opposite eigenvalue order} via the longest permutation $w_0(i) = n + 1 - i$ --- \emph{not} by antisymmetrization. Conflating reverse spectral ordering ($\beta^\uparrow$) with antisymmetrization fails under all five canonical meanings of the term: (1)~matrix antisymmetrization $\Ap(M) = \frac{1}{2}(M - M^*)$ vanishes identically on $\Herm_n$, while non-Hermitian skew parts $M_r = H_r + K_r$ destroy Weyl/Ky Fan and Fiedler bounds altogether; (2)~skew-symmetric orthogonal conjugation $A + K B K^\top$ fails already at $n = 2$ ($\det = 400 < 625$ on explicit integer positive semidefinite matrices) and is impossible in all odd dimensions $n \ge 3$ ($\det K = 0$), while complex skew-Hermitian unitary conjugation ($U^* = -U$) fails for $n \ge 3$ by a $3$-cycle spectral obstruction; (3)~the Lie commutator $[A, B] = A B - B A$ vanishes at \emph{both} Lidskii--Fiedler extrema and yields strictly interior spectra when maximized; (4)~the Young alternating projector $\Alt(\beta)$ vanishes identically for $n \ge 3$ and violates trace conservation for $n = 2$, while $\mathrm{sgn}(w_0) = +1$ whenever $n \equiv 0, 1 \pmod{4}$; and (5)~for Fiedler's (1973) Laplacian algebraic connectivity $\lambda_2^\uparrow(L_1 + L_2)$, additive equality is realized by commuting symmetric Laplacians whereas skew-symmetric circulations produce non-real eigenvalues. All positive theorems and all five refutations are proved in self-contained Core Lean~4.19.0 with zero external dependencies and zero \texttt{sorry}s, and independently verified in pure Python.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Statement and Mathematical Diagnosis}

\subsection{Source Conjecture}

\end{quote}
The parallel Chinese text (\emph{``Fiedler: additive inequalities of eigenvalues. Conjecture: Fiedler's additivity: the ordering correction of additive inequalities [holds] and the optimum of the [cor]rection is antisymmetrization''}) confirms that the conjecture asserts two linked claims:

  \item \textbf{Clause $Q$ (Antisymmetrization Optimality):} The optimal (tightest) realization of this ordering correction is achieved by \emph{antisymmetrization} (\emph{f\v{a}n du\`{\i}ch\={e}ng hu\`{a}}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Origin of the Conflation and Summary of Results}
Let $\Herm_n \subset M_n(\C)$ denote the real vector space of $n \times n$ Hermitian matrices (containing $\Sym_n(\R)$). For $A \in \Herm_n$, write $\lambda^\downarrow(A) = (\lambda_1^\downarrow(A) \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n^\downarrow(A))$ and $\lambda^\uparrow(A) = (\lambda_n^\downarrow(A), \dots, \lambda_1^\downarrow(A))$. In Lidskii--Wielandt majorization~\cite{Lidskii1950,Wielandt1955} and Miroslav Fiedler's classical 1971 theorem on $\det(A + B)$~\cite{Fiedler1971}, the two extreme spectra over the unitary orbit $\{(U A U^*, V B V^*) : U, V \in \mathrm{U}(n)\}$ are:
\[
  \bigl(\lambda^\downarrow(A) + \lambda^\uparrow(B)\bigr)^\downarrow
  \;\prec\;
  \lambda^\downarrow(U A U^* + V B V^*)
  \;\prec\;
  \lambda^\downarrow(A) + \lambda^\downarrow(B).
\]
The lower majorization bound (which maximizes $\det(A + B)$ when $A, B \ge 0$ and minimizes convex symmetric spreads such as $\sum_i \gamma_i^2$) pairs $\lambda^\downarrow(A)$ with $\lambda^\uparrow(B)$ via the \emph{order-reversing permutation} $w_0(i) = n + 1 - i$. Crucially, a matrix pair $(A', B')$ realizing $(\lambda^\downarrow(A) + \lambda^\uparrow(B))^\downarrow$ consists of \textbf{commuting Hermitian matrices} ($A' = U \diag(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n) U^*$, $B' = U \diag(\beta_n,\dots,\beta_1) U^*$) whose sum $A' + B'$ is again Hermitian/symmetric with zero skew-Hermitian part.

\section{Positive Ordering-Corrected Additive Eigenvalue Theorems}

We first establish the precise positive theorems validating Clause~$P$ and characterizing its true extremizers.

\subsection{Failure of Uncorrected Additivity and Weyl--Ky Fan--Lidskii Corrections}
Naive entrywise subadditivity $\gamma_k \le \alpha_k + \beta_k$ fails for every $n \ge 2$: taking $A = \diag(2, 0) \oplus 0_{n-2}$ and $B = \diag(0, 2) \oplus 0_{n-2}$ gives $\alpha^\downarrow = \beta^\downarrow = (2, 0, \dots, 0)$ whereas $\lambda^\downarrow(A + B) = (2, 2, 0, \dots, 0)$, so $\gamma_2 = 2 > 0 + 0 = \alpha_2 + \beta_2$.

\begin{theorem}[Ky Fan Additive Majorization for All $n \ge 1$ \cite{KyFan1949}]\label{thm:kyfan}
Let $A, B \in \Herm_n$ (or orthogonally diagonalizable matrices over any linearly ordered commutative ring $R$) have non-increasing eigenvalues $\alpha = (\alpha_1 \ge \dots \ge \alpha_n)$ and $\beta = (\beta_1 \ge \dots \ge \beta_n)$, and let $A + B$ have non-increasing eigenvalues $\gamma = (\gamma_1 \ge \dots \ge \gamma_n)$. Then $\tr(A + B) = \sum_{i=1}^n \gamma_i = \sum_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_i)$, and for every $1 \le k \le n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kyfan}
  \sum_{i=1}^k \gamma_i \;\le\; \sum_{i=1}^k \alpha_i + \sum_{i=1}^k \beta_i.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $A = Q_A \diag(\alpha) Q_A^\top$, $B = Q_B \diag(\beta) Q_B^\top$, and $A + B = U \diag(\gamma) U^\top$ with $Q_A, Q_B, U$ orthogonal (or unitary). Denoting the columns of $U$ by $u_1, \dots, u_n$, we have $\sum_{r=1}^k \gamma_r = \sum_{r=1}^k \langle u_r, (A + B) u_r \rangle = \sum_{r=1}^k \langle u_r, A u_r \rangle + \sum_{r=1}^k \langle u_r, B u_r \rangle$. Setting $W = U^\top Q_A$ (which is orthogonal), $\sum_{r=1}^k \langle u_r, A u_r \rangle = \sum_{j=1}^n w_j \alpha_j$ where $w_j = \sum_{r=1}^k W_{rj}^2$ satisfies $0 \le w_j \le \sum_{r=1}^n W_{rj}^2 = 1$ and $\sum_{j=1}^n w_j = \sum_{r=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^n W_{rj}^2 = k$. If $k = n$, $1 - w_j \ge 0$ and $\sum_{j=1}^n (1 - w_j) = 0$ force $w_j = 1$ for all $j$, proving trace conservation. If $k < n$, choosing the pivot $p = \alpha_{k+1}$ gives
\[
  \sum_{j=1}^k \alpha_j - \sum_{j=1}^n w_j \alpha_j
  = \sum_{j=1}^k (1 - w_j)\alpha_j - \sum_{j=k+1}^n w_j \alpha_j
  \ge \sum_{j=1}^k (1 - w_j) p - \sum_{j=k+1}^n w_j p
  = \Bigl(k - \sum_{j=1}^n w_j\Bigr) p = 0.
\]
Applying the same argument to $B$ yields \eqref{eq:kyfan}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fiedler's Permutation Product Bounds and Uniqueness of Reverse Ordering}
Fiedler~\cite{Fiedler1971} proved that for positive semidefinite Hermitian matrices $A, B \ge 0$, $\det(A + B)$ is bounded between the comonotonic product $\prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_i)$ and the reverse-order product $\prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_{n+1-i})$. At the heart of Fiedler's theorem lies the sharp symmetric-group extremal theorem, which we prove together with its strict uniqueness condition in Core Lean~4 for all $n \in \N$.

\begin{theorem}[All-$n$ Rearrangement and Fiedler Product Theorem]\label{thm:fiedler-perm}
Let $R$ be a linearly ordered commutative ring, let $\alpha_1 \ge \dots \ge \alpha_n$ and $\beta_1 \ge \dots \ge \beta_n$ in $R$, and let $\pi \in S_n$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Two-sided rearrangement:} $\sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i \beta_{n+1-i} \le \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i \beta_{\pi(i)} \le \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i \beta_i$.
  \item \textbf{Fiedler product bounds:} If $\alpha_n \ge 0$ and $\beta_n \ge 0$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fiedler-perm}
    \prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_i)
    \;\le\;
    \prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_{\pi(i)})
    \;\le\;
    \prod_{i=1}^n (\alpha_i + \beta_{n+1-i}).
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Strict uniqueness of reverse ordering:} If $\alpha_1 > \dots > \alpha_n > 0$ and $\beta_1 > \dots > \beta_n > 0$, then equality holds on the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:fiedler-perm} \emph{if and only if} $\pi = w_0$, i.e., $\pi(i) = n + 1 - i$ for every $1 \le i \le n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any $a_1 \ge a_2$ and $b_1 \ge b_2$, direct expansion gives the two-point Fiedler exchange identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swap-id}
  (a_1 + b_2)(a_2 + b_1) - (a_1 + b_1)(a_2 + b_2)
  = (a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2) - (a_1 b_2 + a_2 b_1)
  = (a_1 - a_2)(b_1 - b_2) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
which is strictly positive whenever $a_1 > a_2$ and $b_1 > b_2$. We prove the upper bound in~\eqref{eq:fiedler-perm} and Part~3 by induction on $n$, rewriting $\pi = w_0 \circ q$ for $q \in S_n$. For $n = m + 1$, if $q(m+1) = m + 1$, then $\pi(m+1) = 1$, peeling off the positive factor $\alpha_{m+1} + \beta_1 > 0$ and reducing to $S_m$ on $(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_m)$ and $(\beta_2,\dots,\beta_{m+1})$. If $q(m+1) = s \le m$, let $r = q^{-1}(m+1) \le m$ and set $q' = q \circ (r,\; m+1)$. Since $\alpha_r \ge \alpha_{m+1}$ and $\beta_1 = \beta_{w_0(m+1)} \ge \beta_{w_0(s)}$, \eqref{eq:swap-id} yields
\[
  (\alpha_r + \beta_1)(\alpha_{m+1} + \beta_{w_0(s)})
  \;\le\;
  (\alpha_r + \beta_{w_0(s)})(\alpha_{m+1} + \beta_1),
\]
with strict inequality when $\alpha, \beta$ are strictly decreasing. Multiplying by the remaining non-negative (resp.\ strictly positive) product and applying the $q'(m+1) = m+1$ case establishes both the upper bound and the characterization $q = \mathrm{id} \iff \pi = w_0$. The lower bound in~\eqref{eq:fiedler-perm} and Part~1 follow analogously.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete $2 \times 2$ Non-Commuting Lidskii--Wielandt--Fiedler Theorem]\label{thm:lwf-2x2}
Let $A, B \in \Sym_2(R)$ be orthogonally diagonalizable with eigenvalues $\alpha_1 \ge \alpha_2$ and $\beta_1 \ge \beta_2$, and let $A + B$ have eigenvalues $\gamma_1 \ge \gamma_2$. Then $\gamma_1 + \gamma_2 = (\alpha_1 + \alpha_2) + (\beta_1 + \beta_2)$,
\[
  \max(\alpha_1 + \beta_2,\; \alpha_2 + \beta_1) \;\le\; \gamma_1 \;\le\; \alpha_1 + \beta_1,
  \qquad
  (\alpha_1 + \beta_1)(\alpha_2 + \beta_2) \;\le\; \gamma_1 \gamma_2 \;\le\; (\alpha_1 + \beta_2)(\alpha_2 + \beta_1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:kyfan} at $k = 1, 2$ gives $\gamma_1 \le \alpha_1 + \beta_1$ and $\gamma_1 + \gamma_2 = (\alpha_1 + \alpha_2) + (\beta_1 + \beta_2)$. Evaluating the Rayleigh quotient of $A + B$ at the unit top eigenvector $u_1$ of $A$ yields $\gamma_1 \ge \langle u_1, A u_1 \rangle + \langle u_1, B u_1 \rangle \ge \alpha_1 + \beta_2$; evaluating at the unit top eigenvector $v_1$ of $B$ yields $\gamma_1 \ge \alpha_2 + \beta_1$. Finally, whenever $x_1 + x_2 = y_1 + y_2$ and $y_2 \le y_1 \le x_1$, the algebraic identity $y_1 y_2 - x_1 x_2 = (x_1 - y_1)(x_1 - y_2) \ge 0$ implies $x_1 x_2 \le y_1 y_2$; applying this to $(x_1, x_2) = (\alpha_1 + \beta_1, \alpha_2 + \beta_2)$, $(y_1, y_2) = (\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$ and to $(x_1, x_2) = (\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$, $(y_1, y_2) = (\alpha^\downarrow + \beta^\uparrow)^\downarrow$ yields both determinant inequalities without even requiring $\alpha_2, \beta_2 \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cartesian Decomposition and Bendixson's Quadratic-Form Identity}
Over any linearly ordered field $F$ (and over $\C$), every matrix $M \in M_n(F)$ admits a unique Cartesian (Toeplitz) decomposition
\[
  M = \Sp(M) + \Ap(M), \qquad
  \Sp(M) = \tfrac{1}{2}(M + M^\top), \qquad
  \Ap(M) = \tfrac{1}{2}(M - M^\top),
\]
with $\Sp(M)^\top = \Sp(M)$ and $\Ap(M)^\top = -\Ap(M)$.

\begin{proposition}[Bendixson Quadratic-Form Vanishing]\label{prop:bendixson}
For every $K \in M_n(R)$ satisfying $K^\top = -K$ and every $x \in R^n$, $\langle x, K x \rangle = 0$. Consequently, over a linearly ordered field $F$, $\langle x, M x \rangle = \langle x, \Sp(M) x \rangle$ for all $M \in M_n(F)$ and $x \in F^n$; in particular, every real eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $M$ satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\Sp(M)) \le \lambda \le \lambda_{\max}(\Sp(M))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Swapping summation indices gives $\sum_{i,j} x_i K_{ij} x_j = \sum_{i,j} x_j K_{ji} x_i = -\sum_{i,j} x_i K_{ij} x_j$, so $2 \langle x, K x \rangle = 0$, forcing $\langle x, K x \rangle = 0$ in the ordered domain $R$.
\end{proof}

\section{Disproof Across All Five Readings of Antisymmetrization}

We now prove that under every standard mathematical reading of ``antisymmetrization,'' Clause~$Q$ is false for all dimensions $n \ge 2$.

\subsection{Reading 1: Matrix Antisymmetrization $\Ap(M) = \frac{1}{2}(M - M^*)$}

\begin{proposition}[Refutation of Reading 1 for All $n \ge 2$]\label{prop:refute1}
Let $n = m + 2 \ge 2$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Vanishing on the Hermitian/symmetric domain:} For all $A, B \in \Sym_n(F)$ (or $\Herm_n$), $\Ap(A) = \Ap(B) = \Ap(A + B) = 0$. For $A = \diag(n, n-1, \dots, 1)$ and $B_{\mathrm{rev}} = \diag(1, 2, \dots, n)$, the true Fiedler optimum $A + B_{\mathrm{rev}} = (n+1) I_n$ is purely symmetric ($\Ap(A + B_{\mathrm{rev}}) = 0$) with $\tr(A + B_{\mathrm{rev}}) = n(n+1) > 0 = \tr(\Ap(A + B_{\mathrm{rev}}))$.
  \item \textbf{Breakdown of Weyl/Ky Fan and Fiedler bounds under nonzero skew parts:} Define $M_1, M_2 \in M_n(\Z)$ by
  \[
    M_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \oplus 3 I_{n-2},
    \qquad
    M_2 = M_1^\top = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \oplus 3 I_{n-2}.
  \]
  Then $\Ap(M_1) = -\Ap(M_2) \ne 0$, and $(M_1 - 3 I_n)^2 = (M_2 - 3 I_n)^2 = 0$, so $M_1$ and $M_2$ each have exact real spectrum $(3, \dots, 3)$. Every permutation sum of $\lambda(M_1)$ and $\lambda(M_2)$ equals $(6, \dots, 6)$. Yet $M_1 + M_2 = \diag(8, 4) \oplus 6 I_{n-2}$ is symmetric with top eigenvalue $\lambda_1(M_1 + M_2) = 8 > 3 + 3 = 6$ (violating Weyl and Ky Fan subadditivity) and determinant $\det(M_1 + M_2) = 32 \cdot 6^{n-2} < 36 \cdot 6^{n-2}$ (violating Fiedler's lower determinant bound).
  \item \textbf{Loss of real spectrum:} Adding $K_3 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & 3 \\ -3 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ to $H = \diag(4, 0)$ yields characteristic polynomial $x^2 - 4x + 9$ with discriminant $\Delta = 16 - 36 = -20 < 0$ (non-real roots $2 \pm i\sqrt{5}$) and unbounded determinant $\det(H + K_t) = t^2 \to +\infty$ as $t \to \infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each identity is direct: $(M_1 - 3 I_n)$ has top-left block $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -1 & -1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ whose square is zero, while $M_1 + M_2$ cancels the skew-symmetric blocks to give $\diag(8, 4) \oplus 6 I_{n-2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reading 2: Skew-Symmetric and Skew-Hermitian Basis Conjugation}
For $2 \times 2$ \emph{already-diagonal} matrices $A = \diag(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ and $B = \diag(\beta_1, \beta_2)$, the signed swap matrix $J_2 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ happens to be skew-symmetric and orthogonal ($J_2^\top = -J_2$, $J_2^\top J_2 = I_2$) and satisfies $J_2 B J_2^\top = \diag(\beta_2, \beta_1)$. One might ask whether conjugating $B$ by an orthogonal skew-symmetric matrix $K$ (or unitary skew-Hermitian matrix $U^* = -U$) realizes the Fiedler optimum in general. This fails in every dimension $n \ge 2$.

\begin{theorem}[Refutation of Reading 2 Across Real and Complex Domains]\label{thm:refute2}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Universal $2 \times 2$ (and block-lifted $n \ge 2$) real failure:} Consider the positive semidefinite integer symmetric matrices
  \[
    A_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 25 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
    B_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 16 & 12 \\ 12 & 9 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
    Q_2 = \frac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 4 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Both $A_2$ and $B_2$ have eigenvalues $(25, 0)$. The \emph{symmetric} orthogonal reflection $Q_2$ ($Q_2^\top = Q_2$, $Q_2^2 = I_2$) achieves the Fiedler reverse-order optimum $A_2 + Q_2 B_2 Q_2^\top = 25 I_2$ with $\det = 625$. By contrast, \emph{every} skew-symmetric orthogonal matrix $K \in M_2(\R)$ ($K^\top = -K$, $K^\top K = I_2$) satisfies $K_{11} = K_{22} = 0$, $K_{21} = -K_{12}$, and $K_{12}^2 = 1$, hence
  \[
    A_2 + K B_2 K^\top = \begin{pmatrix} 34 & -12 \\ -12 & 16 \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \det(A_2 + K B_2 K^\top) = 400 < 625,
  \]
  with eigenvalues $(40, 10) \ne (25, 25)$. Taking direct sums with $I_{n-2}$ extends this strict gap ($400 \cdot 2^{n-2} < 625 \cdot 2^{n-2}$) to every $n \ge 2$.
  \item \textbf{Odd-dimension singularity ($n = 2m + 1 \ge 3$):} Every skew-symmetric matrix $K \in M_{2m+1}(\R)$ satisfies $\det(K) = \det(-K^\top) = (-1)^{2m+1}\det(K) = -\det(K)$, so $\det(K) = 0$; no orthogonal or invertible skew-symmetric matrix exists in any odd dimension.
  \item \textbf{Complex skew-Hermitian $3$-cycle obstruction ($n \ge 3$):} Let $A_3 = \diag(3, 2, 1)$ and $B_3 = \diag(2, 3, 1)$ in $\Herm_3$. The real $3$-cycle permutation matrix $P_3$ ($P_3 e_3 = e_1, P_3 e_1 = e_2, P_3 e_2 = e_3$) is unitary and achieves the Fiedler optimum $A_3 + P_3 B_3 P_3^* = 4 I_3$ ($\det = 64$). However, there exists \emph{no} skew-Hermitian unitary matrix $U \in M_3(\C)$ ($U^* = -U$, $U U^* = I_3$) satisfying $A_3 + U B_3 U^* = 4 I_3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Parts~1 and~2 are immediate from the entries of $K B_2 K^\top$ and skew-symmetry of $\det$. For Part~3, suppose $U \in M_3(\C)$ satisfies $U U^* = I_3$ and $M := U B_3 U^* = 4 I_3 - A_3 = \diag(1, 2, 3)$. Comparing $(M)_{11} = 2|U_{11}|^2 + 3|U_{12}|^2 + |U_{13}|^2 = 1$ with $(U U^*)_{11} = |U_{11}|^2 + |U_{12}|^2 + |U_{13}|^2 = 1$ gives $|U_{11}|^2 + 2|U_{12}|^2 = 0$, whence $U_{11} = U_{12} = 0$ and $|U_{13}|^2 = 1$. Similarly, comparing $3(U U^*)_{33} = 3$ with $(M)_{33} = 2|U_{31}|^2 + 3|U_{32}|^2 + |U_{33}|^2 = 3$ gives $|U_{31}|^2 + 2|U_{33}|^2 = 0$, so $U_{31} = 0$. If $U^* = -U$, then $U_{13} = -\overline{U_{31}} = 0$, contradicting $|U_{13}|^2 = 1$. Direct sum with $2 I_{n-3}$ extends the obstruction to all $n \ge 3$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reading 3: Lie-Algebraic Product Antisymmetrization $[A, B] = A B - B A$}

\begin{proposition}[Bloch-Sphere Commutator Identity]\label{prop:refute3}
Let $a, b > 0$ and $c^2 + s^2 = 1$, and set
\[
  A = \begin{pmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & -a \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
  B(c, s) = \begin{pmatrix} b c & b s \\ b s & -b c \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $-\det(A + B(c, s)) = a^2 + b^2 + 2 a b c$ (equal to the squared top eigenvalue of $A + B(c,s)$), whereas $\|[A, B(c, s)]\|_F^2 = 8 a^2 b^2 s^2 = 8 a^2 b^2 (1 - c^2)$. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Both the comonotonic extremum ($c = 1$, eigenvalues $\pm(a+b)$) and the Fiedler reverse-order extremum ($c = -1$, eigenvalues $\pm|a-b|$) occur at $[A, B] = 0$.
  \item Maximizing $\|[A, B]\|_F^2$ ($c = 0, s = \pm 1$) yields $-\det(A + B(0, \pm 1)) = a^2 + b^2$, which satisfies $(a - b)^2 < a^2 + b^2 < (a + b)^2$ and strictly misses both extrema.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Reading 4: Young Alternating Antisymmetrizer and Parity of $w_0$}

\begin{proposition}[Refutation of Permutation Antisymmetrization]\label{prop:refute4}
Consider the Young alternating operator $\Alt_{\mathrm{raw}}(\beta) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,\sigma(\beta)$ on $R^n$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For $n = 2$, $\Alt_{\mathrm{raw}}(\beta_1, \beta_2) = (\beta_1 - \beta_2,\; \beta_2 - \beta_1)$ has coordinate sum $0$, violating trace additivity whenever $\beta_1 + \beta_2 \ne 0$.
  \item For every $n \ge 3$, $\Alt_{\mathrm{raw}}(\beta) = (0, \dots, 0)$ vanishes identically for all $\beta \in R^n$ because each point stabilizer $S_{n-1}$ ($n - 1 \ge 2$) has equally many even and odd permutations.
  \item The order-reversing permutation $w_0(i) = n + 1 - i$ has $\binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ inversions, which is \textbf{even} ($\mathrm{sgn}(w_0) = +1$) whenever $n \equiv 0, 1 \pmod{4}$ (e.g., $n = 4, 5, 8, 9$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Reading 5: Fiedler Algebraic Connectivity on Graph Laplacians}
In spectral graph theory, Fiedler~\cite{Fiedler1973} introduced the \emph{algebraic connectivity} $a(G) = \lambda_2^\uparrow(L_G)$ of an undirected graph Laplacian $L_G \ge 0$ ($L_G \mathbf{1} = 0$). Because all undirected Laplacians share the null line $\R \mathbf{1}$, restricted to $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ the Fiedler value is $\lambda_{\min}(L_G|_{\mathbf{1}^\perp})$, so Courant--Fischer gives superadditivity $\lambda_2^\uparrow(L_1 + L_2) \ge \lambda_2^\uparrow(L_1) + \lambda_2^\uparrow(L_2)$ directly without sign reversal. On the complete graph $K_3$, $L_{K_3} = 3 I_3 - \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$ satisfies $\Ap(L_{K_3}) = 0$ and attains exact additivity $\lambda_2^\uparrow(L_{K_3} + L_{K_3}) = 3 + 3 = 6$ via symmetric commuting alignment, whereas adding any nonzero zero-row-sum skew-symmetric circulation $K_t = t\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ ($t \ne 0$) yields characteristic polynomial $x^2 - 12x + (36 + 3t^2)$ on $\mathbf{1}^\perp$ with strictly negative discriminant $\Delta = -12 t^2 < 0$ (complex eigenvalues $6 \pm i\sqrt{3}\,t$).
