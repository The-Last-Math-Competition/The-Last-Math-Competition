\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} for fixed $I$, $\mu(I^{[p^e]})$ is eventually a
polynomial in $p^e$ of degree $\dim(R) - \operatorname{ht}(I)$, with
leading coefficient depending only on the initial ideal.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Take the complete intersection $I = (x, y)$ in $R = k[x, y, z]$
($\dim 3$, $\operatorname{ht} 2$, claimed degree $1$).  The
Frobenius power is exactly $I^{[q]} = (x^q, y^q)$ for every $q$.
Since $x^q$ and $y^q$ are coprime monomials (any common divisor is
a unit), the ideal is not principal, and it is generated by two
elements:
\[
\mu\bigl((x^q, y^q)\bigr) = 2 \quad\text{for every } q :
\]
the ``polynomial'' is the constant $2$ --- degree $0$, not the
claimed $\dim - \operatorname{ht} = 1$.  A linear polynomial
$a\,q + b$ through the points $(2, 2)$ and $(4, 2)$ satisfies
$4a + b = 2$ and $2a + b = 2$, hence $2a = 0$ and $a = 0$
(kernel-certified): no polynomial of the claimed degree fits.  The
phenomenon is universal for complete intersections: for
$I = (x_1, \dots, x_r)$ in $k[x_1, \dots, x_{r+1}]$, $\mu = r$ is
constant while the claimed degree is $\dim - \operatorname{ht} = 1$.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} verifies the coprimality (hence
$\mu = 2$) at $q = 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 16, 27, 81$, the two-point linear
fit (slope forced to $0$, exact rationals), and the family of
complete intersections $r = 2..5$.  The Lean package (core Lean~4,
v4.33.1) certifies \texttt{mu\_q2}..\texttt{mu\_q16},
\texttt{degree\_zero\_forcing}, \texttt{degree\_mismatch}
($3 - 2 = 1 \ne 0$), and \texttt{conjecture\_refuted}.  All seven
audited theorems report \texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies the constant values and the degree-zero
forcing; the non-principality of $(x^q, y^q)$ is classical, cited
in prose and mirrored by the script's gcd check.  The claimed
degree formula is refuted at the certified complete intersection.
