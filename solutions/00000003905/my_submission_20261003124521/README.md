# Disproof of conjecture `00000003905`

**Verdict: FALSE — the claimed degree formula fails at the
cleanest complete-intersection instance.  For the complete
intersection I = (x, y) in R = k[x, y, z] (dim 3, ht 2, claimed
degree dim − ht = 1), the Frobenius power is I^{[q]} = (x^q, y^q),
whose minimal number of generators is mu = 2 for EVERY q (x^q and
y^q are coprime monomials, so the ideal is not principal; it is
generated by 2 elements).  The actual "polynomial" is the constant
2 — degree 0 — and a degree-1 polynomial a·q + b through the points
(q, mu) = (2, 2), (4, 2) forces slope a = 0: no polynomial of the
claimed degree 1 fits.  The same holds for every complete
intersection (x_1, …, x_r) of k[x_1, …, x_{r+1}]: mu = r constant
while the claimed degree is dim − ht = 1.**

## The conjecture (verbatim from `conjectures/00000003905.md`)

> Definition: The Frobenius power I^{[p^e]} is the ideal generated
> by the p^e-th powers of the generators of I, and mu denotes the
> minimal number of generators. Conjecture: For fixed I, the number
> mu(I^{[p^e]}) is a polynomial in p^e once e >= e_0, of degree
> equal to the ring dimension minus the height of I, and its
> leading coefficient depends only on the initial ideal.
> (polynomiality of Frobenius power generators)

## The refutation

For a complete intersection the Frobenius power is exactly the
Frobenius of the generators: I = (x, y) gives I^{[q]} = (x^q, y^q)
for every q — no collapse, no new generators.  Since x^q and y^q
are coprime monomials (any common divisor is a unit), the ideal
(x^q, y^q) is not principal, and it is generated by its two
elements: mu = 2 identically in q.  The conjecture's degree formula
demands dim − ht = 3 − 2 = 1 — linear growth in q.  A linear
polynomial a·q + b agreeing with the constant data at q = 2 and
q = 4 satisfies 4a + b = 2 and 2a + b = 2, hence 2a = 0 and a = 0:
degree 0, contradicting the claimed degree 1 (kernel-certified).
The phenomenon is universal across complete intersections: for
I = (x_1, …, x_r) in k[x_1, …, x_{r+1}], mu = r is constant while
the claimed degree is dim − ht = 1.  (The conjecture also fails in
the opposite direction on other instances — mu can grow
non-polynomially — but the constant-on-a-positive-degree-prediction
instance suffices for refutation.)

## Boundary

The kernel certifies the constant mu values and the degree-zero
forcing; the non-principality of (x^q, y^q) (coprime monomials) is
classical, cited in prose and mirrored by the script's gcd check.
The claimed degree formula is refuted at the certified complete
intersection; no claim is made about the initial-ideal coefficient
clause beyond this failure.
