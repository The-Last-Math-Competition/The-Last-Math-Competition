\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} the number of cyclic factors of the critical
group $\mathrm{Jac}(G)$ (the sandpile group) is the number of its
cyclic $\mathbb{Z}$-components. \textit{Conjecture:} the
classification of graphs with zero cyclic factors (i.e.\ Jac entirely
cyclic-free): exactly subdivisions of closed Eulerian graphs (trivial
exceptions exhausted).
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

$C_6$ is a subdivision of the closed Eulerian graph $C_3$ (the
triangle: all degrees $2$, Eulerian circuit exists): each of the 3
triangle edges split once gives $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ edges.  By the
Matrix-Tree theorem, $|\mathrm{Jac}(C_6)| = $ \#spanning trees of
$C_6 = 6$ (delete one of the 6 edges: a spanning path), and the exact
Smith normal form of the $5 \times 5$ reduced Laplacian is
$\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,1,6)$:
\[
\mathrm{Jac}(C_6) = \mathbb{Z}/6 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times
\mathbb{Z}/3,
\]
with \textbf{two} nontrivial cyclic torsion factors --- the cyclic
factor count is $2 \ne 0$.  The classification's inclusion 1 fails at
$C_6$: the ``exactly'' classification is refuted.  (Independent
second failure: any tree has $\mathrm{Jac} = 0$ but is not a
subdivision of a closed Eulerian graph.)
