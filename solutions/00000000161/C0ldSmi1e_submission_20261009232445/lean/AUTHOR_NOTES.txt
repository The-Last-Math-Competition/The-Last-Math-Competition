CONJECTURE 00000000161 — INDEPENDENT AUTHOR NOTES

Result
======
The conjecture is false under the standard meaning of a positive polynomial
denominator in n, independent of p. At fixed dimension n = 4, for every real
C > 0, the probability in the question is exactly zero whenever p >= 3C.
In particular it tends to zero, and cannot tend to one, as p tends to infinity
through primes. No distributional approximation or auxiliary numerical
computation is needed: the event is eventually empty for every group element.

Original statement and scope
============================
Both original language versions and both rules files were read before deriving
the argument. The English source says:

"Conjecture: The order of a random element of GL_n(F_p): with probability
tending to 1 (as p → ∞ with fixed n ≥ 2), a random A ∈ GL_n(F_p) has order
divisible by a prime factor exceeding p^{n−1}/poly(n)."

The Chinese version has the same conditions and threshold. The notation
poly(n) does not identify a particular polynomial or supply formal quantifiers.
The conventional reading is that a positive polynomial in the fixed dimension
serves as the denominator, with coefficients independent of p, and the claimed
conclusion applies to every fixed n >= 2. OriginalClaim makes that reading
explicit by existentially quantifying a real polynomial P positive at every
integer n >= 2, followed by the asserted limit for each such fixed n.

The main result is stronger than needed for that reading: it excludes every
positive real constant C at dimension four, and also excludes every positive
dimension-only denominator function D : Nat -> Real. Thus changing the degree,
coefficients, or choice of a positive polynomial cannot rescue the claim.
Any conventional positive multiplicative constants can be absorbed into C.
No conclusion depends on a precise growth degree in n. A denominator depending
on p or a nonpositive denominator would change the source's standard meaning
and is not silently substituted here.

Mathematical derivation
=======================
Fix a prime p, an element A of GL_4(F_p), and a prime r dividing its order.
Lagrange's theorem gives r | order(A) | |GL_4(F_p)|. Counting ordered linearly
independent columns gives

  |GL_4(F_p)| = product_{i=0}^3 (p^4 - p^i)
              = p^6 (p-1)^4 (p+1)^2 (p^2+1) (p^2+p+1).

The four individual factors expand as follows:

  p^4 - 1   = (p-1)(p+1)(p^2+1),
  p^4 - p   = p(p-1)(p^2+p+1),
  p^4 - p^2 = p^2(p-1)(p+1),
  p^4 - p^3 = p^3(p-1).

Euclid's lemma says r divides at least one of p, p-1, p+1, p^2+1, or p^2+p+1.
All these integers are positive for p >= 2. A positive integer's prime divisor
does not exceed the integer. Each factor is at most p^2+p+1, so

  r <= p^2+p+1.

Now let C > 0 be arbitrary. If p >= 3C, then, using p >= 1,

  p^2+p+1 <= 3p^2 <= p^3/C.

Consequently no prime divisor of any element order can strictly exceed p^3/C.
The numerator in the uniform counting probability is therefore zero. For fixed
C this holds for all sufficiently large primes. The prime-indexing filter is
nontrivial, and its values are unbounded by Euclid's theorem, so uniqueness of
real limits rules out a simultaneous limit of one. Since 4 >= 2 and n-1 = 3
at n = 4, this directly contradicts the stated assertion for every fixed n.

Exact formal correspondence
===========================
* PrimeIndex is the subtype {p : Nat // Nat.Prime p}, with inherited usual order.
  Its atTop filter is shown nontrivial, and the coercion to Nat is proved to
  tend to atTop using Nat.exists_infinite_primes.
* GeneralLinear n p is Mathlib's Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup (Fin n) (ZMod p).
  Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup is the group of units in the square matrix ring;
  for prime p, ZMod p is the p-element field. Thus this is the actual GL_n(F_p).
* LargePrimeFactor n C p A means there exists a natural prime r dividing the
  actual group-theoretic orderOf A, with the strict inequality p^(n-1)/C < r.
  It does not replace "prime factor" with "divisor", or allow a large composite.
* successProbability is the number of such A divided by the number of all A.
  This is exactly a uniformly random element's probability. The sample space
  is proved finite/nonempty via positive Nat.card; the ratio is also proved
  to belong to the real interval [0,1].
* prime_divisor_order_le joins the generic arithmetic lemma to orderOf_dvd_natCard
  and the stock Matrix.card_GL_field theorem; this is the essential bridge from
  arithmetic to actual elements of the actual group.
* no_largePrimeFactor_four proves the event's emptiness for every A at p >= 3C.
  The subsequent theorems establish exact zero probability, eventual zero,
  convergence to zero, and failure to converge to one, without sampling.
* no_positive_denominator_function excludes every positive dimension-only
  function, and conjecture_false proves the negation of OriginalClaim.
