\section{The explicit spectral condition}
The source explicitly requires every eigenvalue of the Burau representation
at real parameter points to have real part at least $-1/2$.
Take the legal nonzero real parameter $t=3/4$ and the generator $\sigma_1$
of the three-strand braid group. Its standard Burau matrix has the
actual complex eigenvalue $-3/4$, whose real part is below $-1/2$.
We give the reduced and unreduced versions, and prove the violation
persists under every invertible change of basis.

This refutes the explicit necessary spectral clause of the stated
conjunction. It does not refute an assertion merely about positivity
of the ordinary character trace. It also does not construct or verify
the proposed holomorphic-disk, LKB, or hyperbolic-volume mechanisms.
Those additional structures cannot change an eigenvalue of the Burau
representation by choosing a different basis.

\section{A genuine braid-group representation}
Use the standard Artin presentation
\[
 B_3=\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2\mid
 \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2\rangle.
\]
Morier-Genoud, Ovsienko and Veselov,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04240}{\emph{Burau representation of
braid groups and $q$-rationals}}, p.1, equation (1.1), define the reduced
Burau representation on these generators by
\[
 A(t)=\begin{pmatrix}-t&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\t&-t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their p.2 defines specialization at every nonzero complex $t$,
including nonzero real parameters. Lean implements these very matrices,
proves both determinants equal $-t$, and proves $ABA=BAB$ for all
complex $t$. For $t\ne0$ they are actual elements of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf C)$.
The universal property of the presented group then constructs the
group homomorphism and proves its values on $\sigma_1$.
This is not an isolated matrix assigned a representation's name.

For the standard unreduced version, use
\[
 U(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1-t&t&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 V(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1-t&t\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These are the generator blocks in Kassel and Turaev,
\href{https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~bigelow/books/kasselturaev.pdf}
{\emph{Braid Groups}}, Section 3.1.1, pp.93--94.
Lean separately proves their determinants, braid relation, actual
invertibility, induced group homomorphism, and generator value.

\section{Actual eigenvectors and arbitrary bases}
At $t=3/4$, the nonzero vectors
\[
 e=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 u=\begin{pmatrix}-3\\4\\0\end{pmatrix}
\]
satisfy
\[
 A(3/4)e=-\frac34 e,\qquad
 U(3/4)u=-\frac34 u.
\]
Lean proves both vector equations and both nonzeroness conditions, then
uses Mathlib's actual \texttt{Module.End.HasEigenvalue} for the
complex linear maps induced by these matrices. The result is a complex
eigenvalue statement, not just an unconnected scalar inequality.
It also proves that the parameter is nonzero, has zero imaginary part,
and lies strictly between zero and one on the real axis, and that
\[
 \operatorname{Re}(-3/4)=-3/4<-1/2.
\]

For any invertible complex matrix $P$, if $Mv=\mu v$ and $v\ne0$,
put $w=P^{-1}v$. Invertibility implies $w\ne0$, and
\[
 (P^{-1}MP)w=P^{-1}Mv=\mu P^{-1}v=\mu w.
\]
The general Lean theorem \texttt{conjugate\_eigenvalue} proves this
using actual invertible matrices and nonzero vectors. Applying it in
dimensions two and three shows that every conjugate of the respective
$\sigma_1$ matrix violates the requested real-part lower bound.
Thus a proposed Hill basis, if it is a basis of the same representation,
cannot repair this spectral violation. No definition of that unspecified
basis is needed to prove the stronger assertion for every basis.
