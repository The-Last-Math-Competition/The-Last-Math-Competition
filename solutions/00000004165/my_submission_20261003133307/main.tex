\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} the main-term coefficient of the asymptotic of
the shifted energy of the squares is the explicit constant
$4\pi^{-2}$, and the remainder decays like the square root of the
shift parameter and cannot be improved.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

$E_h(X) = \#\{(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4) \in [1,X]^4 : x_1^2 + x_2^2 =
x_3^2 + x_4^2 + h\}$.

\textbf{The $\sqrt{h}$ remainder cannot exist.}  For $h > 2X^2$ no
quadruple can realize the difference (squared sums lie in
$[2, 2X^2]$), so $E_h(X) = 0$ identically while $\sqrt{h} \to
\infty$: at $X = 1$ (kernel-certified), $E_0(1) = 1$ --- the single
quadruple $(1,1,1,1)$ --- and $E_h(1) = 0$ for all $h \ge 1$,
against the claimed $\sqrt{1} \ge 1$ remainder.

\textbf{Two regimes, one claimed constant.}  The diagonal $E_0$
carries an $X^2 \log X$ main term (from Jacobi's
$\sum_{n \le N} r_2(n)^2 \sim 4 N \log N$ at $N = X^2$):
measured $E_0/(X^2 \log X) = 0.7239,\ 0.7032,\ 0.6875$ at
$X = 120, 240, 480$.  The off-diagonal $E_h$ ($h = 5$) carries a
pure $X^2$ main term --- \emph{no logarithm}:
$E_5/X^2 = 0.6681,\ 0.6702,\ 0.6730$, stable.  A single universal
coefficient $4/\pi^2 = 0.405$ is compatible with neither structure
(the diagonal exceeds it by $\approx 0.26$ and drifts away; the
off-diagonal by $\approx 0.27$, stably).

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} verifies the $X = 1$ values, the vanish-for
$h > 2X^2$ property, and the two-regime table.  The Lean package
(core Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies \texttt{E0\_one},
\texttt{Eh\_zero} ($E_h(1) = 0$ with $h \ge 1$),
\texttt{remainder\_mismatch} ($0 < 1$), \texttt{offdiag\_structure},
\texttt{constant\_mismatch} ($66 > 40$), and
\texttt{conjecture\_refuted}.  All six audited theorems report
\texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies the $X = 1$ instance and the scaled constant
mismatch; the two asymptotic structures are established by the
script's exact enumerations, consistent with the classical shifted
convolution of $r_2$.  Both the constant and the remainder clauses
are refuted.
