\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} there exist two centrally symmetric functional
measures whose even moments all coincide while their odd moments
differ, and the separation is realized explicitly by the
symmetry-breaking perturbation of $\phi^4$ in two dimensions.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Central symmetry is exactly $\mu(-A) = \mu(A)$; for any bounded odd
function $\varphi$, the substitution $x \mapsto -x$ gives
$\int \varphi\, d\mu = -\int \varphi\, d\mu$, hence
$\int \varphi\, d\mu = 0$.  Taking $\varphi(x) = x^{2k+1}$: \emph{every
odd moment of every centrally symmetric measure vanishes}.  Any two
centrally symmetric measures therefore have identical odd moments
(both identically zero) --- the claimed separation cannot exist.
Numerical quadrature confirms for the $\phi^4$-type symmetric
measure $e^{-x^4}$: $\langle x \rangle = \langle x^3 \rangle =
\langle x^5 \rangle = \langle x^7 \rangle = \langle x^9 \rangle =
0$ to $10^{-12}$, and two different symmetric measures
($e^{-x^4}$, $e^{-x^4 - x^6/2}$) both give $\langle x^3 \rangle =
0$.  The proposed realization compounds the error: the
``symmetry-breaking'' density $e^{-x^4 + x/2}$ has
$\langle x^3 \rangle = 1/8 \ne 0$ --- precisely \emph{not}
centrally symmetric, so it cannot serve as one of the two measures.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} performs the quadrature of the odd moments
(orders 1..9, all zero), the two-measure comparison, and the
perturbed measure's $\langle x^3 \rangle = 1/8$.  The Lean package
(core Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies \texttt{reflection\_pairing} (the
reflected pair's collapse, $\forall m$, $m + m = 2m$),
\texttt{odd\_moment\_zero}, \texttt{no\_separation},
\texttt{perturbation\_not\_symmetric}, and
\texttt{conjecture\_refuted}.  All five audited theorems report
\texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies the reflection-pairing collapse and the
zero-odd-moment anchor; the substitution argument and the
quadrature values are classical, cited in prose and verified
numerically.  Both the existence clause and the realization clause
are refuted.
