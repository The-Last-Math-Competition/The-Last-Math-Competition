\section*{Odd moments under central symmetry}
Let $\mu$ be a centrally symmetric measure, so that it is invariant under the
map $T(x)=-x$.  Assume the relevant moments exist.  For every $k\ge0$, the
change of variables $x\mapsto-x$ gives
\begin{align*}
 m_{2k+1}(\mu)
 &=\int x^{2k+1}\,d\mu(x)\\
 &=\int (-x)^{2k+1}\,d\mu(x)\\
 &=-m_{2k+1}(\mu).
\end{align*}
Hence $2m_{2k+1}(\mu)=0$, and over the real numbers
$m_{2k+1}(\mu)=0$.

\begin{proposition}
Two centrally symmetric measures cannot have different odd moments.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the calculation above to each measure.  Every odd moment of each measure
is zero, so their odd moments agree term by term.
\end{proof}

The equality or inequality of the even moments is irrelevant to this
obstruction.  Moreover, a symmetry-breaking perturbation that makes an odd
moment nonzero also makes the measure non-centrally-symmetric, so it cannot be
the promised witness.

The Lean development formalizes the complete logical consequence for moment
sequences and verifies cancellation on explicit finite paired supports.
