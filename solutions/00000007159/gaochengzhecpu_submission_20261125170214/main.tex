\begin{abstract}
Conjecture \texttt{00000007159} proposes that for multiplicative inequalities of matrices (associated with matrix multiplicative trace / min-max extrema, abbreviated MMT), the complete system of trace-product inequalities holds and is generated by the simplex lattice. Let $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)} = \{\alpha = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_m) \in \mathbb{N}^m : \alpha_1 + \dots + \alpha_m = k\}$ denote the $m$-variable degree-$k$ integer simplex lattice. Because the concise formulation leaves open which canonical trace-product hierarchy on $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$ is intended, we analyze all six natural mathematical realizations for positive semidefinite (and strictly positive definite) matrices $A_1,\dots,A_m$: (1) simplex-lattice ordered-product positivity $\tr(A_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots A_m^{\alpha_m}) \ge 0$; (2) lattice-midpoint log-convexity $F(\beta)^2 \le F(\alpha)F(\gamma)$ for $2\beta = \alpha + \gamma$ in $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$; (3) squared Frobenius-norm (Hermitian sandwich) log-convexity $G(\beta)^2 \le G(\alpha)G(\gamma)$ where $G(\alpha) = \|A_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots A_m^{\alpha_m}\|_F^2$; (4) lattice-semigroup trace-product submultiplicativity $F(\alpha + \beta) \le F(\alpha)F(\beta)$; (5) Lieb--Thirring / word-grouping extrema $\tr(W(A_1,\dots,A_m)) \le F(\alpha(W))$; and (6) both ordered ($\Mord_\alpha$) and word-averaged Hurwitz/BMV ($\Psym_\alpha$) symmetric matrix Muirhead majorization systems generated by elementary $T$-transfers on $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$.

We first prove from first principles why the conjecture appears compelling: in the commuting positive semidefinite regime ($m \ge 1$ arbitrary), all six systems hold universally across all dimensions $n$ and degrees $k$; moreover, even for noncommuting positive semidefinite matrices, the two-matrix simplex lattice $\DeltaLat_k^{(2)}$ ($m = 2$) still satisfies universal midpoint log-convexity $\tr(A^{p+1}B^{q+1})^2 \le \tr(A^{p+2}B^q)\tr(A^p B^{q+2})$, Schur-convexity of $\Mord_{(p,q)}(A,B)$, degree-$2$ submultiplicativity $0 \le \tr(AB) \le \tr(A)\tr(B)$, and low-degree word-grouping inequalities. Nevertheless, we prove that Conjecture \texttt{00000007159} is \textbf{false}: every one of the six candidate simplex-lattice systems fails already for $2 \times 2$ strictly positive definite symmetric integer Gram matrices $A_i = L_i^T L_i \in M_2(\mathbb{Z})$ with $\det L_i \ne 0$. All positive theorems and counterexamples are formally verified from scratch in Core Lean 4.19.0 without external dependencies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Original Statement and Terminological Analysis}

The verbatim statement of Conjecture \texttt{00000007159} in \texttt{SOURCE.md} reads:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{English.} Definition: MMT extrema with the multiplicative inequalities of matrices. Conjecture: The complete system of trace product inequalities holds, and the generation of the system is the simplex lattice. (simplex-lattice MMT trace-product system)

\textbf{Chinese (translated context).} Definition: MMT extrema: multiplicative inequalities of matrices. Conjecture: Family of multiplicative inequalities: the complete system of product inequalities of traces [holds], and the generation of the system is the simplex lattice. (MMT simplex-lattice product-trace inequality complete generating system)
\end{quote}

Notice that the original text uses both ``product inequalities of traces'' (\emph{ji de chengji budengshi}, e.g., inequalities comparing products or powers of traces such as $F(\beta)^2 \le F(\alpha)F(\gamma)$ or $F(\alpha+\beta) \le F(\alpha)F(\beta)$) and ``product-trace inequalities'' (\emph{chengji ji budengshi}, e.g., inequalities for traces of matrix products $\tr(A_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots A_m^{\alpha_m})$ and matrix words $\tr(W(A_1,\dots,A_m))$). In matrix analysis, multiplicative matrix inequalities arise when studying products of Hermitian positive semidefinite (PSD) or symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices $A_1,\dots,A_m \succeq 0$, where eigenvalues and traces of products are governed by variational min-max principles and Schatten--H\"older / Lieb--Thirring inequalities \cite{Bhatia1997,HornJohnson1991,LiebThirring1976}.

Let $\mathbb{N} = \{0,1,2,\dots\}$. For $m \ge 1$ matrices of size $n \times n$ and total degree $k \ge 1$, the \emph{integer simplex lattice} is
\[
\DeltaLat_k^{(m)} = \left\{ \alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m) \in \mathbb{N}^m : \sum_{i=1}^m \alpha_i = k \right\},
\qquad \bigl|\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}\bigr| = \binom{k + m - 1}{m - 1}.
\]
Its $m$ extremal vertices $k e_i = (0,\dots,k,\dots,0)$ correspond to the pure power traces $\tr(A_i^k)$. By the cyclic Schatten--H\"older inequality \cite{Bhatia1997}, every matrix word $W(A_1,\dots,A_m)$ of multi-degree $\alpha \in \DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$ obeys the pure vertex bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex-holder}
\bigl|\tr\bigl(W(A_1,\dots,A_m)\bigr)\bigr|^k \le \prod_{i=1}^m \tr(A_i^k)^{\alpha_i}.
\end{equation}
However, \eqref{eq:vertex-holder} compares $\alpha$ directly to the $m$ \emph{vertices} of the simplex rather than generating a complete system of inequalities across the \emph{simplex lattice} $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$. In classical commutative algebra and majorization theory (such as H\"older log-convexity and Muirhead's theorem \cite{Muirhead1903,MarshallOlkinArnold2011}), a ``complete system of trace-product inequalities generated by the simplex lattice'' means that elementary local relations on $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$---lattice midpoints $2\beta = \alpha + \gamma$, additive decompositions $\alpha + \beta$, word abelianizations $W \mapsto \alpha(W)$, or elementary majorization transfers $\alpha \to \alpha - e_p + e_q$---generate valid trace inequalities throughout $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$.

\subsection{Six Canonical Candidate Systems}

To ensure an airtight and comprehensive resolution, we test all six natural interpretations over real symmetric Gram PSD matrices $A_i = X_i^T X_i \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ (and restrict counterexamples to strictly positive definite integer Gram matrices $A_i = L_i^T L_i \in M_2(\mathbb{Z})$ with $\det L_i \ne 0$, which automatically refutes every weaker matrix class including general PSD, Hermitian, real symmetric, and arbitrary square matrices).

For $\alpha = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_m) \in \DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$ and $A = (A_1,\dots,A_m)$, define the ordered product trace and squared Frobenius norm (Hermitian sandwich trace):
\begin{align*}
F_A(\alpha) &= \tr\bigl(A_1^{\alpha_1} A_2^{\alpha_2} \cdots A_m^{\alpha_m}\bigr), \\
G_A(\alpha) &= \bigl\|A_1^{\alpha_1} A_2^{\alpha_2} \cdots A_m^{\alpha_m}\bigr\|_F^2
= \tr\bigl((A_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots A_m^{\alpha_m})^T (A_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots A_m^{\alpha_m})\bigr).
\end{align*}
For $m = 3$ and $\alpha = (a,b,c) \in \DeltaLat_k^{(3)}$, define also the $S_3$-symmetrized \emph{Ordered Matrix Muirhead} functional and \emph{Word-Averaged (Hurwitz/BMV) Matrix Muirhead} functional:
\begin{align*}
\Mord_{(a,b,c)}(A_1,A_2,A_3) &= \sum_{\sigma \in S_3} \tr\bigl(A_{\sigma(1)}^a A_{\sigma(2)}^b A_{\sigma(3)}^c\bigr), \\
\Psym_{(a,b,c)}(A_1,A_2,A_3) &= \binom{k}{a,b,c}^{-1} \sum_{\sigma \in S_3} \sum_{\substack{W : \mathrm{wt}(W)=(a,b,c)}} \tr\bigl(W(A_{\sigma(1)},A_{\sigma(2)},A_{\sigma(3)})\bigr),
\end{align*}
where $\binom{k}{a,b,c} = \frac{k!}{a!\,b!\,c!}$ counts words of multi-degree $(a,b,c)$.

\begin{definition}[Candidate Simplex-Lattice Systems]\label{def:systems}
For dimension $n \ge 1$ and arity $m \ge 1$, we define:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{System $\mathcal{S}_1$ (Ordered-Product Positivity):} $F_A(\alpha) \ge 0$ for all $\alpha \in \DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$.
\item \textbf{System $\mathcal{S}_2$ (Simplex-Lattice Midpoint Log-Convexity):} $F_A(\beta)^2 \le F_A(\alpha) F_A(\gamma)$ whenever $\alpha,\beta,\gamma \in \DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$ satisfy $2\beta = \alpha + \gamma$.
\item \textbf{System $\mathcal{S}_3$ (Frobenius-Sandwich Log-Convexity):} $G_A(\beta)^2 \le G_A(\alpha) G_A(\gamma)$ whenever $\alpha,\beta,\gamma \in \DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$ satisfy $2\beta = \alpha + \gamma$.
\item \textbf{System $\mathcal{S}_4$ (Lattice-Semigroup Trace-Product Submultiplicativity):} $F_A(\alpha + \beta) \le F_A(\alpha) F_A(\beta)$ for all nonzero $\alpha \in \DeltaLat_{k_1}^{(m)}$ and $\beta \in \DeltaLat_{k_2}^{(m)}$.
\item \textbf{System $\mathcal{S}_5$ (Word-Grouping / Lieb--Thirring Extrema):} $\tr(W(A_1,\dots,A_m)) \le F_A(\alpha(W))$ for every word $W$ of multi-degree $\alpha(W) \in \DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$.
\item \textbf{Systems $\mathcal{S}_{6a}, \mathcal{S}_{6b}$ (Ordered and Word-Averaged Matrix Muirhead Majorization):} Along every elementary Robin-Hood transfer $\alpha \to \beta = \alpha - e_p + e_q$ ($\alpha_p \ge \alpha_q + 2$) on $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$, $\Mord_\alpha \ge \Mord_\beta$ ($\mathcal{S}_{6a}$) and $\Psym_\alpha \ge \Psym_\beta$ ($\mathcal{S}_{6b}$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\section{Positive Regimes: Commuting Systems and the Two-Matrix Hierarchy}

Before presenting the counterexamples, we establish the exact positive theorems that explain why low-arity and commuting experiments appear to support Conjecture \texttt{00000007159}. All proofs below are purely algebraic over any ordered commutative ring (avoiding spectral decompositions or irrational matrix roots), matching our Core Lean 4.19.0 formalization.

\begin{lemma}[Frobenius Cauchy--Schwarz and Submultiplicativity]\label{lem:frob}
For $X, Y \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$, define $\langle X, Y \rangle_F = \tr(X^T Y) = \sum_{i,j=1}^n X_{ij} Y_{ij}$ and $\|X\|_F^2 = \langle X, X \rangle_F \ge 0$. Then
\[
\langle X, Y \rangle_F^2 \le \|X\|_F^2 \, \|Y\|_F^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\|X Y\|_F^2 \le \|X\|_F^2 \, \|Y\|_F^2.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expanding Lagrange's identity gives
\[
0 \le \sum_{i,j=1}^n \sum_{k,l=1}^n (X_{ij} Y_{kl} - X_{kl} Y_{ij})^2
= 2\,\|X\|_F^2 \|Y\|_F^2 - 2\,\langle X, Y \rangle_F^2,
\]
proving the first inequality. Applying $1$-dimensional Cauchy--Schwarz to each entry $(XY)_{ij}^2 = \bigl(\sum_{k=1}^n X_{ik} Y_{kj}\bigr)^2 \le \bigl(\sum_{k=1}^n X_{ik}^2\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{l=1}^n Y_{lj}^2\bigr)$ and summing over $i,j$ yields $\|XY\|_F^2 \le \|X\|_F^2 \|Y\|_F^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Commuting PSD Completeness across All $m, k, n$]\label{thm:commuting}
Let $D_1, \dots, D_m \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$ be commuting non-negative diagonal matrices, $D_i = \mathrm{diag}(d_{i,1},\dots,d_{i,n})$ with $d_{i,j} \ge 0$. Then for all $m \ge 1$ and $k \ge 1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every word $W$ of weight $\alpha \in \DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$, $\tr(W(D_1,\dots,D_m)) = F_D(\alpha) = \sum_{j=1}^n \prod_{i=1}^m d_{i,j}^{\alpha_i} \ge 0$.
\item Whenever $2\beta = \alpha + \gamma$ in $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$, $F_D(\beta)^2 \le F_D(\alpha) F_D(\gamma)$.
\item For all $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{N}^m$, $F_D(\alpha + \beta) \le F_D(\alpha) F_D(\beta)$.
\item Whenever $\alpha \succ \beta$ in $\DeltaLat_k^{(m)}$, $\Mord_\alpha(D_1,\dots,D_m) = \Psym_\alpha(D_1,\dots,D_m) \ge \Mord_\beta(D_1,\dots,D_m)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Part (1) is immediate from diagonal multiplication. For (2), set $u_j = \prod_{i=1}^m d_{i,j}^{\alpha_i} \ge 0$, $v_j = \prod_{i=1}^m d_{i,j}^{\gamma_i} \ge 0$, and $w_j = \prod_{i=1}^m d_{i,j}^{\beta_i} \ge 0$. Since $2\beta_i = \alpha_i + \gamma_i$, we have $w_j^2 = u_j v_j$ for each $j$. For every pair $(j,l)$, $(u_j v_l + u_l v_j)^2 - (2 w_j w_l)^2 = (u_j v_l - u_l v_j)^2 \ge 0$, and since $u_j v_l + u_l v_j \ge 0$ and $2 w_j w_l \ge 0$, we obtain $2 w_j w_l \le u_j v_l + u_l v_j$. Summing over $j, l \in \{1,\dots,n\}$ gives $2 F_D(\beta)^2 \le 2 F_D(\alpha) F_D(\gamma)$. For (3), $u_j, v_j \ge 0$ implies $F_D(\alpha+\beta) = \sum_{j=1}^n u_j v_j \le \bigl(\sum_{j=1}^n u_j\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{l=1}^n v_l\bigr) = F_D(\alpha) F_D(\beta)$ by dropping non-negative off-diagonal products $u_j v_l$ ($j \ne l$). Finally, (4) follows by summing classical scalar Muirhead's inequality \cite{Muirhead1903} over each diagonal index $j = 1,\dots,n$.
\end{proof}

Even more strikingly, on the two-matrix simplex lattice $\DeltaLat_k^{(2)}$ ($m = 2$), noncommutativity does \emph{not} destroy midpoint log-convexity or low-degree word grouping:

\begin{theorem}[Universal Two-Matrix Positive Identities and Log-Convexity]\label{thm:two-mat}
Let $A = X^T X$ and $B = Y^T Y$ be arbitrary $n \times n$ Gram PSD matrices ($X, Y \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Degree-$2$ bounds:} Both $0 \le \tr(AB) \le \tr(A)\,\tr(B)$ and $\tr(AB)^2 \le \tr(A^2)\,\tr(B^2)$ hold.
\item \textbf{Degree-$4$ Lieb--Thirring commutator identity:} For any symmetric $A, B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$,
\[
2\,\tr(A^2 B^2) - 2\,\tr\bigl((AB)^2\bigr) = \bigl\|[A, B]\bigr\|_F^2 \ge 0,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad \tr\bigl((AB)^2\bigr) \le \tr(A^2 B^2).
\]
\item \textbf{Degree-$5$ word-grouping identity:} For $A = X^T X$ and symmetric $B \in M_n(\mathbb{R})$,
\[
\tr(A^3 B^2) - \tr(A^2 B A B) = \bigl\|X [A, B]\bigr\|_F^2 \ge 0,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad \tr(A^2 B A B) \le \tr(A^3 B^2).
\]
\item \textbf{Universal log-convexity on $\DeltaLat_k^{(2)}$:} For all integers $p, q \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m2-logconv}
\tr\bigl(A^{p+1} B^{q+1}\bigr)^2 \le \tr\bigl(A^{p+2} B^q\bigr)\,\tr\bigl(A^p B^{q+2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) By cyclic trace, $\tr(AB) = \tr(X^T X Y^T Y) = \tr(Y X^T X Y^T) = \|X Y^T\|_F^2 \ge 0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}, $\|X Y^T\|_F^2 \le \|X\|_F^2 \|Y^T\|_F^2 = \tr(A)\,\tr(B)$, while $\tr(AB)^2 = \langle A, B \rangle_F^2 \le \|A\|_F^2 \|B\|_F^2 = \tr(A^2)\,\tr(B^2)$.

(2) Since $A^T = A$ and $B^T = B$, $[A,B]^T = (AB - BA)^T = BA - AB$. Hence
\begin{align*}
\|[A,B]\|_F^2 &= \tr\bigl((BA - AB)(AB - BA)\bigr) \\
&= \tr(BAAB) - \tr(BABA) - \tr(ABAB) + \tr(ABBA)
= 2\,\tr(A^2 B^2) - 2\,\tr\bigl((AB)^2\bigr).
\end{align*}

(3) Similarly, setting $C = [A,B]$ so $C^T = BA - AB$ and $X^T X = A$,
\begin{align*}
\|X C\|_F^2 &= \tr(C^T X^T X C) = \tr\bigl((BA - AB) A (AB - BA)\bigr) \\
&= \tr(B A^3 B) - \tr(B A^2 B A) - \tr(A B A^2 B) + \tr(A B A B A)
= \tr(A^3 B^2) - \tr(A^2 B A B),
\end{align*}
since cyclic invariance equates all three last traces: $\tr(B A^2 B A) = \tr(A B A^2 B) = \tr(A B A B A) = \tr(A^2 B A B)$.

(4) We prove \eqref{eq:m2-logconv} without matrix square roots by splitting into the four parities of $(p, q)$ and applying $\langle U, V \rangle_F^2 \le \|U\|_F^2 \|V\|_F^2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Case 1 ($p = 2r, q = 2s$):} Choose $U = A^{r+1} B^s$ and $V = A^r B^{s+1}$. Then $\langle U, V \rangle_F = \tr(B^s A^{r+1} A^r B^{s+1}) = \tr(A^{2r+1} B^{2s+1})$, $\|U\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r+2} B^{2s})$, and $\|V\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r} B^{2s+2})$.
\item \emph{Case 2 ($p = 2r, q = 2s + 1$):} Choose $U = Y B^s A^{r+1}$ and $V = Y B^{s+1} A^r$. Using $Y^T Y = B$, $\langle U, V \rangle_F = \tr(A^{r+1} B^s B B^{s+1} A^r) = \tr(A^{2r+1} B^{2s+2})$, $\|U\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r+2} B^{2s+1})$, and $\|V\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r} B^{2s+3})$.
\item \emph{Case 3 ($p = 2r + 1, q = 2s$):} Choose $U = X A^{r+1} B^s$ and $V = X A^r B^{s+1}$. Using $X^T X = A$, $\langle U, V \rangle_F = \tr(B^s A^{r+1} A A^r B^{s+1}) = \tr(A^{2r+2} B^{2s+1})$, $\|U\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r+3} B^{2s})$, and $\|V\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r+1} B^{2s+2})$.
\item \emph{Case 4 ($p = 2r + 1, q = 2s + 1$):} Choose $U = X A^{r+1} B^s Y^T$ and $V = X A^r B^{s+1} Y^T$. Using both $X^T X = A$ and $Y^T Y = B$, cyclic trace yields $\langle U, V \rangle_F = \tr(A^{2r+2} B^{2s+2})$, $\|U\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r+3} B^{2s+1})$, and $\|V\|_F^2 = \tr(A^{2r+1} B^{2s+3})$. \qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two-Matrix Ordered Muirhead Inequality]\label{cor:m2-muirhead}
For all PSD matrices $A, B \succeq 0$ and all $(p, q) \succ (p', q')$ in $\DeltaLat_k^{(2)}$, $\tr(A^p B^q) + \tr(B^p A^q) \ge \tr(A^{p'} B^{q'}) + \tr(B^{p'} A^{q'})$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:m2-logconv} and the AM--GM inequality, the non-negative sequence $g(p) = \tr(A^p B^{k-p})$ satisfies
\[
g(p+1) \le \sqrt{g(p+2)\,g(p)} \le \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(g(p+2) + g(p)\bigr),
\]
so $g$ is discretely convex on $\{0,1,\dots,k\}$. Therefore $h(p) = g(p) + g(k-p)$ is symmetric and discretely convex on $\{0,\dots,k\}$, hence non-increasing on $[0, \lfloor k/2 \rfloor]$ and non-decreasing on $[\lceil k/2 \rceil, k]$, which is equivalent to majorization monotonicity on $\DeltaLat_k^{(2)}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete Disproof of All Candidate Systems in $M_2(\mathbb{Z})$}

We now show that as soon as one passes beyond the special regimes of Section~2---to lattice-semigroup submultiplicativity at $m = 2, k = 4$, or to any of the simplex-lattice systems at $m = 3$ on the two-dimensional simplex lattice $\DeltaLat_k^{(3)}$---the conjecture breaks down completely, already for $2 \times 2$ strictly positive definite integer Gram matrices $M = L^T L \in M_2(\mathbb{Z})$ ($\det L \ne 0$, so $\tr(M) > 0$ and $\det(M) = (\det L)^2 \ge 1$).

\begin{proposition}[Disproof of Lattice-Semigroup Submultiplicativity $\mathcal{S}_4$ for $m = 2$ and $m = 3$]\label{prop:submult}
System $\mathcal{S}_4$ fails already at $n = 2$ for both $m = 2$ and $m = 3$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $m = 2$, let $L_A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $L_B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det L_A = \det L_B = 1$), giving unimodular SPD matrices
\[
A = L_A^T L_A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
B = L_B^T L_B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For $\alpha = \beta = (1, 1) \in \DeltaLat_2^{(2)}$ with $\alpha + \beta = (2, 2) \in \DeltaLat_4^{(2)}$, we have $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, $B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, and $A^2 B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} -5 & -9 \\ 9 & 16 \end{pmatrix}$, whence
\[
\tr(A^2 B^2) = 11 > 9 = 3^2 = \tr(AB)^2.
\]
(For the normalized trace $\tau(X) = \frac{1}{2}\tr(X)$, setting $A' = \mathrm{diag}(10,1)$ and $B' = \begin{pmatrix} 11 & 9 \\ 9 & 11 \end{pmatrix}$ similarly gives $\tau(A'^2 B'^2) = 10201 > \frac{14641}{4} = \tau(A' B')^2$.)
\item For $m = 3$, let $L_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $L_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and $L_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det L_i = 1$), giving unimodular SPD matrices
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
C = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
At $(1, 1, 1) = (1, 1, 0) + (0, 0, 1) \in \DeltaLat_3^{(3)}$, we have $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 3 \\ 9 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $ABC = \begin{pmatrix} 44 & 27 \\ 57 & 35 \end{pmatrix}$, so
\[
\tr(ABC) = 79 > 77 = 11 \times 7 = \tr(AB)\,\tr(C).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each matrix product and trace evaluation is verified directly from the displayed $2 \times 2$ entries.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Disproof of Simplex-Lattice Interior Positivity $\mathcal{S}_1$]\label{prop:positivity}
At the first interior lattice point $(1, 1, 1) \in \DeltaLat_3^{(3)}$, System $\mathcal{S}_1$ fails for unimodular SPD matrices in $M_2(\mathbb{Z})$. Specifically, with $L_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $L_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and $L_3 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det L_i = 1$), let
\[
A = L_1^T L_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
B = L_2^T L_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
C = L_3^T L_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 3 \\ 9 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $ABC = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -6 \\ 6 & -7 \end{pmatrix}$, so
\[
F_{(A,B,C)}(1, 1, 1) = \tr(ABC) = 5 - 7 = -2 < 0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Direct multiplication confirms $\det L_1 = 1$, $\det L_2 = 1$, $\det L_3 = 2 - 1 = 1$, and $\tr(ABC) = -2$. Geometrically, three rank-$1$ projections $u_i u_i^T$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ at pairwise $60^\circ$ angles with cyclic inner product $\langle u_1, u_2 \rangle \langle u_2, u_3 \rangle \langle u_3, u_1 \rangle < 0$ have negative product trace, and unimodular Gram perturbations preserve strict negativity.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:positivity} shows that $\log F_A(\alpha)$ is not even real-valued on $\DeltaLat_3^{(3)}$. Crucially, even when we restrict to triples and lattice midpoints where \emph{all} ordered product traces are strictly positive---or replace $F_A$ by the manifestly positive Frobenius norm $G_A(\alpha) = \|A_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots A_m^{\alpha_m}\|_F^2 > 0$---midpoint log-convexity on $\DeltaLat_k^{(3)}$ still fails:

\begin{proposition}[Disproof of Midpoint Log-Convexity $\mathcal{S}_2$ and Frobenius Log-Convexity $\mathcal{S}_3$]\label{prop:logconv}
Systems $\mathcal{S}_2$ and $\mathcal{S}_3$ fail in $M_2(\mathbb{Z})$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Failure of $\mathcal{S}_2$ on $\DeltaLat_4^{(3)}$ with strictly positive traces:} Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = L_1^T L_1$, $B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = L_B^T L_B$ with $L_B = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, and $C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = L_C^T L_C$ with $L_C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. At the midpoint $(1, 2, 1) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl((2, 1, 1) + (0, 3, 1)\bigr) \in \DeltaLat_4^{(3)}$,
\[
F(1, 2, 1) = \tr(A B^2 C) = 36, \quad
F(2, 1, 1) = \tr(A^2 B C) = 39, \quad
F(0, 3, 1) = \tr(B^3 C) = 33,
\]
all strictly positive, yet $F(1, 2, 1)^2 = 36^2 = 1296 > 1287 = 39 \times 33 = F(2, 1, 1)\,F(0, 3, 1)$.

\item \textbf{Failure of $\mathcal{S}_2$ strictly inside the interior of $\DeltaLat_5^{(3)}$ with unimodular SPD matrices:} Keep $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and set $C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = L_{C'}^T L_{C'}$ with $L_{C'} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det = 1$). At the pure interior midpoint $(2, 1, 2) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl((3, 1, 1) + (1, 1, 3)\bigr) \in \DeltaLat_5^{(3)}$,
\begin{align*}
F(2,1,2) &= \tr(A^2 B C^2) = 459, \\
F(3,1,1) &= \tr(A^3 B C) = 207, \qquad F(1,1,3) = \tr(A B C^3) = 1014,
\end{align*}
and $F(2, 1, 2)^2 = 459^2 = 210{,}681 > 209{,}898 = 207 \times 1014 = F(3, 1, 1)\,F(1, 1, 3)$.

\item \textbf{Failure of Frobenius-sandwich log-convexity $\mathcal{S}_3$ on $\DeltaLat_3^{(3)}$:} Keep $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and set $C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = L_{C''}^T L_{C''}$ with $L_{C''} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det = 1$). At $(1, 1, 1) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl((2, 0, 1) + (0, 2, 1)\bigr) \in \DeltaLat_3^{(3)}$,
\[
G(1, 1, 1) = \|ABC\|_F^2 = 43, \quad
G(2, 0, 1) = \|A^2 C\|_F^2 = 7, \quad
G(0, 2, 1) = \|B^2 C\|_F^2 = 70,
\]
so $G(1, 1, 1)^2 = 43^2 = 1849 > 490 = 7 \times 70 = G(2, 0, 1)\,G(0, 2, 1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For (1), $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, $B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, and $B^3 = \begin{pmatrix} 13 & 8 \\ 8 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, whence $A B^2 C = \begin{pmatrix} 8 & 20 \\ 11 & 28 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\tr = 36$), $A^2 B C = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & 20 \\ 11 & 32 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\tr = 39$), and $B^3 C = \begin{pmatrix} 13 & 32 \\ 8 & 20 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\tr = 33$). Parts (2) and (3) follow by analogous direct matrix evaluation; in (3), $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $ABC = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -1 \\ 5 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ yield $\|ABC\|_F^2 = 4^2 + (-1)^2 + 5^2 + (-1)^2 = 43$, while $A^2 C = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ gives $\|A^2 C\|_F^2 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 = 7$ and $B^2 C = \begin{pmatrix} 7 & -2 \\ 4 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ gives $\|B^2 C\|_F^2 = 7^2 + (-2)^2 + 4^2 + (-1)^2 = 70$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Disproof of Three-Matrix Word-Grouping / Lieb--Thirring Extrema $\mathcal{S}_5$]\label{prop:lt3}
Unlike the two-matrix inequality $\tr((AB)^2) \le \tr(A^2 B^2)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:two-mat}(2)), System $\mathcal{S}_5$ fails at $\alpha = (2, 2, 2) \in \DeltaLat_6^{(3)}$ in $M_2(\mathbb{Z})$. With $L_C = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det L_C = 4$), let
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
C = L_C^T L_C = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -2 \\ -2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For the alternating word $W = A B C A B C = (ABC)^2$ of multi-degree $(2, 2, 2) \in \DeltaLat_6^{(3)}$, we have $ABC = \begin{pmatrix} 11 & 2 \\ 14 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $(ABC)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 149 & 30 \\ 210 & 44 \end{pmatrix}$. Meanwhile $A^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, $B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, and $C^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 29 & -18 \\ -18 & 20 \end{pmatrix}$ give $A^2 B^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 19 & 12 \\ 30 & 19 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A^2 B^2 C^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 335 & -102 \\ 528 & -160 \end{pmatrix}$, so $\tr(A^2 B^2 C^2) = 335 - 160 = 175 > 0$, whereas
\[
\tr\bigl((ABC)^2\bigr) = 149 + 44 = 193 > 175 = \tr(A^2 B^2 C^2),
\]
and simultaneously $\|ABC\|_F^2 = \tr(A B C^2 B A) = 11^2 + 2^2 + 14^2 + 4^2 = 337 > 175 = \tr(A^2 B^2 C^2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All intermediate $2 \times 2$ entries are computed explicitly above.
\end{proof}

Finally, one might ask whether symmetrizing over all $m!$ permutations of the matrices---or additionally averaging over all noncommutative words of weight $\alpha$, as in the multivariate trace polynomial $\tr((t_1 A_1 + \dots + t_m A_m)^k)$ studied in connection with the Bessis--Moussa--Villani conjecture and Stahl's theorem \cite{LiebSeiringer2004,Stahl2013}---restores Muirhead majorization on $\DeltaLat_k^{(3)}$. Both symmetrized systems still fail:

\begin{proposition}[Disproof of Symmetrized Ordered and Word-Averaged Muirhead Systems $\mathcal{S}_{6a}, \mathcal{S}_{6b}$]\label{prop:muirhead}
Both $S_3$-symmetrized matrix Muirhead systems fail along elementary Robin-Hood transfers on $\DeltaLat_k^{(3)}$ for unimodular SPD matrices in $M_2(\mathbb{Z})$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Failure of Ordered Muirhead $\mathcal{S}_{6a}$ on $\DeltaLat_6^{(3)}$:} Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, and $C = L^T L = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & -3 \\ -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with $L = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det L = 1$). Along the covering step $(3, 2, 1) \succ (2, 2, 2)$ in $\DeltaLat_6^{(3)}$,
\[
\Mord_{(3,2,1)}(A, B, C) = -8606 < -6900 = \Mord_{(2,2,2)}(A, B, C) < 0.
\]
Moreover, $\mathcal{S}_{6a}$ also fails when all symmetrized traces are strictly positive: for the SPD matrices $M_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ -2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, $M_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, and $M_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ along $(4, 1, 1) \succ (3, 2, 1)$ in $\DeltaLat_6^{(3)}$, $0 < \Mord_{(4,1,1)}(M_1,M_2,M_3) = 147{,}776 < 147{,}792 = \Mord_{(3,2,1)}(M_1,M_2,M_3)$.

\item \textbf{Failure of Word-Averaged Hurwitz/BMV Muirhead $\mathcal{S}_{6b}$ on $\DeltaLat_5^{(3)}$:} Let $L_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $L_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and $L_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ ($\det L_i = 1$), giving unimodular SPD matrices
\[
A = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -3 \\ -3 & 10 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
C = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Along the covering step $(3, 1, 1) \succ (2, 2, 1)$ in $\DeltaLat_5^{(3)}$ (with word counts $\binom{5}{3,1,1} = 20$ and $\binom{5}{2,2,1} = 30$), the $S_3$-symmetrized word sums are $144{,}480$ and $220{,}800$, giving strictly positive word-averaged traces
\[
0 < \Psym_{(3,1,1)}(A, B, C) = \frac{144{,}480}{20} = 7224 < 7360 = \frac{220{,}800}{30} = \Psym_{(2,2,1)}(A, B, C).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each finite sum of $2 \times 2$ integer matrix traces is exact and verified both by recursive Pascal dynamic programming on $\DeltaLat_k^{(3)}$ (in Lean 4 and Python) and by direct enumeration over all $20$ and $30$ distinct words in Python.
\end{proof}

Combining Propositions~\ref{prop:submult}--\ref{prop:muirhead} establishes our main negative conclusion:

\begin{theorem}[Master Disproof of Conjecture \texttt{00000007159}]\label{thm:main}
Conjecture \texttt{00000007159} is \textbf{false}. Already in dimension $n = 2$ over strictly positive definite symmetric integer Gram matrices $A_i = L_i^T L_i \in M_2(\mathbb{Z})$ ($\det L_i \ne 0$), none of the six canonical simplex-lattice multiplicative trace-product systems ($\mathcal{S}_1$, $\mathcal{S}_2$, $\mathcal{S}_3$, $\mathcal{S}_4$, $\mathcal{S}_5$, $\mathcal{S}_{6a}$, $\mathcal{S}_{6b}$) holds in general.
\end{theorem}
