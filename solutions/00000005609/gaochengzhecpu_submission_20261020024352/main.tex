\begin{abstract}
For $t\geq1$, the rectangular Euclidean lattice
$\Lambda_t=t\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$
has packing radius $1/2$ and covering radius
$\sqrt{t^2+2}/2$. Its covering-packing gap is therefore
$\sqrt{t^2+2}$, which is unbounded. This disproves the
assertion of an upper endpoint attained by a cubic
lattice. We do not assess the separate BCC minimum
claim. Lean verifies actual Euclidean lattices and both
radius extrema, including discreteness and full rank.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Definitions and the rectangular family}
For a full-rank Euclidean lattice $\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^3$,
its packing radius is
\[
 r_{\rm pack}(\Lambda)=\frac12
     \min_{0\ne v\in\Lambda}\|v\|_2.
\]
Equivalently it is the largest radius for which the open
balls at distinct lattice points are disjoint. Its
covering radius is the least $r\geq0$ such that the
closed radius-$r$ balls about its points cover all of
$\mathbb R^3$. The source defines the gap as the ratio
of covering radius to packing radius:
\[
 g(\Lambda)=r_{\rm cover}(\Lambda)/r_{\rm pack}(\Lambda).
\]

For every real $t\geq1$ take
\[
 \Lambda_t=\{(tm,n,p):m,n,p\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
It is an additive subgroup generated by the three
linearly independent vectors
\[
 (t,0,0),\qquad(0,1,0),\qquad(0,0,1).
\]
Thus its real span is all of $\mathbb R^3$.
Every nonzero lattice vector has a coordinate of
absolute value at least one, hence has Euclidean norm
at least one. Differences of distinct lattice points
have the same property. The lattice is discrete, with
distinct points at distance at least one. Moreover,
$(0,1,0)\in\Lambda_t$ has norm one. Consequently
\[
 r_{\rm pack}(\Lambda_t)=\frac12.
\]
This proves both admissibility as a full-rank discrete
lattice and the packing extremum directly.

\section*{The exact covering radius and unbounded gap}
Given any $x=(x_0,x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb R^3$, choose
integers $m,n,p$ nearest to $x_0/t,x_1,x_2$ respectively.
Then the three coordinate errors to $(tm,n,p)$ have
absolute values at most $t/2,1/2,1/2$. Therefore
\[
 \|x-(tm,n,p)\|_2^2\leq\frac{t^2+2}{4},\qquad
 r_{\rm cover}(\Lambda_t)\leq\frac{\sqrt{t^2+2}}2.
\]
For the reverse inequality use the centre
$c_t=(t/2,1/2,1/2)$. For every integer $k$ we have
$|1/2-k|\geq1/2$. Hence, for every $(tm,n,p)\in\Lambda_t$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|c_t-(tm,n,p)\|_2^2
 &=t^2(1/2-m)^2+(1/2-n)^2+(1/2-p)^2\\
 &\geq\frac{t^2+2}{4}.
 \end{split}
\]
Any radius whose lattice balls cover $c_t$ must be at
least $\sqrt{t^2+2}/2$. The upper bound is therefore
attained, giving
\[
 r_{\rm cover}(\Lambda_t)=\frac{\sqrt{t^2+2}}2,
 \qquad g(\Lambda_t)=\sqrt{t^2+2}.
\]

\begin{theorem}
The covering-packing gap over full-rank Euclidean
lattices in dimension three is unbounded above.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any real $B$, set $t=\max(1,B+1)$. Then $t\geq1$
and $\sqrt{t^2+2}>t\geq B+1>B$. The displayed exact
gap formula supplies an admissible lattice with gap
larger than $B$.
\end{proof}

Thus the spectrum cannot have a finite upper endpoint
attained by a cubic lattice, or by any lattice.
For example the simple cubic lattice $\Lambda_1$ has
gap $\sqrt3$, whereas $\Lambda_4$ has gap $\sqrt{18}$.
Unboundedness alone is not asserted to rule out a closed
half-infinite interval: it refutes the explicit attained
upper-endpoint clause. The separate assertions concerning
the BCC minimum and its value are not assessed.
The source has no restriction on aspect ratio or
well-roundedness. No such restriction is assumed here.

\section*{Formalization and reproducibility}
Lean uses the actual Mathlib type
\texttt{EuclideanSpace R (Fin 3)} and its Euclidean norm.
The map \texttt{point t} sends integer triples to
$(tm,n,p)$; \texttt{latticeGroup} gives its actual additive
subgroup. Theorems \texttt{point\_injective},
\texttt{lattice\_discrete} and
\texttt{lattice\_full\_rank} prove its integer coordinate
representation, actual discrete subtype topology and
full real span for every $t\geq1$.

The formal packing radius is the supremum of the set
of nonnegative $r$ satisfying $2r\leq\|v\|$ for every
nonzero lattice vector. The theorem
\texttt{packing\_geometric} proves equivalence to the
pairwise-distance condition for distinct lattice points.
The formal covering radius is the infimum of the set
of nonnegative $r$ for which every point of the actual
Euclidean space is within distance $r$ of some lattice
point. No explicit radius formula is inserted as a
definition or assumed.

Theorems \texttt{packing\_exact} and
\texttt{covering\_exact} identify these extrema.
The covering proof uses integer rounding for the upper
bound and the half-integer centre for a bound against
\emph{every} lattice point. Theorem
\texttt{valid\_lattice\_unbounded} includes both the
discrete-topology and full-rank certificates in each
unboundedness witness; \texttt{no\_upper\_endpoint}
directly negates a uniform finite upper bound.
The cubic comparison is also checked.
