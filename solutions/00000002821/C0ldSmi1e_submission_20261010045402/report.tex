\begin{abstract}
A constant sequence of symmetric two-point probability measures satisfies a
large deviation principle at speed $n+1$ with rate zero at $-1$ and $1$, and
$+\infty$ elsewhere. This rate is proper, nonnegative, lower semicontinuous,
and good. Every finite nonnegative sublevel is the compact but nonconvex set
$\{-1,1\}$; its exact zero level is the same set. Thus the assertion that LDP
rate level sets are always compact and convex is false, even after adding
goodness. The accompanying Lean project verifies the actual probability
measures and the complete open/closed LDP bounds, using the extended logarithm
at probability zero. It does not assign invented meanings to the undefined
extreme-point counting or affine-degeneration wording.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source and precise logical scope}

The supplied English source states:
\begin{quote}
Definition: Compactness and convexity of LDP level sets: the regularity layer
of rate functions. Conjecture: Convex-hull rigidity of level sets: level sets
of the rate are compact and convex, with the number of extreme points of the
hull an exponential-type finite value of the level; the extreme-point
structure degenerates for affine rates. (level set extreme point spectrum)
\end{quote}
The supplied Chinese version likewise asserts compactness and convexity of
the rate's level sets, then the finite exponential-type count and affine
degeneration. Both versions were read before selecting the example. Neither
specifies a Cram\'er setting, sample means, a convexity hypothesis on the rate,
an ambient finite-dimensional restriction beyond an unspecified space, a
formula for ``exponential-type,'' or a definition of ``degenerates.''

We refute the explicit compact-and-convex assertion under the standard meaning
of an LDP rate. An example on the usual real line suffices for an unqualified
claim. The necessary consequence being refuted is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:claim}
\begin{gathered}
\text{For every sequence of probability measures satisfying an LDP}\\
\text{with a proper good rate }I,\quad
\{x:I(x)\le r\}\text{ is convex for every finite }r\ge0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Adding ``good'' and ``proper'' here narrows the class of potential
counterexamples: the example meets both conditions. Thus it refutes the
unqualified assertion as well as this more restricted necessary consequence.
We also handle the alternate convention that ``level set'' means
$\{x:I(x)=r\}$, by a nonconvex exact zero level. For sublevels, the obstruction
occurs at every finite positive level as well, so it is not confined to the
boundary level zero.

The nouns following ``Definition'' do not provide a mathematical hypothesis
restricting the problem to convex rates or already-convex sublevels. If such
a restriction was intended, it is an additional hypothesis absent from the
supplied text, and our result does not purport to settle that amended problem.
Nor do we claim a formalization of the undefined counting or degeneration
phrases. No such choice is necessary: a conjunction containing the false
convexity assertion is false. We make no independent assertion here about an
extreme-point growth law or affine degeneration.

\section{Definitions and conventions}

The state space is $\R$ with its usual topology and Borel sigma algebra.
A rate function takes values in $[0,+\infty]$ and is lower semicontinuous.
We call it \emph{proper} when it is finite at some point. We call it
\emph{good} when every sublevel $\{I\le r\}$ for finite $r\ge0$ is compact.
The extended logarithm has $\log 0=-\infty$ and agrees with the ordinary real
logarithm on positive finite arguments. Infima of empty sets of rates are
$+\infty$. All limits of logarithmic probabilities below take place in the
extended real line $[-\infty,+\infty]$ with its order topology.

For a positive speed $a_n\to+\infty$, the LDP means that for every open
$G\subseteq\R$ and every closed $F\subseteq\R$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:lower}
 -\inf_{x\in G}I(x)&\le
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_n}\log\mu_n(G),\\
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_n}\log\mu_n(F)&\le
 -\inf_{x\in F}I(x).\label{eq:upper}
\end{align}
This is the definition encoded by \texttt{HasLDP}. It additionally records
that every $\mu_n$ is a probability measure, the speed is positive and tends
to infinity, and $I$ is lower semicontinuous. The formal infimum is an infimum
in $[0,+\infty]$, followed by its canonical embedding in the extended reals
and negation. In particular, empty sets and infinite rates are included.

\section{The example and its regularity}

Set
\[
 S=\{-1,1\},\qquad
 \mu=\tfrac12\delta_{-1}+\tfrac12\delta_1,\qquad
 \mu_n=\mu\quad(n\in\N),\qquad a_n=n+1,
\]
and define
\[
 I(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\in S,\\+\infty,&x\notin S.\end{cases}
\]
Here $\N$ starts at zero. The shift in the speed ensures every denominator
is positive, while $a_n\to+\infty$. Dirac measures are Borel probability
measures and the two weights sum to one, hence every $\mu_n$ is a genuine
probability measure. Equivalently, one may realize the same laws by taking
one symmetric random variable with values $-1$ and $1$ and using it at
every index; no independence or sample-mean assumption is part of the LDP.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:regularity}
The function $I$ is a proper good rate. For every finite $r\ge0$,
$\{I\le r\}=S$, and $\{I=0\}=S$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At either point of $S$, the rate is zero, so $I$ is proper and its minimum
is zero. Off $S$, it is infinite. These observations give the two stated set
equalities. The set $S$ is finite, hence compact and closed. All finite
nonnegative sublevels are consequently compact and closed; the sublevel at
$+\infty$ is the whole space. Thus every sublevel in the ordered codomain
$[0,+\infty]$ is closed, which is the closed-sublevel characterization of
lower semicontinuity. This proves goodness and the rate-function property.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification of the genuine LDP}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:log}
For a finite $p\in[0,+\infty)$,
\[
 \frac{1}{n+1}\log p\ \longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}-\infty,&p=0,\\0,&p>0.\end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $p=0$, the extended logarithm is $-\infty$. Multiplication by the strictly
positive finite number $1/(n+1)$ leaves it $-\infty$ at every index.
If $p>0$, its logarithm is a finite real constant. The real sequence
$1/(n+1)$ tends to zero, so the real product tends to zero. The canonical
embedding of the reals into the extended reals is continuous, giving the
stated extended-real limit. In particular, a totalized real logarithm at
zero is never used in this argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:eventwise}
For every set $A\subseteq\R$, including the empty set,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\log\mu_n(A)
 =-\inf_{x\in A} I(x).
\]
For nonmeasurable sets the left side uses the measure's outer-measure
extension; the LDP itself uses only open and closed sets.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any set $A$, the two Dirac atoms give
\[
 \mu(A)=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{-1\in A\}}
       +\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{1\in A\}}.
\]
If $A\cap S\ne\varnothing$, then $\mu(A)$ is $1/2$ or $1$, and
$\inf_{x\in A}I(x)=0$: nonnegativity gives the lower bound and an atom in
$A$ attains zero. Lemma~\ref{lem:log} gives limit zero, as required.
If $A\cap S=\varnothing$, then $\mu(A)=0$ and every rate in $A$ is infinite,
so its infimum is $+\infty$. This remains true for $A=\varnothing$ under the
empty-infimum convention. The normalized logarithm is identically
$-\infty$ and has that limit, which is precisely minus the rate infimum.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:ldp}
The sequence $(\mu_n)$ satisfies the LDP at speed $a_n=n+1$ with the proper
good rate $I$ defined above.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The probability, speed, and regularity conditions were checked above.
For each open $G$, Proposition~\ref{prop:eventwise} identifies its logarithmic
limit, and hence its liminf, with $-\inf_G I$; this gives
\eqref{eq:lower} with equality. For each closed $F$ the same proposition
identifies its limsup with $-\inf_F I$, giving \eqref{eq:upper} with equality.
No set or endpoint exception is required.
\end{proof}

\section{Failure of convexity}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
Every finite nonnegative sublevel of the proper good LDP rate $I$ above is
nonconvex. Its exact zero level is also nonconvex. Consequently
\eqref{eq:claim}, and hence the source's unqualified compact-and-convex
assertion, is false.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both $-1$ and $1$ belong to $S$, but their midpoint
$\tfrac12(-1)+\tfrac12(1)=0$ does not. Thus $S$ is not convex.
Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity} identifies every indicated sublevel and the exact
zero level with $S$. Theorem~\ref{thm:ldp} verifies all LDP hypotheses.
For the formal negation of the universal statement, take the positive
finite level $r=1$ in this example.
\end{proof}
