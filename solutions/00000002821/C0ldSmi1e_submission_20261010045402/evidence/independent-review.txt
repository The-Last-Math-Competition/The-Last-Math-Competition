INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL CHECKS AND SOURCE MAP

1. State space, probability measures, and absence of a disguised deterministic assertion

Basic.lean:16–21 defines S = {-1,1}, a Measure ℝ equal to (1/2)δ_{-1} + (1/2)δ_1, and I(x)=0 on S and +∞ elsewhere. LDP.lean:35 makes μ_n this same genuine measure at every natural index. Standard real topology and measurable instances apply; no custom topology, altered measure semantics, or nonstandard convexity predicate is introduced.

Basic.lean:74–80 proves the actual measure of every set by the two-atom indicator formula. Basic.lean:82–88 proves IsProbabilityMeasure by calculating μ(ℝ)=1. The field is not a hypothesis or an assumed theorem. Basic.lean:90 supplies finite measure for every event, and 92–96 establishes μ(A)≠0 exactly when A meets S. The two possibilities are μ(A)=0 for a miss and μ(A)∈{1/2,1} for a hit. Report.tex:109–124 and 169–180 match these facts. A constant sequence of laws is permitted in a general LDP; the source imposes no sample-mean or independence restriction.

The arbitrary-set measure formula is legitimate on ℝ: stock Measure.dirac_apply in Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Dirac.lean:50–57 works for every set under MeasurableSingletonClass. Report.tex:165–166 appropriately describes nonmeasurable-set values using the outer-measure extension. The actual LDP only needs open and closed Borel events.

2. Speed and sign of normalization

LDP.lean:14–15 defines IsSpeed as positivity at every index together with Tendsto a atTop atTop. LDP.lean:34,37–42 proves this for a_n=n+1. The n=0 denominator equals 1 and is positive, so there is no zero-index hole. LDP.lean:18–19 normalizes by 1/a_n, in the conventional direction. It does not invert the speed convention or assume exponential decay.

3. Nonnegative extended rate, properness, and normalization

The codomain ℝ≥0∞ in Basic.lean:21 enforces nonnegativity and allows infinite values. Basic.lean:28–30 confirms the endpoint values and I(0)=+∞. Properness at 51 is an explicit witness x=1 with I(x)<+∞. In particular inf I=0 is attained, although no extra axiom asserting normalization is needed. Report.tex:84–91 and 126–138 agree. Infinite off-support values are allowed for ordinary rate functions; assuming a rate must be everywhere finite would be an added condition absent from the source.

4. Lower semicontinuity and every finite sublevel

Basic.lean:32–36 proves {I≤r}=S for every r in ℝ≥0∞ except +∞. This is exactly the nonnegative finite range. Basic.lean:23–26 proves S compact and closed using finiteness and the standard Hausdorff topology of ℝ. Basic.lean:38–45 treats both finite r and r=+∞ when proving lower semicontinuity; the latter sublevel is ℝ and is closed. The stock theorem lowerSemicontinuous_iff_isClosed_preimage at Mathlib/Topology/Semicontinuous.lean:250–253 uses all closed lower halfspaces, precisely the ordinary condition needed here.

Basic.lean:47–49 proves compactness for every r:ℝ≥0. LDP.lean:31–32 defines GoodRate to require lower semicontinuity and all these compact sublevels, and 66 supplies both. Negative real thresholds, if one uses the all-real formulation, have empty sublevels because I is nonnegative; this adds no missing goodness case. Goodness does not require compactness of the +∞ sublevel. Report.tex:126–138 states these distinctions correctly.

The discontinuity from a zero atom value to +∞ nearby does not violate lower semicontinuity: it is an upward jump away from a closed zero set. Every point outside S has a neighborhood disjoint from S. The submission does not confuse lower and upper semicontinuity.

5. Extended logarithms, zeros, and absence of invalid limit multiplication

Limits.lean:10–11 uses ENNReal.log with values in EReal. I checked the stock definition in Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Log/ENNRealLog.lean:42–47: log 0 is -∞, log +∞ is +∞, and the remaining case is Real.log of the finite real value. The positive finite identification used in the proof is the stock theorem at 62–63. The proof does not silently use Lean's totalized real logarithm at zero.

Limits.lean:13–16 proves that every normalized logarithm of zero is -∞, using strict positivity of 1/(n+1). Limits.lean:28–32 then takes a limit of this constant -∞ sequence. It does not multiply a limiting zero by an infinite limiting value or replace 0·(-∞) with the desired answer.

Limits.lean:18–26 handles p≠0 and p≠+∞ separately: the logarithm is a fixed finite real constant; the reciprocal speed tends to zero in ℝ; their product tends to zero; the continuous real-to-EReal embedding transfers the result. Limits.lean:34–39 combines the cases. Report.tex:142–156 gives the same valid argument. The p=1 case has exactly zero logarithm; p=1/2 has a negative finite logarithm tending to zero after normalization.

6. Infima and all event cases, especially the empty set

Basic.lean:98–102 proves inf_A I=0 whenever A meets S, using a zero-rate witness and nonnegativity. Basic.lean:104–109 proves inf_A I=+∞ whenever A misses S. The latter uses an indexed infimum in the complete lattice ℝ≥0∞; it includes A=∅, whose infimum is +∞. It does not require a point to exist in A.

LDP.lean:45–57 combines precisely those two exhaustive cases with the actual event probability. For a hit, the probability is nonzero and finite, and the limit is 0. For a miss, the probability is 0 and the limit is -∞. Coercion ℝ≥0∞→EReal followed by negation sends +∞ to -∞. Thus the target is -inf_A I in both cases. In particular:
  • A=∅: μ(A)=0, inf_A I=+∞, profile≡-∞;
  • A={0}, or any event wholly off S: the same values;
  • A containing exactly one atom: μ(A)=1/2, inf_A I=0, profile→0;
  • A containing both atoms, including ℝ: μ(A)=1, inf_A I=0, profile≡0.

The proof works for every set without having to classify its topology. Report.tex:159–180 includes the empty-event and nonmeasurable-event conventions explicitly and matches the formal theorem.

7. The actual LDP, not a stand-in predicate with missing analytic requirements

LDP.lean:23–28 defines HasLDP with five substantive components: all μ_n probability measures; positive divergent speed; lower semicontinuous I; the open-event lower bound -inf_G I ≤ liminf; and the closed-event upper bound limsup ≤ -inf_F I. The rates, measure sequence, and speed in the bounds are the same arguments as in the predicate. All quantifiers range over the intended sets on ℝ. Directions of the two inequalities are correct. There is no vacuous filter, skipped zero probability, restriction to favorable events, or weakened finite-only endpoint convention.

LDP.lean:59–64 derives both bounds from eventwise convergence. Stock Filter.Tendsto.liminf_eq and limsup_eq in Mathlib/Topology/Algebra/Order/LiminfLimsup.lean:185–193 apply on the nontrivial natural atTop filter to the EReal order topology, including the -∞ limit. The lower bound uses the symmetric equality and the upper bound uses the forward equality. The unused open/closed hypotheses are harmless because the stronger all-event equality was actually established. Report.tex:93–105 and 183–193 match this proof.

8. Nonconvexity and the equality/sublevel ambiguity

Basic.lean:57–63 applies the standard Convex ℝ definition to -1 and 1 with weights 1/2 and 1/2. The midpoint 0 lies outside S, giving a contradiction. Basic.lean:65–68 transfers this to every finite nonnegative sublevel. Basic.lean:53–55 and 70–72 also identify and refute convexity of {I=0}. Hence interpreting “level” as a sublevel or as an exact level does not avoid the counterexample when level zero is allowed.

Report.tex:68–71 and 198–210 correctly distinguish these readings. For positive exact levels, this particular example has empty exact level sets; it does not disprove a different claim limited to nonempty strictly positive exact levels. No such limitation appears in the source. For sublevels the formal final contradiction uses r=1, so it has no reliance on a zero-threshold edge case.

The example's convex hull is of course a different set, [-1,1], and is convex. Neither the Lean proof nor the report confuses a level set with its hull. The source's actual sentence asserts convexity of the level sets themselves. If only properties of the hull were intended, that would be a materially different assertion.

9. Final theorem strength and quantifier audit

LDP.lean:70–75 proves a closed conjunction with no mathematical hypotheses: HasLDP laws speed rate, GoodRate rate, an explicit properness witness, failure of convexity of every finite nonnegative sublevel, and failure of convexity of the exact zero level.

LDP.lean:79–85 proves the negation of:
  for all μ:ℕ→Measure ℝ, a:ℕ→ℝ, I:ℝ→ℝ≥0∞,
  HasLDP μ a I → GoodRate I → (∃x,I x<+∞) →
  for all r:ℝ≥0, Convex ℝ {x | I x≤r}.
It instantiates the hypothetical universal theorem with the concrete measure sequence, speed, and rate and with r=1; it then applies the already proved nonconvexity. There are no hidden free parameters, contradictory assumptions, admitted witnesses, or assumptions of the desired counterexample. Requiring good and proper rates restricts the universal claim's domain, so failure even on this domain refutes the broader unqualified convexity assertion. Report.tex:238–246 accurately describes the quantified formal statement.
