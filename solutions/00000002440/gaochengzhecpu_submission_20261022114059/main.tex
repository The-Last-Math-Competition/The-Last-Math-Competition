\title{Third \'etale homotopy groups are abelian\newline
\large Disproof of Conjecture 00000002440}
\author{gaochengzhecpu}\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Every third homotopy group is abelian by the Eckmann--Hilton
argument. Applying this to each pointed space in an \'etale
homotopy type shows that its third homotopy pro-group has an
abelian-valued representative. Thus no smooth variety can have
the internally nonabelian third \'etale homotopy group requested
in the conjecture. Lean verifies the obstruction for actual
cube-loop homotopy groups and entire diagrams of those groups.
The standard \'etale-type and realization constructions connecting
this universal result to varieties are explicitly stated as
mathematical definition bridges outside the Lean formalization.
\end{abstract}

\section*{The obstruction for actual pointed spaces}
Let $(Y,y)$ be any pointed topological space.
Represent $\pi_3(Y,y)$ by continuous maps
\[
 f:[0,1]^3\longrightarrow Y,\qquad f(\partial[0,1]^3)=\{y\},
\]
modulo homotopy relative to the entire boundary. Concatenating
in either of the first two coordinate directions gives two
operations $m_0,m_1$ on these homotopy classes. Both have the
constant class $e$ as a two-sided unit, and their actual
coordinate concatenations satisfy interchange:
\[
 m_0\bigl(m_1(a,b),m_1(c,d)\bigr)
 =m_1\bigl(m_0(a,c),m_0(b,d)\bigr).
\]
The interchange identity holds for the concrete cube maps;
the unit identities hold on relative-boundary homotopy classes.
Substitution gives
\begin{align*}
 m_0(a,b)
 &=m_0\bigl(m_1(a,e),m_1(e,b)\bigr)\\
 &=m_1\bigl(m_0(a,e),m_0(e,b)\bigr)=m_1(a,b),\\
 m_0(a,b)
 &=m_0\bigl(m_1(e,a),m_1(b,e)\bigr)\\
 &=m_1\bigl(m_0(e,b),m_0(a,e)\bigr)=m_1(b,a).
\end{align*}
The operations coincide and commute. In particular, the genuine
group $\pi_3(Y,y)$ is abelian for every pointed space.

\section*{Application to the \'etale homotopy pro-group}
For a pointed locally noetherian scheme $(X,\bar x)$, the standard
Artin--Mazur--Friedlander \'etale homotopy type is a pointed
pro-object in the homotopy category of simplicial sets. Its
higher \'etale homotopy pro-groups are obtained by applying the
ordinary pointed homotopy-group functor; see~\cite{Moutand}, \S2.1.
Choose a pointed simplicial representative at each level and use
geometric realization. Its third group is the actual group
\[
 \pi_3\bigl(|K_i|,k_i\bigr).
\]
Each of these groups is abelian by the previous argument. Every
transition homomorphism is consequently a homomorphism of abelian
groups. Hence the entire defining diagram factors through
abelian groups before any inverse limit is taken. The \'etale
third homotopy pro-group is therefore a pro-abelian group.

\begin{theorem}
Under the standard \'etale homotopy-group definition, no smooth
variety $X$ has internally nonabelian $\pi_3^{\et}(X,\bar x)$.
Consequently the variety required by Conjecture 00000002440
does not exist.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A smooth variety is locally noetherian. Its \'etale homotopy type
and third homotopy pro-group have the representation just
described, with all groups abelian. This is incompatible with
internal noncommutativity. The additional condition that
$\pi_1^{\et}$ be finite and the proposed Enriques-type construction
cannot change this universal obstruction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Pro-objects and profinite conventions.}
The conclusion is that the pro-group has an abelian-valued
representative. It does not assert that every arbitrary
pro-isomorphic presentation has abelian groups at every level.
Also, an abelian inverse limit alone would not establish the
required statement about a pro-group; the argument above is
levelwise and gives the entire diagram factorization.

If profinite completion is included in the convention, it
preserves commutativity. Indeed every quotient image of an
abelian group is abelian. A compatible-tuple inverse limit of
abelian groups is a subgroup of their product with pointwise
multiplication, which is again commutative. These observations
are additional to, rather than replacements for, the levelwise
pro-group argument.

A possibly nontrivial action of $\pi_1$ on $\pi_3$ is a separate
structure. Such an action cannot make the internal group
operation on $\pi_3$ noncommutative, as explicitly required by
both versions of the conjecture.

\section*{What the Lean project verifies}
The input \texttt{BasedSpace} contains only a carrier, a topology
and a base point. Its \texttt{Pi3} is Mathlib's actual
\texttt{HomotopyGroup.Pi 3}, the relative-boundary cube-loop
homotopy quotient. No group or commutativity hypothesis is part
of the input. Mathlib's proved Eckmann--Hilton construction
supplies its commutative group structure. The submitted file
exposes the actual distinct-coordinate interchange identity and
the two unit laws; it also proves that reversing concatenation
gives actual cube maps homotopic relative to the boundary.

A diagram has actual pointed spaces at its objects. Its arrows
are homomorphisms of their actual third homotopy groups, with
identity and composition laws. It assumes
no commutativity. For every indexing category the project
constructs an explicit natural isomorphism between its
group-valued diagram and the forgetful image of a diagram in
\texttt{CommGrp}. Cofiltered diagrams are included. Thus the
checked result handles every transition diagram that can arise
in the stated application. It does not infer pro-commutativity
from an inverse-limit calculation.

Further theorems prove commutativity of arbitrary surjective
quotient images and normal quotients of actual third homotopy
groups. They construct the subgroup of all transition-compatible
tuples and prove its multiplication commutative, for original
groups and quotient diagrams alike.

\paragraph{Explicit formal boundary.}
Lean does not construct schemes, \'etale hypercovers, the \'etale
type functor, geometric realization, its homotopy-group comparison,
or the homomorphism functor induced by pointed maps. Those standard
constructions are the mathematical bridges on page 2. The
universal theorem for every actual pointed space and every
homomorphism diagram is proved in Lean. No custom object is
merely named an \'etale group and then assumed commutative.
