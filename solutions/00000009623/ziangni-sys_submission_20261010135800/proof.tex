\noindent\textbf{Disproof of conjecture 00000009623.}
The conjecture asserts that a surjective cellular automaton has zero
damage propagation speed if and only if its rule is a permutative
factor. The binary coordinate shift refutes this clause.

Let $X=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}$ have the product topology, with the finite
alphabet discrete, and define $F:X\to X$ by
\[
 (Fx)_i=x_{i+1}.
\]
Each output coordinate is a coordinate projection, so $F$ is continuous.
It commutes with the one-dimensional shift, being that shift itself.
Its inverse is $(F^{-1}x)_i=x_{i-1}$, so it is surjective. Its finite
neighborhood is $\{1\}$ and its local rule is $f(b)=b$. This rule is a
permutation of the binary alphabet and hence is permutative. Even if a
symmetric radius-one neighborhood is used, it is right-permutative:
fixing the other coordinates, the map of the rightmost coordinate to
the output is the identity permutation.

For every configuration $x$, let $y$ differ from $x$ only at site $j$,
where its bit is flipped. Induction gives
\[
 (F^t x)_i=x_{i+t},\qquad
 (F^t y)_i\ne(F^t x)_i\ \Longleftrightarrow\ i=j-t.
\]
Thus the damage travels left by exactly $t$ sites in $t$ iterations.
Its leftward spatial propagation speed is
\[
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{|(j-t)-j|}{t}=1.
\]
This is spatial displacement, not the count of damaged sites, which
remains one.

The same conclusion follows from the directional half-line front
used for cellular-automaton Lyapunov exponents. Suppose $x,y$ agree on
$[j,\infty)\cap\mathbb Z$, Their iterates agree on
$[j-t,\infty)\cap\mathbb Z$. This boundary is sharp: flip $x$ at
$j-1$ to produce $y$; the disagreement is exactly at $j-1-t$.
Hence the left-moving boundary advances exactly $t$ sites. Reversing
orientation swaps the two directional exponents; one is $1$ and the
other $0$, so the propagation speed does not vanish.

These identities hold for \emph{every} configuration, so they also
hold almost surely under the fair Bernoulli product measure (indeed
under any measure on $X$). There is no exceptional set or probabilistic
limit to estimate. This surjective permutative factor has positive
propagation speed, contradicting the claimed equivalence. The other
conjuncts need not be settled.
