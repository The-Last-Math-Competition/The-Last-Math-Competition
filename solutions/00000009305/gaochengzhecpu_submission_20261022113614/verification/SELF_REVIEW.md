# 9305: local limits need not decompose into laws supported on transitive graphs

## Exact source and scope

The exact bilingual source is `round5/source/00000009305.md`. It asserts an ergodic decomposition of local limits into extreme points whose supports are transitive graphs. Transitive means vertex-transitive under actual graph automorphisms. No connectedness assumption on the approximating finite graphs, and no infinitude assumption on root components, occurs in either language.

We disprove the transitive-support restriction. Our local limit cannot even be a mixture of arbitrary measures concentrated on vertex-transitive graphs, so adding the further condition that the components be extreme cannot rescue the assertion. We do not need to assert or formalize extremality of the counterexample, and do not dispute the usual ergodic decomposition without the false transitivity condition.

## Mathematical proof

Let P3 have vertices 0,1,2 and edges 01,12. Let G_m be m+1 disjoint copies of P3, on the actual vertex set Fin(m+1) × Fin(3). Uniformly choose an actual vertex, then take its connected component with the chosen root. Each second coordinate has probability 1/3. Thus every G_m has exactly the same uniform-root law μ, the pushforward of uniform Fin(3) under r ↦ [P3,r]. On rooted isomorphism classes it assigns mass 2/3 to the endpoint-root class and 1/3 to the middle-root class. The vertex counts 3(m+1) tend to infinity. All cylinder probabilities are identically their limiting values, so μ is a genuine local weak limit.

Let A be the union, over r=0,1,2, of the radius-two rooted-ball cylinders of (P3,r). This is a measurable finite cylinder union and μ(A)=1. No vertex-transitive graph belongs to A. Indeed the full neighbor sets of the root and of any neighbor of the root lie in the radius-two ball. In every rooted P3 pattern, the root has a neighbor of a different degree: respectively 1 versus 2, or 2 versus 1. A graph automorphism induces a bijection between neighbor sets, so a vertex-transitive locally finite graph has constant degree.

Any measure ν concentrated on vertex-transitive graphs consequently has ν(A)=0. If μ were a barycentric mixture ∫ν_i dη(i) of such measures, evaluating the defining barycentric identity on the genuine measurable event A would give 1=μ(A)=∫0 dη=0. The Lean theorem proves this for arbitrary mixing measure spaces and arbitrary measure-valued families satisfying almost-everywhere concentration, without needing measurability of the entire family beyond the asserted integral identity. Genuine measurable probability kernels are included.

## Adversarial self-review

1. **Are root labels incorrectly distinguished?** No. The state space is the quotient by actual root-preserving graph isomorphisms. Endpoint roots are isomorphic; the underlying uniform PMF correctly pushes both to the same class.
2. **Are disconnected graphs silently substituted for a connected-graph hypothesis?** The source imposes no such hypothesis. Every state in the limiting rooted space is connected: it is the actual selected component. The approximating graphs are allowed to be disconnected as in standard local weak convergence.
3. **Is a bounded finite graph alone used instead of a size-diverging sequence?** No. The actual graph cardinalities tend to infinity and every actual uniform-root law is identified exactly with μ.
4. **Does a radius-two P3 ball force the whole graph to be P3?** No, and the proof never claims this. The endpoint of P4 is a concrete counterexample to that stronger statement, included in the Python verifier. Only full degrees of the root and its immediate neighbors are read from the ball.
5. **Could unseen edges change those degrees?** No. Any neighbor of a root neighbor has a walk of length two from the root and must be in the induced ball. The required neighbor-set equivalence is explicitly formalized.
6. **Is transitivity confused with a root orbit?** No. `VertexTransitive` requires actual automorphisms of the same underlying graph, with one taking u to v for every vertex pair.
7. **Is the decomposition weakened to a finite convex combination?** No. The theorem handles arbitrary measure-valued families and arbitrary mixing measure spaces, with the actual integral identity on all measurable sets.
8. **Must the counterexample itself be proved extreme?** No. It cannot be a mixture of any transitive-supported measures. This directly excludes the narrower claimed class of extreme transitive-supported component measures.
9. **Is a topological support assertion replaced by an unrelated condition?** Concentration is the usual almost-everywhere support-on-a-class condition. The separating event is an open local cylinder union, so the same contradiction applies to the usual topological support interpretation as well. The formal statement uses almost-everywhere concentration and makes that convention explicit.
10. **What if a reformulated conjecture required infinite components or connected approximants?** Such a restriction would be a different statement and is not claimed to be disproved here. Neither restriction appears in the exact source.
11. **Does the Python check substitute for the proof?** No. It independently enumerates all simple graphs on at most six vertices, tests the true radius-two obstruction, and computes forty actual finite uniform-root laws using exact rational arithmetic. Lean proves the unrestricted graph and mixture arguments.
