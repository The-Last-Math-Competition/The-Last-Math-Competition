\title{Three derived classes from faithful actions of one cyclic group}
\author{The Last Math Competition, conjecture 00000001658}

\maketitle

Fix the same algebra \(A=\mathbb Q^6\) and the same cyclic group
\(C_2=\{1,s\}\) for all three examples. For \(t\in\{1,2,3\}\), let \(s\)
interchange the first \(t\) disjoint coordinate pairs and fix the
remaining \(6-2t\) coordinates. Write this involution as \(\sigma_t\).
Every action is faithful because it interchanges the first pair.
Let \(B_t=A\rtimes_{\sigma_t}C_2\).

Write \(e_i\) for the \(i\)-th coordinate idempotent of \(A\). The skew
group algebra has the basis \(e_i[s^a]\) with \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and
multiplication
\[
 (e_i[s^a])(e_j[s^b])=
 \begin{cases}
 e_i[s^{a+b}], & i=\sigma_t^a(j),\\
 0, & \text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
where the group exponent is taken modulo two. For each coordinate orbit
\(O\), the sum \(\sum_{i\in O}e_i[1]\) is a central idempotent. These
idempotents are pairwise orthogonal and sum to one, so their blocks give
a direct product decomposition.

A fixed coordinate gives a block isomorphic to \(\mathbb Q[C_2]\).
The map \(a+b[s]\mapsto(a+b,a-b)\) identifies this block with
\(\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q\), with inverse
\((x,y)\mapsto\frac{x+y}{2}+\frac{x-y}{2}[s]\).
For a two-element orbit \(O=\{p,q\}\), map \(e_i[s^a]\) to
the matrix unit \(E_{i,\sigma_t^a(i)}\) in the rows and columns labeled
by \(p,q\). Its four basis elements map bijectively to the four matrix
units. The displayed multiplication becomes matrix-unit multiplication,
so this block is \(M_2(\mathbb Q)\). Consequently
\[
 B_t \cong \bigl(\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q\bigr)^{6-2t}
              \times M_2(\mathbb Q)^t .
\]

Each field factor and each matrix-ring factor contributes one simple
module up to isomorphism. Thus the three semisimple algebras have
\(2(6-2t)+t=12-3t\) simple classes, giving \(9,6,3\), respectively.
These counts are derived invariants in this situation. Every bounded
complex over a semisimple algebra splits in the derived category as the
sum of its cohomology modules in their respective degrees. The
indecomposable derived objects are therefore the shifts of the simple
modules. A triangulated equivalence preserves indecomposability and
commutes with suspension up to isomorphism. It must induce a bijection
between the suspension orbits of indecomposable objects, whose counts
here are \(9,6,3\). Equivalently these counts are the ranks of the
derived Grothendieck groups, with the simple classes as bases and
alternating cohomology multiplicities giving the coordinates.

The algebras \(B_1,B_2,B_3\) are pairwise derived-inequivalent. Hence
even for one fixed algebra and one fixed cyclic group with faithful
actions, the asserted upper bound of two fails.

\paragraph{Lean verification.}
The Lean project verifies the involutions and faithfulness, the coordinate
orbit decomposition, the change of basis for the fixed blocks and the
matrix-unit multiplication for the pair blocks. It counts the simple
labels and verifies the obstruction to a bijection between the three
sets of derived suspension-orbit labels. The algebra and derived-category
identifications are justified above.
