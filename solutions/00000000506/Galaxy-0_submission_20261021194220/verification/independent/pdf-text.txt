1. Claim, convention, and counterexample

The conjecture asserts, in both its English and Chinese versions, that the support of the first Betti
row of a monomial ideal is connected and has no isolated points. It adds a proposed explanation
involving the Taylor resolution. We refute the explicit no-isolated-points clause. No interpretation
or verification of the additional Taylor-resolution clause is needed to refute the conjunction.
We use the standard total grading and the Betti numbers of the ideal as a module, not the Hilbert
function of its entire syzygy module. For a minimal graded presentation F0 ↠ I with kernel K,
the first Betti support is
                                    {j ∈ Z : (K/mK)j ̸= 0}.
This is the minimal-generator definition of the graded Betti row; it agrees with the shifts in a
minimal graded free resolution and with graded Tor dimensions. See the definitions in [2, 3]. The
formal proof computes this quotient explicitly rather than assigning a numerical table.

Theorem 1. Let R = F2 [X, Y ], with deg X = deg Y = 1, and let I = (X, Y ) = m. Then I has
minimal graded free resolution
                    d               d
     0 −→ R(−2) −−1→ R(−1)2 −−0→ I −→ 0,                d1 (h) = (Y h, Xh),   d0 (a, b) = Xa + Y b.

Consequently β1,2 (I) = 1 and β1,j (I) = 0 for j ̸= 2. Its first Betti support is the singleton {2},
which has an isolated point.

The field F2 is allowed: the source imposes no characteristic restriction. In characteristic two
the usual Koszul relation (−Y, X) is (Y, X). The ideal is genuinely monomial, generated by the
monomials X and Y .


2. Exactness, with every syzygy accounted for

Represent a polynomial by its finitely supported coefficients pij ∈ F2 for i, j ≥ 0. Multiplication
by X or Y shifts the respective index by one, with zero on the new boundary.
Surjectivity of d0 follows from the definition of (X, Y ). Also d0 d1 (h) = XY h + Y Xh = 0. To
compute the entire kernel, suppose Xa + Y b = 0. Comparing coefficients gives

                        ai,0 = 0,       b0,j = 0,   ai,j+1 = bi+1,j   (i, j ≥ 0).

Define hij = ai,j+1 . This still has finite support. The displayed identities yield a = Y h and
b = Xh. Conversely every such pair is a syzygy. The coefficient identity (Y h)i,j+1 = hij proves
uniqueness of h and injectivity of d1 . Thus the sequence is exact, including at its left end.

3. Minimality and the actual graded quotient

3.1 The original presentation is minimal

A polynomial lies in I if and only if its constant coefficient is zero. Indeed, split the terms with
positive X exponent from those with X exponent zero and positive Y exponent. Thus p ∈ I
precisely when p = Xa + Y b.
Similarly,
                         p ∈ mI = XI + Y I        ⇐⇒       p00 = p10 = p01 = 0.
For the reverse implication the same term split gives coefficients a, b whose constant coefficients
are p10 , p01 , hence a, b ∈ I. It follows that
                                         ∼
                                I/mI −−→ F22 ,          [p] 7−→ (p10 , p01 ).

The classes of X, Y are therefore a basis, both in degree one. This verifies minimality of the
generators used to define K, a necessary point when computing first Betti numbers from a
presentation.


3.2 Minimality of the differential

The map d1 is R-linear and additive; d0 is also R-linear and additive. The entries Y, X of d1
lie in m. Thus the image of the free differential lies in mR(−1)2 , the standard minimal-complex
condition. There is no further differential because d1 is injective.
The grading is not implicit: if h is homogeneous of ordinary degree r, then (Y h, Xh) has degree
r + 2 in R(−1)2 . This is exactly the shift R(−2). The augmentation preserves degree. The ring
multiplication is also proved to respect the standard total grading.


3.3 The quotient K/mK

The established R-linear bijection d1 : R(−2) −→ K takes m onto mK. In detail, finite sums of
arbitrary products of an element of m with an element of K equal XK + Y K, since m = (X, Y ).
Under d1 , membership in this submodule is equivalent to h ∈ m, that is, h00 = 0.
Therefore the map
                                 K/mK −→ F2 ,            [d1 (h)] 7−→ h00
is a well-defined additive, F2 -linear bijection. Its inverse sends c ∈ F2 to [d1 (c)]. In particular, the
nonzero class of d1 (1) is a one-element basis. This is a quotient of the actual polynomial syzygy
module, not a separately invented two-element model.
Now a homogeneous syzygy of degree j can have nonzero quotient class only when h00 = 1. That
constant coefficient contributes degree two, forcing j = 2. Conversely d1 (1) is homogeneous of
degree two and has nonzero class. This proves, for every integer j, including negative degrees,

                                    (K/mK)j ̸= 0        ⇐⇒       j = 2.

The one-element basis also gives the stated numerical value β1,2 (I) = 1.

4. Why the singleton disproves the source

A singleton is connected. The argument does not claim otherwise. It contradicts the separate
requirement that the support have no isolated points.

Proposition 1. In every ambient topology, the sole point of a singleton subset is isolated in that
subset. With any irreflexive adjacency relation, a one-vertex support also has no neighbor.

Proof. The whole ambient space is open, and its intersection with the singleton is the singleton
itself. Hence it is an isolating neighborhood. For adjacency, any putative neighbor in the support
must be the same point, contradicting irreflexivity.

Both statements are formalized. The topological theorem quantifies over an arbitrary open-set
predicate, requiring only that the whole space is open; therefore it applies to any genuine topology.
The graph theorem applies in particular to adjacency of consecutive integer degrees. This avoids
relying on an unstated topological-versus-combinatorial convention in the source.


Alternative indexing: the quotient ring

Some authors tabulate the Betti numbers of R/I instead of I. In that convention the first relation
module is I, and the first minimal-generator quotient is I/mI. Section 3.1 shows that it has two
independent generators, both in degree one. Thus the alternative first support is {1}, again a
singleton with an isolated point. The Lean source independently proves that the homogeneous
nonzero-residue support of I/mI is exactly {1}.

to refute the universal claim.


5. Formal model and scope

The project uses only Lean 4 core and Std, with no Mathlib dependency.
• A polynomial is a coefficient function N2 → {0, 1} with an existential finite-support bound.
  Equality ignores the chosen bound.
• Addition is coefficientwise exclusive-or. Multiplication is obtained from finite lists of monomials
  by adding exponents and collecting parity. Every finitely supported coefficient family has a list
  representation; representation independence and all commutative-ring laws are proved.
• The coefficient operations are proved to satisfy the two-element field laws. The free rank-two
  module has the actual polynomial scalar action.
• The ideal, kernels, finite-sum ideal products, grading, and quotient equivalence relation are
  explicit. The final support is not postulated.

6. Proof map and reproducibility

The final theorem is TLMC506.conjecture506_counterexample in Resolution.lean. It bundles
the monomial-ideal certificate, the verified minimal resolution, the integer-indexed support identity,
and failure of the no-isolated clause.
Key supporting results are:
• polynomial_ring_laws, free_module_laws, and homogeneous_mul: actual ring, module, and
  grading structures.
• kernel_free_rank_one: unique polynomial parametrization of every syzygy; presentation_
  minimal_basis: the basis of I/mI.
• maximal_times_syzygy_is_ideal_product: identification with the genuine finite-sum ideal-
  module product mK.
• quotient_basis: unique coefficient for every class in K/mK; first_betti_supportZ_iff:
  exact support over all integer degrees.
• first_betti_supportZ_graph_isolated: the graph reading; quotient_first_betti_
  supportZ_iff: the alternative indexing.
