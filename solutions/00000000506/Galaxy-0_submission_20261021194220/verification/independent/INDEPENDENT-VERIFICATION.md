## Source-first semantic assessment

Both source languages explicitly require a connected support with no isolated points. The standard meaning of β₁,j is the number of minimal homogeneous syzygy generators in degree j, equivalently the dimension of the degree-j part of K/mK for a minimal presentation. The wording about a graded distribution is loose; the report correctly distinguishes this invariant from the Hilbert function of the entire syzygy module K. The latter is nonzero in all degrees j≥2 for this example, so the two notions must not be conflated.

The source does not restrict characteristic. F₂ is therefore an admissible coefficient field. The ideal-module convention gives support {2}; the package also verifies the homogeneous minimal-generator support {1} under the quotient-module convention R/I.

The singleton is connected, but has an isolated point in every topology. It also has an isolated vertex for every irreflexive adjacency relation on its support. Both isolation statements are proved. Consequently the counterexample does not depend on choosing a particular topology or consecutive-degree graph. No precise statement about the source's undefined Taylor-resolution merging mechanism is asserted or needed: one explicit conjunct is already false.

The conventional interpretation is corroborated by [Madden's Betti-number lecture notes](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~madden/M7290Fall2006/lecture7.pdf) and Eisenbud, *The Geometry of Syzygies*, §1B, especially the minimal-complex criterion, Corollary 1.5, and Proposition 1.7 ([course-hosted book](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~reiner/REU/REU2019notes/2005_Book_TheGeometryOfSyzygies.pdf)). These references clarify terminology; they do not replace the concrete formal proofs.

## Mathematical and formal coverage inspected

All eleven mathematical modules were read against the report and original claim.

1. `Poly` is genuinely the finitely supported coefficient model of F₂[X,Y]. Finite monomial lists with parity provide multiplication by exponent addition. Surjectivity of this representation, independence of chosen representatives, complete commutative-ring laws, nontriviality, and the two-element coefficient-field laws are proved.
2. Multiplication by X and Y is proved equal to the coefficient shifts used in the syzygy calculation. The componentwise scalar action and free-module laws are verified, as is multiplicativity of the standard grading.
3. The witness is proved to be an ideal generated by the actual monomials X and Y. The augmentation has precisely that image. The entire kernel is uniquely parametrized by h↦(Yh,Xh), and this map is injective, additive, and polynomial-linear. This is exactness, not merely existence of a relation.
4. The shifted homogeneous degrees are checked, giving R(-2)→R(-1)². Both maps preserve degree. The differential has entries in m, and the original presentation is minimal: coefficient residues give the unique two-generator expansion in I/mI.
5. The formulas XI+YI and XK+YK are proved equal to the genuine finite-sum ideal products mI and mK, respectively. Thus the quotient is not an artificial selected family of shifts.
6. K/mK is implemented as an actual quotient by the difference-in-mK equivalence relation. The constant-coefficient residue map is a bijection with F₂; its addition and scalar action agree with operations on representatives. The nonzero relation class has a one-vector basis.
7. A homogeneous syzygy has nonzero residue exactly in integer degree 2, including exclusion of negative degrees. `FirstBettiSupportZ` is computed from that actual kernel and product submodule. The final theorem packages the monomial-ideal witness, verified minimal resolution, exact support, and failure of the no-isolated-points clause.

No general-purpose Tor, arbitrary minimal-resolution uniqueness, or Taylor-resolution library is formalized. Those are not necessary for this concrete first minimal-generator quotient. The formalization uses explicit algebraic certificates rather than Mathlib typeclasses; the report accurately describes this scope.
