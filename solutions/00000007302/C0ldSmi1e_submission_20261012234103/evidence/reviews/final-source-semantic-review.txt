1. GENUINE TENSOR AND EUCLIDEAN FACTORS
TensorNuclear.lean:11-12 defines
  Factor := EuclideanSpace R (Fin 2)
  Tensor := PiTensorProduct R (fun _ : Fin 3 => Factor).
DecompositionBridge uses definitionally identical abbreviations. There are
three actual factors, each two-dimensional over the reals; Tensor is not a
scalar alias or arbitrary eight-coordinate type renamed tensor.

Pinned Mathlib's PiL2.lean:104 defines EuclideanSpace as PiLp 2, and its
norm_eq at lines 141-143 gives sqrt(sum of squared coordinate norms). Thus
the decomposition costs use Euclidean factor norms, not the default sup
norm on a function type. Pinned PiTensorProduct.lean:81-103 constructs the
actual tensor product as a quotient by the multilinearity relations;
tprod at line 278 is its canonical multilinear map. The lift equivalence
at lines 433-446 is the tensor product universal property.

2. ALL FINITE PURE DECOMPOSITIONS AND THEIR ACTUAL INFIMUM
DecompositionBridge.lean:28-33 defines the sum and cost of arbitrary finite
lists of pure tensors. The cost is precisely the sum of products of the
three Euclidean factor norms. ordinaryCosts at lines 66-67 requires the
ordinary tensor sum to equal the tensor in question. No restriction on the
number of summands or artificial preferred basis is imposed.

The library's weighted list uses entries (c,m), with tensor c * tprod(m)
and cost |c| times the product of factor norms. I inspected the library's
lifts, mem_lifts_iff, and nonempty_lifts (PiTensorProduct.lean:312-329), and
projectiveSeminormAux and projectiveSeminorm_apply
(ProjectiveSeminorm.lean:51-52,108-109). They have exactly that meaning.

The author's absorb operation puts c into factor 0. tprod_absorb and
cost_absorb prove preservation of both tensor and cost, including negative
or zero coefficients. Conversely ordinaryToWeighted assigns coefficient 1.
The two conversions preserve feasibility and cost. Therefore costRange_eq
at lines 90-100 really proves equality of the full feasible cost sets, and
projectiveSeminorm_eq_sInf_ordinaryCosts at lines 103-107 equates the library
seminorm with the ordinary finite-decomposition infimum. It is not a
one-sided estimate or merely a chosen decomposition's cost.

ordinaryDecomposition_exists proves existence for every tensor, using an
actual quotient representative and the conversion. ordinaryCost_nonneg,
ordinaryCosts_nonempty, and ordinaryCosts_bddBelow give nonnegativity,
nonemptiness, and the zero lower bound. Consequently the real infimum is
finite and is not relying on Lean's convention for an empty or unbounded
infimum. Allowing zero pure tensors among summands is harmless: these have
zero cost and can be omitted. The construction is the ordinary projective
(tensor nuclear) decomposition norm.

3. SEMINORM, DEFINITENESS, AND INSTALLED NORM
I read the entire pinned ProjectiveSeminorm.lean. Its seminorm construction
at lines 88-105 proves zero, subadditivity, and scalar control from the
nonempty bounded-below weighted cost sets; Seminorm.ofSMulLE supplies exact
absolute scalar homogeneity. This is verified library mathematics, not an
axiom assumed by the author.

TensorNuclear.lean:14-34 proves the finite coordinate expansion of every
scalar-valued trilinear map and derives continuity. The eight terms are
indexed by Fin 3 -> Fin 2. Their coefficient is the product of the three
factor coordinates. This is the full expansion, not a diagonal-only one.

For projective_definite, every algebraic linear functional phi on Tensor
pulls back along tprod to such a continuous scalar-valued trilinear map.
The library bound norm_eval_le_projectiveSeminorm gives
  |phi(x)| <= projectiveSeminorm(x) * ||f||.
The actual library proof was inspected at lines 118-139. If the seminorm
vanishes, all algebraic functionals vanish on x. The author then uses the
verified algebraic dual separation theorem
Module.forall_dual_apply_eq_zero_iff, whose definition and prerequisites I
inspected in Dual/Lemmas.lean:159-195. Over R its needed module hypotheses
are provided by the genuine tensor module; no extra hypothesis remains in
the author's theorem.

There is no circularity: continuity here is on the product of the three
already normed Euclidean factors, with scalar target R. Neither the
continuity proof nor the library functional bound needs a preexisting norm
or topology on Tensor. Definiteness is established before the new tensor
normed-space instances are installed.

nuclearAddGroupNorm adds this proved definiteness to the library seminorm.
tensorNormedAddCommGroup constructs the metric/normed group from that very
norm. tensorNormedSpace derives its scalar inequality from the seminorm's
homogeneity law. norm_eq_projective is definitional equality. I checked
AddGroupNorm's fields and the to_additive-generated normed group

4. NONZERO TENSOR, NOT AN ASSUMED NONTRIVIALITY INSTANCE
TensorNuclear.lean:66-78 uses e0 tensor e0 tensor e0 as witness. The product
of the three zeroth-coordinate functionals is a continuous trilinear map;
its tensor lift takes this witness to 1 and zero to 0. The proof of
witness_ne_zero therefore supplies the real Nontrivial Tensor instance.
It does not assume injectivity of pure-tensor formation or postulate a
nonzero tensor. Computing the witness's nuclear norm is unnecessary.

5. OFFICIAL EXTREME POINTS AND THE GEOMETRIC PROOF
GenericExtreme.lean imports Mathlib.Analysis.Convex.Extreme and uses
Set.extremePoints with scalar field R. The inspected library definition at
Extreme.lean:60-61 requires membership in the original set and the standard
open-segment extremity condition. It is not a custom surrogate predicate.

The proof takes a point of norm less than 1, selects y with positive norm
(1 - ||x||)/2, and puts x+y and x-y in the closed unit ball by the triangle
inequality. Their midpoint is x. Extremity would force x+y=x and hence
y=0, contradicting its positive norm. The result places every extreme point
on the unit sphere. Closedness of that sphere then places their entire
closure there. All required hypotheses of the generic lemma are actually
instantiated by the tensor construction and witness above.

6. EXACT NORM, BALL, TOPOLOGY, AND RELATIVE NONDENSITY
Conjecture7302.lean:20 defines nuclearNorm by the explicit ordinary cost
infimum, and norm_eq_nuclearNorm at lines 24-26 proves that this is the
installed norm. nuclearBall is its <= 1 ball; nuclearBall_eq_closedBall
identifies it with the actual metric closed unit ball.

The closure and sphere appearing in the final declarations use the topology
of tensorNormedAddCommGroup, constructed from this nuclear norm. There is
no second ambient norm whose equivalence would need an unproved bridge.
The theorem zero_not_mem_closure_extremePoints combines the sphere
inclusion with the fact that zero is not on the unit sphere. The final
declarations prove both
  0 in nuclearBall and 0 not in closure(nuclearBall.extremePoints R),
and
  not (nuclearBall subset closure(nuclearBall.extremePoints R)).
Because extreme points belong to the ball, this is precisely the failure
of their density in the ball with its relative norm topology. It is not
merely failure of density in the entire ambient tensor space.

7. SOURCE MATCH AND REMAINING REPORT OBLIGATIONS
The English first conjunct asserts density of extreme points themselves;
neither language explicitly replaces them by their convex combinations or
the ball by its boundary. Under the ordinary meaning of density in the
ball, the counterexample falsifies a necessary claim. A true order-three
2 by 2 by 2 example meets the ordinary tensor scope and avoids a degenerate
scalar-only interpretation. The argument actually applies to every genuine
nonzero real norm ball, so choice of a particular nuclear convention does
not manufacture the obstruction.

The remaining ambiguity is in the source, not in the Lean conclusion. The

It should not advertise a unique formal translation of all supplied words.
Nor should it claim to disprove density of convex combinations or boundary
