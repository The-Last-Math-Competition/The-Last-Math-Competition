EXACT TARGET AND LOGIC
The English original says: "The density of extreme points of the nuclear-norm
ball holds, and the combination of the density is the parameter space of
rank-one decompositions." Its first clause separately asserts density of
extreme points. The second is joined by "and". The Chinese is grammatically
compressed, but also explicitly associates extreme points and density. It
does not expressly replace extreme points by their convex combinations or
replace the ball by its boundary.

The ordinary completion of "dense" here is dense in the ball, with the
relative topology inherited from the tensor norm. It is not density in the
entire tensor space. For B the closed unit ball the exact useful negation is
  not (B subset closure_T(extremePoints_R(B))).
Producing 0 in B outside that ambient closure establishes this relative
nondensity. Merely proving that the extreme points are not dense in T would
not establish the required statement.

Under this reading, falsifying the first conjunct suffices to falsify the
conjunction. It is unnecessary and inappropriate to fabricate a unique
rank-one decomposition parameter space in order to do that. No statement
about density of convex combinations follows from the proposed argument.
The report should explicitly distinguish that different assertion, which
the vague word "combination" might have been intended to evoke.

The source does not explicitly quantify over all dimensions or orders. A
generic assertion about tensor nuclear-norm balls ordinarily includes
nonzero finite-dimensional tensor spaces. A genuine order-three 2 by 2 by 2
example therefore supplies a counterexample under ordinary conventions and
does not rely on a degenerate scalar-only specialization. The theorem also
shows the density obstruction is inherent to any genuine norm ball in a
nonzero real normed space, not to a specially chosen nuclear norm.

CONVENTIONS VERSUS REAL AMBIGUITY
Reasonable conventions to state openly:
  * real Euclidean factors R^2, tensor order three, and positive-radius ball;
  * the centered closed unit ball, real convex extreme points;
  * the usual projective/nuclear norm and its norm topology;
  * density relative to that ball.
Unit radius and zero center do not create the obstruction: positive radii
and translations have the same issue. In the finite-dimensional real model,
choosing another norm to generate the topology does not change the topology.

Genuine omissions remain. "Convex envelope of low-rank tensors" is not a
complete norm definition; no scalar function/domain or normalized atomic
set is given. The target of density is unstated, and the parameter-space
clause lacks mathematical definitions. Thus the final report should quote
both versions, identify these conventions, and say precisely what it
disproves. It should not announce that every possible interpretation or a
specified parameter-space identification has been settled. Neither boundary
density nor convex-hull density is an authorized replacement target in this
submission; if a maintainer later confirms either, scope must be reconsidered.

REQUIRED ACTUAL TENSOR/NORM BRIDGE
For this submission the generic normed-space theorem is a useful lemma, not
the final source-matching theorem. The formal dependency chain must reach
an actual real order-three 2 by 2 by 2 tensor model. Acceptable models include
an iterated tensor product of Euclidean R^2, or the eight-coordinate array
space with a proved correspondence to that tensor product and rank-one
tensors given by outer products.

The final nuclear/projective norm must be tied to finite rank-one
decompositions with cost the sum of products of the three Euclidean factor
norms, or to the gauge of normalized rank-one atoms with a proved bridge to
that construction. It is not enough to rename an arbitrary ambient norm
"nuclear", assume an unspecified nuclear norm exists, or specialize only to
R tensor_R R. If an existing verified library construction is used, identify
its precise definition and show that the final tensor type carries it. If a
new construction is used, formalize the needed existence, finiteness,
positive definiteness and norm laws, rather than leave these in prose.

In particular the final chain needs a proved nonzero tensor, genuine normed
space structure for this norm, the official real extreme-point predicate,
and the closure in its norm topology. If a standard ambient topology is
used instead, prove or invoke its agreement with the constructed norm
topology. An atomic-gauge route must ensure the chosen set and gauge give
the advertised nuclear ball; arbitrary rank-one atoms without normalization
do not do so. Any identification of the ball with a closed convex hull in
the report should be justified, not smuggled in as the source's definition.

MATHEMATICAL CHECK OF THE PLAN
The proposed elementary argument is sound. In a nonzero real normed space,
an interior point of the closed unit ball can be perturbed in opposite
nonzero directions and written as the midpoint of distinct ball points.
It cannot be extreme. Hence all extreme points lie on the unit sphere.
That sphere is closed, so their closure misses the origin, which belongs
to the ball. No description of which rank-one tensors are extreme and no
decomposition uniqueness theorem is needed.
