## Verdict: **DISPROVED (FALSE)**

## Mathematical Summary

Conjecture `00000008559` asserts the conjunction $P_1 \wedge P_2$ of two order-theoretic claims about complete lattices and the infinite distributive law
$$\bigwedge_{i \in I} \bigvee_{j \in J(i)} x_{i,j} \;=\; \bigvee_{f \in \prod_{i \in I} J(i)} \bigwedge_{i \in I} x_{i, f(i)} \quad\text{(and dually)}.$$

1. **Unconditional Refutation of Clause 2 ($P_2$):**
   - **Sharpness of minimum cardinality $5$:** On finite complete lattices, infinite complete distributivity is equivalent to ordinary distributivity $x \wedge (y \vee z) = (x \wedge y) \vee (x \wedge z)$. Any lattice failing distributivity contains five pairwise distinct elements $\bigl(y \wedge z,\, y,\, z,\, y \vee z,\, x\bigr)$, so **every complete lattice with $|L| \le 4$ is completely distributive** ($C_1, C_2, C_3, C_4$, and $B_2 = C_2 \times C_2$).
   - **Non-uniqueness at $|L| = 5$:** At the minimal cardinality $|L| = 5$, there is **no unique** smallest complete lattice failing complete distributivity. There are **two non-isomorphic** $5$-element complete lattices failing complete distributivity:
     1. **$M_3 = \{\bot, a, b, c, \top\}$**, the bounded / Dedekind–MacNeille completion $\hat{0} \oplus A_3 \oplus \hat{1}$ of the $3$-element antichain $A_3$ (which is self-dual: $M_3^{\mathrm{op}} \cong M_3$), and
     2. **$N_5 = \{\bot, u, v, w, \top\}$**, the pentagon lattice ($\bot \lt u \lt v \lt \top$, $\bot \lt w \lt \top$).
     Because $M_3$ is **modular** (3 atoms, maximal chain size 3) whereas $N_5$ is **non-modular** (2 atoms, maximal chain size 4), **$M_3 \not\cong N_5$** and **$M_3^{\mathrm{op}} \not\cong N_5$**, and neither order-embeds into the other.
   - **Alternative down-set completion reading:** Under the down-set (order-ideal) completion of $A_3$, $\mathcal{O}(A_3)^{\mathrm{op}} \cong (C_2^3)^{\mathrm{op}} = B_3^{\mathrm{op}}$ has cardinality $8 \gt 5$ and **is completely distributive**.
   - **Universal impossibility:** For *every* candidate complete lattice $L_*$, $L_*$ is neither the unique minimum-cardinality complete lattice failing complete distributivity (up to order isomorphism) nor order-embeddable into every complete lattice failing complete distributivity.

2. **Complete Analysis of Clause 1 ($P_1$):**
   - **Positive theorem (Raney 1953):** Every complete sublattice of a direct product of complete chains $\prod_{\alpha \in A} C_\alpha$ is completely distributive (and by Raney's subdirect representation theorem, this characterizes complete distributivity).
   - **Literal generation failure:** Under the literal wording *"generated by complete chain products"*, $M_3$ is both (a) internally complete-lattice generated as the union of three complete sublattices $\{\bot, a, \top\}, \{\bot, b, \top\}, \{\bot, c, \top\} \cong C_3^{\mathbf{1}}$ and (b) a surjective complete-join homomorphic image $\pi : C_2^3 \twoheadrightarrow M_3$ of the complete chain product $C_2 \times C_2 \times C_2$, yet $M_3$ fails complete distributivity.
