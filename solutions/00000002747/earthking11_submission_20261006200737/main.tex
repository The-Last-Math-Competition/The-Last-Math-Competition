\section*{Statement and scope}
The conjecture states that the capacity of $M_m(M_n)$ is
\[
  (m-1)n^2+n,
\]
where capacity is defined as the maximal dimension of a matrix space on which
the determinant vanishes. The text does not specify a base field. We use the
standard interpretation over $\mathbb Q$ and refute the explicitly stated
capacity formula at $m=n=1$. Because the conjecture asserts the formula along
with a classification clause, falsifying the formula suffices to disprove the
conjunction; no claim about the classification clause is made here.

\section*{The case $m=n=1$}
The space $M_1(M_1(\mathbb Q))$ is naturally the one-dimensional vector space
of $1\times1$ rational matrices. A matrix has the form $A=[a]$, and its
determinant is $\det A=a$. Let $W$ be any linear subspace on which the
determinant vanishes identically. For every $A=[a]\in W$, the condition
$\det A=0$ implies $a=0$, hence $A=0$. Thus $W=\{0\}$ and
$\dim_{\mathbb Q}W=0$. The zero subspace itself is admissible, so the maximum
dimension is exactly $0$.

The conjectured expression at these parameters is
\[
  (1-1)\cdot 1^2+1=1,
\]
not $0$. Therefore the capacity formula is false, and hence so is the stated
conjunction.

\section*{Formal verification}
The accompanying Lean 4 project imports Mathlib's matrix determinant and
finite-dimensional linear algebra. It defines the standard space of
$1\times1$ rational matrices, determinant-zero linear subspaces, and an
attained maximum dimension. The Lean development proves that every admissible
subspace is the zero subspace, that its finrank is $0$, and that the zero
subspace attains this bound. It then proves that the maximum cannot equal the
formula's value $1$. The formal theorem and this argument use the same
interpretation and the same counterexample.
