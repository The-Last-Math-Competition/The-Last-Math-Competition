\section{The literal conjunction}
The English source asserts:
\begin{quote}
The minimal rank of an Euler system for the higher K-group is
$2k-1$, and when $k$ is coprime to $p-1$ the rank is exactly $1$,
the formula being complete.
\end{quote}
The Chinese source gives the same two numerical clauses. We refute
their literal conjunction for the \emph{same rank}. A reading in
which the conditional clause overrides the first formula, defining
a corrected piecewise function, is a different assertion and is
not refuted here. The complete original source accompanies this
submission.

\section{A legal witness and the contradiction}
Choose $k=3$, $p=5$. Then $k>0$, $p$ is an odd prime,
$k\le p-2$, and $\gcd(k,p-1)=\gcd(3,4)=1$.
If $r$ denotes the one rank referred to by the two clauses, they
require simultaneously
\[
 r=2\cdot3-1=5,\qquad r=1,
\]
which is impossible. Even imposing the additional restriction
$1\le k\le p-2$ on both formulas, or fixing the single odd prime
$p=5$, leaves this contradiction. That restriction only weakens
the required universal clauses; it is not an added hypothesis
needed from the source.

The proof does not assign a value to an Euler-system rank.
Rather, it proves that \emph{every} candidate rank function fails
at least one of the two necessary clauses. Consequently no actual
mathematical construction could satisfy their conjunction,
regardless of its true rank. No Euler system or higher K-group is
replaced with an artificial object, and no theorem computing their
actual ranks is claimed.

\section{Formal statement and verification}
Lean proves the pointwise obstruction for every value in every
characteristic-zero semiring, then quantifies over all functions
$\rho:\mathbb N\times\mathbb N\to R$. It proves both the restricted
and unrestricted universal-formula negations and the fixed-$p=5$
negation. Explicit specializations cover natural, integer and
cardinal-valued candidate ranks, including the ordinary cardinal
notion of rank. The named predicates are only the two numerical
necessary conditions; they are not definitions of Euler systems.
