\begin{abstract}
The strictly positive analytic symbol $f(\theta)=5/4+\cos\theta$ on
the unit circle has Toeplitz determinants
$D_n=\frac43(1-4^{-(n+1)})$. Its logarithmic leading coefficient is
zero, but its secondary term tends to $\log(4/3)>0$. The circle has no
corners, so the claimed disappearance of the smooth secondary term and
the absence of a global correction are false. Lean verifies the actual
Toeplitz matrices, their determinants for every size, and both limits.
\end{abstract}

\section*{The assertion being tested}
The bilingual source states that the secondary term of logarithmic
Toeplitz determinant asymptotics depends entirely on boundary corners,
vanishing in the smooth case. We test this necessary smooth-case
subclause in the classical circle setting of Szeg\H{o}'s theorem.
The unit circle is a smooth boundary, with no corner angles. A universal
claim for smooth domains must in particular hold in this case.

For a positive circle symbol $f$, write
\[
 \widehat f(k)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
      f(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta,\quad
 T_n(f)=(\widehat f(i-j))_{0\leq i,j<n},\quad D_n(f)=\det T_n(f).
\]
These are the finite sections of the classical Toeplitz operator,
compression of multiplication by $f$ to the span of
$1,e^{i\theta},\ldots,e^{i(n-1)\theta}$ in the circle Hardy space.
The logarithmic leading coefficient is
\[
 L(f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log f(\theta)\,d\theta.
\]
The tested assertion requires
$\log D_n(f)-nL(f)\to0$ for smooth positive symbols on this smooth
boundary. Our example has $L(f)=0$, so changing the size convention from
$n$ to $n+1$ cannot change the secondary constant.

\section*{An analytic positive symbol and its matrix}
Choose
\[
 f(\theta)=\frac54+\cos\theta
   =\left|1+\tfrac12e^{i\theta}\right|^2.
\]
It is real analytic and $2\pi$-periodic, with $f\geq1/4>0$.
The elementary exponential formula for cosine gives
\[
 \widehat f(0)=\tfrac54,\quad
 \widehat f(1)=\widehat f(-1)=\tfrac12,\quad
 \widehat f(k)=0\quad(|k|>1).
\]
Consequently $T_n(f)$ is the actual tridiagonal Toeplitz matrix with
diagonal $5/4$ and both adjacent diagonals $1/2$.

The power series for $\log(1+z)$, evaluated at
$z=e^{i\theta}/2$, gives
\[
 \log f(\theta)=
  2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k\geq1}
      \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k\,2^k}e^{ik\theta}.
\]
This series converges absolutely and uniformly, since
$\sum_{k\geq1}1/(k2^k)<\infty$. Termwise integration is therefore valid.
Every nonconstant exponential has zero circle mean, so $L(f)=0$.
This is also verified independently as the leading coefficient by the
exact determinant asymptotics below.

\section*{Exact determinants and the nonzero correction}
\begin{theorem}
For every integer $n\geq0$, with the empty determinant $D_0=1$,
\[
 D_n(f)=\frac43\left(1-\left(\frac14\right)^{n+1}\right)>0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a tridiagonal Toeplitz matrix with diagonal $d$ and adjacent
diagonals $a$, expansion in the first row, followed by expansion of
the off-diagonal minor in its first column, gives
\[
 D_{n+2}=dD_{n+1}-a^2D_n.
\]
For the present matrix this becomes
$D_{n+2}=\frac54D_{n+1}-\frac14D_n$, with $D_0=1$ and $D_1=5/4$.
The proposed expression has these initial values and satisfies the
recurrence, since its two exponential bases are $1$ and $1/4$.
Induction proves the identity. Positivity follows from
$0<(1/4)^{n+1}<1$.
\end{proof}

It follows immediately that
\[
 D_n(f)\longrightarrow\frac43,\qquad
 \log D_n(f)\longrightarrow\log\frac43>0,
 \qquad \frac{\log D_n(f)}n\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the actual leading coefficient is zero, consistently with the
circle integral, and
\[
 \log D_n(f)-nL(f)\longrightarrow\log\frac43\ne0.
\]
Equivalently, the normalized determinant has a nonunit multiplicative
factor $4/3$. There are no corners that could supply this factor.
This refutes the smooth-vanishing assertion and hence the stated
corner-only secondary law. No general form of the strong limit theorem
is needed for the counterexample.

\section*{Formal verification and scope}
Lean defines the coefficients of the general Laurent symbol
$f_a(\theta)=1+a^2+2a\cos\theta$ by
$c_0=1+a^2$, $c_{\pm1}=a$, with all other coefficients zero.
It constructs the actual Mathlib matrix
$(c_{i-j})_{0\leq i,j<n}$, proves its equality to the tridiagonal
matrix, and proves the determinant recurrence by Mathlib's Laplace
expansion. It then proves the exact determinant formula for every $n$,
positivity, the logarithmic limit, and the normalized leading limit.
The symbol's smoothness and positivity at $a=1/2$ are also formalized.

The final proposition quantifies over the entire family $f_a$, with
smoothness and positivity hypotheses. It asserts that every logarithmic
leading limit $L$ has a vanishing centered secondary term. This is a
necessary universal subclause of the source's smooth-vanishing assertion.
The theorem
\begin{center}\texttt{Conjecture9774.conjecture9774\_false}\end{center}
negates that proposition using $a=1/2$ and $L=0$. The contradiction
uses uniqueness of limits and $\log(4/3)>0$. The scalar formula in Lean
is proved equal to actual matrix determinants; it does not substitute
an unrelated scalar sequence for Toeplitz determinants.

The identification of the Laurent coefficients with Fourier integrals,
the Hardy-space compression description, the smooth geometry of the
unit circle, and the mean-log integral computation are supplied on
paper. Lean does not implement general Toeplitz operators or a general
Szeg\H{o} theorem. Its normalized determinant limit independently proves
the leading coefficient needed for the formal contradiction. Scalar
extension of the real matrices to complex matrices preserves their
determinants, so using real matrices does not change this example.
