\noindent\textbf{Claim addressed.} The conjecture asserts an exact affine law between $\log(\mathrm{pass@}k)$ and $\log k$ within fixed theorem-difficulty strata. A fixed theorem and a fixed independently sampled generator already contradict every possible slope and intercept.

\begin{proposition}
There is a sound proof-generation experiment for one fixed theorem such that its pass-at-$1$, pass-at-$2$, and pass-at-$4$ probabilities do not lie on any affine line in logarithmic coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix the theorem \texttt{True}. On each attempt toss a fair binary coin. On one outcome output its correct proof \texttt{True.intro}; on the other return failure. Acceptance is deterministic: a returned proof is accepted, and failure is rejected. The theorem, generator, and acceptance rule do not change with the number of attempts. Four independent coin flips give the sample space $\Omega=\{0,1\}^4$, with mass $1/16$ at each outcome.

For $k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, let $p_k$ be the probability that at least one of the first $k$ attempts succeeds. The failed prefixes have respectively $2^{4-k}$ extensions, so
\[
 p_k=1-2^{-k},\qquad
 p_1=\frac12,\quad p_2=\frac34,\quad p_4=\frac{15}{16}.
\]
These are probabilities of actual success events, not prescribed numerical success scores.

Suppose $\log p_k=a+b\log k$ at $k=1,2,4$. Because $\log1=0$ and $\log4=2\log2$, this would imply
\[
 \log p_4+\log p_1=2\log p_2,
 \qquad p_4p_1=p_2^2.
\]
All probabilities are positive, so logarithmic injectivity applies. However,
\[
 p_4p_1=\frac{15}{32}\ne\frac{18}{32}=p_2^2.
\]
Thus no such $a,b$ exist.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} The theorem being attempted is held fixed, so changing its difficulty quantile or combining different tasks cannot account for the failure. A stratum containing this fixed task retains the same contradiction. No particular numerical definition of difficulty is required: every real slope is excluded. Failure is a legitimate unsuccessful attempt, and the successful output contains an actual proof. The statement addressed is the universal exact law; approximate empirical fits for a specified prover are not ruled out.

\section*{Lean formalization}
The sample space is an actual fourfold Cartesian product of \texttt{Fin 2}. The measure is $(1/16)$ times Mathlib's counting measure. Lean proves that its total mass is one and that each prescribed joint coordinate pattern has probability $(1/2)^4$, providing the independent fair-bit model.

The function \texttt{generate} returns either \texttt{none} or \texttt{some} containing a lifted proof of \texttt{True}. A successful event means that at least one of the first $k$ returned options contains a proof. The finite event sets at $k=1,2,4$ have exactly $8,12,15$ outcomes. The definition \texttt{passRate} takes the real value of the measure of those sets; Lean computes the three probabilities from those cardinalities.

The theorem \texttt{no\_affine\_log\_scaling} rules out all real intercepts and slopes at the three sample counts. The final theorem also rules out an affine law throughout $1\leq k\leq4$. All computations are exact and kernel-checked; no simulation, assumed pass-rate formula, or statistical estimate is used.
