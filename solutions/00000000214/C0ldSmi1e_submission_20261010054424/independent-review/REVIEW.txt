1. THE SOURCE ASSERTION AND ITS QUANTIFIERS

The English explicitly states that the third difference of log p(n) is
eventually negative. The Chinese likewise states “三阶差分最终为负”. Both
therefore impose this necessary claim under the usual increasing-index
difference convention:

  exists N in Nat, for all n in Nat,
    N <= n implies log p(n+3) - 3 log p(n+2)
      + 3 log p(n+1) - log p(n) < 0.

Disproof.lean:13 defines EventualNegativity with exactly these existential
and universal quantifiers and a strict inequality. There is no finite bound
on the quantified indices, no sample-only witness, and no special choice of
cutoff concealed in the target. Disproof.lean:16 proves its negation without
theorem hypotheses. In particular, this is not merely a counterexample at one
small n, which would not refute an eventual statement.

Disproof.lean:21 proves the stronger exact statement

  for all N in Nat, exists n in Nat, N <= n and 0 < thirdDifference n.

The generic theorem first denies an eventually nonpositive tail. Classical
negation over the real order yields strict-positive witnesses at arbitrarily
large indices. This is stronger than the nonnegative witnesses needed merely
to refute eventual strict negativity. The conversion in the formal source
preserves the cutoff premise N <= n and has no omitted restriction.

The source's phrase about being stronger than log-concavity is not used as a
hypothesis. Neither log-concavity nor a claimed theorem about its threshold is
assumed. Failure of an explicit necessary conjunct suffices to disprove the
conjunction, irrespective of the other qualitative descriptions.

2. DIFFERENCE CONVENTION AND UNDEFINED LAYER LANGUAGE

The explicit formal difference is diff u n = u(n+1) - u(n). Its third iterate
expands to the polynomial used in thirdDifference, with coefficients
(-1, 3, -3, 1) at successive increasing indices. The generic proof obtains
antitonicity of the second differences by this exact expansion; no sign or
index shift is lost.

For n >= 3 the usual backward difference is

  f(n) - 3 f(n-1) + 3 f(n-2) - f(n-3) = Delta^3 f(n-3).

Disproof.lean:31 separately refutes eventual negativity of this expression.
Given a claimed backward cutoff N, it applies the claim at n+3 for each
n >= N. Then n+3 >= N and n+3 >= 3, and natural subtraction simplifies to the
correct forward indices. Thus all three subtractions avoid truncation
problems. The backward theorem's explicit 3 <= n premise is appropriate.

The supplied source does not state a formula for the difference operator.
The report transparently adopts the conventional forward operator and also
addresses the standard backward operator. A deliberately reversed operator
f(n)-f(n+1) would negate an odd-order difference and would be a different
claim; the submission does not purport to cover that reversal. This is a
limit of the source's terse specification, not a sign error in the proof.
Under both ordinary forward and backward conventions the refutation is
faithful. Starting partition indices at 1 instead of 0 only discards finitely
many terms and cannot change either eventual assertion.

The English “convexification layer” and the Chinese wording “负的组合为凸化层”
are not accompanied by a definition, construction, hypothesis, or alternate
difference convention in either language. The report correctly flags this
absence and does not invent an auxiliary object. The ordinary reading makes
the asserted negativity a necessary conjunct; its proved failure suffices.
The proof makes no mathematical claim about a separately defined layer.
If a future source revision changes the underlying sequence or difference
operator, that would require a fresh source-fidelity assessment.

The conventional meaning of p(n) is reinforced by the source's explicit
partition parenthetical in both languages. The natural logarithm is the
usual formal choice. Any usual logarithm base greater than 1 differs by a
positive constant, so the sign interpretation is unchanged. No unusual base
or reversed-index convention is silently used to obtain the result.

3. ACTUAL MATHEMATICAL OBJECTS

PartitionBounds.lean:11 defines partitionCount n as
Fintype.card (Nat.Partition n). The stock definition is a structure with a
Multiset Nat, a proof that all its members are strictly positive, and a
proof that its sum is n. Equality is equality of the multiset; order of parts
does not create distinct partitions. The stock Fintype instance is produced
from finite compositions via a proved surjection, so this cardinality is
the number of actual integer partitions, not an arbitrary surrogate.

The stock inhabited instance gives the one-part partition after deleting
zeros. It is valid also at n=0. Stock UniquePartitionZero confirms that the
empty partition is the only partition of zero. Consequently p(0)=1, and no
undefined logarithm is reached. Growth.lean:7 defines partitionLog as the
real logarithm of this exact positive natural cardinality cast to Real.

No synthetic partition recurrence, unproved asymptotic formula, arbitrary
sequence parameter, or unconstrained counting function replaces p in the
capstone theorems. All casts preserve positivity and the ordinary real
ordering. Naturals are used only for indices and finite combinatorial data.

4. PARTITION BOUNDS: ALL DECLARATIONS

parts_card_le: summing the lower bound 1 over each positive part gives a total
number of parts at most n. This includes the empty multiset.

part_le: a member of a multiset of nonnegative naturals is at most the sum,
hence at most n.

largeParts: filters exactly those parts a with not (a <= k), equivalently
a > k. The complementary small-part filter and this filter sum to the
original multiset.

large_card_le: the filtered sum is at most n. Every filtered member is at
least k+1, so card * (k+1) <= n <= k*k. This implies card <= k. The bound is
loose but valid; no strict-bound optimization is required. If k=0, the
assumption forces n=0 and the multiset is empty.

Code: the codomain is exactly
  (Fin(k+1) -> Fin(n+1)) x Fin(k+1) x (Fin k -> Fin(n+1)).
The first coordinate records multiplicities for 0 through k. Including the
unused zero count only enlarges the codomain and is harmless. Each count is
at most the total number of parts, hence at most n. The second coordinate
records the number of large parts, at most k. The last coordinate stores the
increasingly sorted large parts, padded by zero to length k. Each real entry
is an actual part bounded by n, and the padding zero belongs to Fin(n+1).

encode_injective: equality of codes first gives equality of large-part
cardinalities. Equal cardinalities give equal sorted-list lengths. Every
index of either sorted list is less than k by large_card_le, so equality of
the padded coordinate functions gives equality at every genuine list index.
Equal sorted lists imply equal large-part multisets. For each a <= k, the
first coordinate gives equal multiplicities. For a > k, equal large
multisets give equal multiplicities in the original partitions. Multiset
extensionality and Nat.Partition.ext finish the injection. This genuinely
reconstructs all parts and has no assumption of injectivity built into the
definition.

partition_count_bound: Fintype.card_le_of_injective is applied to that proved
injection. The codomain cardinality simplifies to
  (n+1)^(k+1) * ((k+1) * (n+1)^k).
The parentheses match the nested product type. At k=n=0 this equals 1,
correctly bounding the unique partition of zero; Fin 0 functions are unique.

hookPartition: for each i : Fin n, the parts are one copy of i+1 and
n-(i+1) copies of 1. Since i<n, natural subtraction equals the intended
n-i-1 and the sum is n. All parts are positive, including when i=0.

hook_injective: the number of parts is 1+(n-(i+1)) = n-i; equality of these
counts determines i for i<n. This handles the all-ones partition at i=0
and the single-part partition at i=n-1. There is no duplicate endpoint.

nat_le_partitionCount: cardinality of Fin n and the proved injection give
p(n)>=n. For n=0, the domain is empty and the inequality remains valid.

partitionCount_pos: the stock inhabited instance supplies positive finite
cardinality for every n. This is separate from p(n)>=n, so the n=0 case does
not incorrectly infer strict positivity from the vacuous lower bound.

5. LOGARITHMIC GROWTH: ALL DECLARATIONS

partitionLog_nonneg: a positive natural count is at least 1 after casting,
which is the actual hypothesis of stock Real.log_nonneg. It does not merely
assume that log of a positive number is nonnegative.

partitionLog_unbounded: for arbitrary real C, choose a natural n>exp(C).
Then p(n)>=n>exp(C)>0, and strict monotonicity of log gives log p(n)>C.
Thus unboundedness is proved with the exact forall C exists n quantifiers.

partitionLog_bound: all log arguments are proved positive before using log
monotonicity and log-product identities. Taking logs of the combinatorial
bound produces (2k+1) log(n+1)+log(k+1). The natural exponents and casts are
handled without changing the bound.

partitionLog_square_bound: setting n=k*k and using
k*k+1 <= (k+1)^2 produces (4k+3) log(k+1). Multiplication by 2k+1 preserves
order because k>=0. The bound is also correct at k=0.

partitionLog_no_affine_lower: for arbitrary real a>0 and b, stock
Real.isLittleO_log_id_atTop supplies an eventual bound
  abs(log x) <= (a/28) abs(x).
Choose a natural k>max(max(M,1),-2b/a). Then k>1, k+1>=M and k+1>0, so
absolute values reduce to ordinary nonnegative log(k+1) and k+1. For k>=1,
  (4k+3)(k+1)=4k^2+7k+3 <= 14k^2.
This yields f(k^2) <= (a/2)k^2. The strict choice k>-2b/a implies
-2b<ak<=ak^2, hence (a/2)k^2 < b+ak^2. Therefore n=k^2 is a valid strict
witness, including when b is negative or zero. Division uses a>0 throughout.

Only a subsequence of square indices is bounded in the final growth
argument. That is enough to violate every proposed global positive affine
lower bound. The generic theorem asks for exactly this violation; it does
not demand a proved all-index asymptotic or eventual growth estimate.
No Hardy--Ramanujan formula, log-concavity assertion, partition monotonicity,
or numerical computation is assumed or needed.

6. GENERIC SEQUENCE OBSTRUCTION: ALL DECLARATIONS

linear_upper: induction telescopes a uniform upper bound A on first
differences to u(n)<=u(0)+nA. It works for any real A.

nonneg_slope: if u>=0 and all first differences are <=A<0, choose
n>u(0)/(-A). The preceding bound is negative, contradicting u(n)>=0.
Thus a uniform first-difference upper bound on a nonnegative sequence must
be nonnegative. The division has positive denominator -A.

diff_nonneg_of_antitone: if diff u is antitone, every first difference in
the tail starting at n is bounded above by diff u n. Apply nonneg_slope to
that nonnegative shifted sequence. This proves diff u n>=0 for each n.

second_nonneg: assume diff(diff u) is antitone and suppose one value at n
is negative. All later second differences are bounded above by this same
negative value; in particular the tail's first differences are antitone.
The preceding lemma makes those first differences nonnegative. Now apply
nonneg_slope to this nonnegative first-difference sequence, whose increments
are bounded by the original negative second difference. Contradiction.
It follows that all second differences are nonnegative. This argument does
not incorrectly infer the sign solely from monotonicity; nonnegativity of
the underlying sequence is essential and is explicitly supplied.

linear_lower: apply linear_upper to -u with upper bound -A to derive
u(n)>=u(0)+nA when all first differences are >=A.

bounded_prefix: an induction constructs a real bound for every finite
prefix 0..N by taking a maximum. This includes N=0 and n=N exactly.

not_eventually_nonpos_third: suppose a nonpositive third-difference tail
begins at N and set u(k)=f(N+k). Its second differences are antitone.
second_nonneg makes them nonnegative, hence its first differences are
monotone. If some first difference at k equals a>0, every later first
difference is at least a. With M=N+k, linear_lower and f(M)>=0 give
f(M+j)>=ja. For n<=M, f(n)>=0>=a(n-M). For n>=M, write n=M+j and use the
tail bound. Thus f(n)>=-aM+an globally. The quantified no-affine-lower
hypothesis applied at a and b=-aM contradicts this. The explicit split at
n<=M is crucial and is present; the global witness cannot escape into the
finite prefix.

Therefore all tail first differences are <=0, so the tail is antitone and
bounded above by f(N). bounded_prefix supplies a bound through N, including
f(N), and hence a bound for every f(n). This contradicts the specified
unboundedness. No eventual negativity or target conclusion was assumed
except locally inside this legitimate contradiction argument.

not_eventually_negative_third: strict-negative tails are nonpositive tails,
so the stronger theorem directly refutes them.

no_affine_lower_of_sublinear: this optional helper is also valid. Given a>0,
use an all-index upper bound with slope a/2 and choose
n>(C-b)/(a/2). Then f(n)<b+an. Its stronger input is not assumed anywhere
for the actual partition sequence.

not_eventually_negative_third_of_sublinear: this optional wrapper correctly
composes the preceding helper and the generic negative-tail theorem. It is
not the theorem used in the concrete capstone. Its presence therefore does
not hide an unproved sublinearity obligation in the actual application.
