\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} the absorption time of ARW is the time when all
particles sleep. \textit{Conjecture:} for fixed $\lambda > \lambda_c$,
the tail of the local absorption time is exponential,
$P(T > t) \le \exp(-c(\lambda)\, t)$, with the explicit linear rate
$c(\lambda) = \lambda - 1$; the rate tends to $\infty$ as
$\lambda \to \infty$ and to $0$ as $\lambda \downarrow \lambda_c$.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

The explicit rate $c(\lambda) = \lambda - 1$ is \emph{negative} on the
whole region $(\lambda_c, 1)$ for any threshold $\lambda_c < 1$ --- the
known ARW regime (the critical density is strictly below $1$;
Rolla--Sidoravicius). At $\lambda = 9/10$:
$c(\lambda) = -1/10 < 0$, so the claimed bound is
$\exp(-c\, t) = e^{t/10}$, which \emph{grows}: it exceeds $1$ for every
$t > 0$ and diverges. A growing bound is not an exponential decay
tail. Moreover, the stated limit forces
$c(\lambda_c) = \lambda_c - 1 = 0$, i.e.\ $\lambda_c = 1$; but for any
$\lambda_c < 1$ one has $c(\lambda_c) = \lambda_c - 1 < 0 \ne 0$,
contradicting the conjecture's own limit at its own critical point.
The formula $c = \lambda - 1$ is compatible with the stated limits
only if $\lambda_c = 1$ --- neither assumed nor true.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} checks with exact fractions that
$c(9/10) = -1/10 < 0$, that $e^{t/10}$ exceeds $1$ and diverges
($t = 1, 10, 100$), and that $c(\lambda_c) < 0 \ne 0$ for
$\lambda_c \in \{1/2, 7/10, 9/10\}$. The Lean package (core Lean~4,
v4.33.1) certifies the scaled-pair rate lemma (negativity and
nonvanishing of $(u-v)/v$ whenever $u < v$), the instance $9 < 10$,
and the growing exponent $10 - 9 = 1 > 0$; all four audited theorems
report \texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies the arithmetic core (negativity and nonvanishing
of the linear rate below $1$, universally in the scaled form); the ARW
threshold fact $\lambda_c < 1$ is the classical
Rolla--Sidoravicius theorem, cited in prose, as is the
probability-theoretic reading of growing versus decaying bounds.
