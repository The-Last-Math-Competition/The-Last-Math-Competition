Geometry.lean

Ambient F d is literally Fin d -> F. IsKakeya asks for a base point for each
nonzero vector and membership of a+t*v for every field element t. It does
not omit directions or replace full lines by a finite sample. line_injective
derives a nonzero coordinate from v!=0 and cancels that coordinate; line_card
then counts the image of the full parameter field. Thus the lines actually
have q distinct points in a finite field.

family is a Finset.univ filter in dimension n+2 with predicate
w 0=0 OR exists z, w 1+(w 0)^2=z^2. It is the planned concrete family.
family_isKakeya splits on v 0=0. In that case the whole parameterized line
lies in w 0=0, including when only a later direction coordinate is nonzero.
Otherwise b=v 1/(2*v 0), and only base coordinate 1 is b^2. The polynomial
identity uses 2*b*v 0=v 1 and yields the square (b+t*v 0)^2. The assumption
(2:F)!=0 is precisely the odd-characteristic requirement, and v 0!=0 supplies
the other nonzero denominator. The first two Fin coordinates are distinct
for every n because the dimension is n+2. No finite check or external result
substitutes for these symbolic witnesses.

Squares.lean

finite_field_square_count explicitly defines the square image and all its
fibers. It proves the zero fiber is {0}; a nonzero square has a representative
z!=0, distinct roots z and -z, and no other roots by the difference-of-squares
identity. It counts over the image, separates zero, and concludes
2*card(square image)=card F+1. The argument uses the same (2:F)!=0 assumption
and applies to any finite field satisfying it. The final omega step closes
an identity already supplied by the symbolic fiber counts, not an enumeration
of field instances.

Counting.lean

slice is exactly the allowed-y predicate at a fixed first coordinate.
familyEquiv maps an actual member of family to its first coordinate, a member
of the corresponding slice, and the two-tail function Fin n -> F. Its inverse
uses Fin.cons twice and checks the actual membership predicate. The inverse
laws show that neither accepted pairs nor free-coordinate assignments are
lost or duplicated.

slice_card counts all q values at x=0, and at x!=0 uses the explicit bijection
y -> y+x^2 onto the square image, with inverse z -> z-x^2. The resulting
family_card_product is (q+(q-1)*|S|)*q^n. The subtraction q-1 here is legitimate:
the field is nonempty, and family_card_identity explicitly proves q>0 and
q-1+1=q before combining the product count with the square count. It then
establishes the exact subtraction-free identity

  2*card(family)+q^n=(q^2+2*q)*q^n.

family_card_real casts this identity to the reals and solves for the actual
cardinality, obtaining ((q^2+2*q-1)/2)*q^n. Subtraction and division in this
formula are genuinely real; there is no truncation or rounded surrogate.
The full counting and real-formula source was present when I read this file,
although no compilation claim is independently made by this review.

Bounds.lean

family_lt_proposed_bound assumes an arbitrary REAL c and 2*(c+1)<q. It derives
q>0 from the actual finite field, uses family_card_real, proves the strict
planar inequality, and multiplies by q^n>0. Its conclusion compares the
ACTUAL family's real cardinality with q^(n+2)*(1-c/q). Denominator nonzeroness
is available from q>0, and the power conversion has the right exponent.

normalized_minimum_upper_bound first invokes minimum_le_card on family and
family_isKakeya. It divides that inequality by q^(n+2)>0 and substitutes the
exact count to conclude

every n, not merely a density calculation on an unexplained numeric sequence.

to 1/2. The remaining asymptotic negation still requires a supply of fields
with arbitrarily large orders.
