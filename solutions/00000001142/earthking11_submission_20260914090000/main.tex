\section*{Verdict: FALSE}

We disprove conjecture \texttt{00000001142} as stated. For the mod-$p$ Witt
algebra $W(1;1)$ the automorphism group has order $24$ at $p=3$ and $500$ at
$p=5$, whereas the conjectured formula $p(p-1)$ gives $6$ and $20$. The claim
is true only at $p=2$; it fails for \emph{every} $p\ge 3$.

\section{The conjecture, quoted verbatim}

From \texttt{conjectures/00000001142.md}:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{English.} Conjecture: The order of the automorphism group of the
mod-$p$ Witt algebra $W(1;1)$ is $p(p-1)$ (Witt automorphisms).
\end{quote}

The conjecture is filed in both English and Chinese; the Chinese original is
reproduced verbatim (in Unicode) in \texttt{README.md}, and the two versions
are identical in content. The PDF font does not embed CJK glyphs, so the
Chinese is kept in the markdown copy rather than duplicated here.

\section{The algebra and its dimension}

\begin{definition}
$W(1;1)=\Der\bigl(\Fp[x]/(x^p)\bigr)$ is the Lie algebra of derivations of the
truncated polynomial ring. It has the $p$ basis elements
\[
b_k = x^k \frac{d}{dx}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \dots, p-1,
\]
and $\dim W(1;1) = p$ (not $p-1$). The bracket is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
[b_k,b_l] = (l-k)\, b_{k+l-1}\quad \text{if } 1 \le k+l \le p,
\qquad\text{and } [b_k,b_l]=0 \text{ otherwise}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

For example $b_0 = d/dx$, $b_1 = x\,d/dx$, \dots, $b_{p-1}=x^{p-1}d/dx$.

\section{The witness $p=3$}

At $p=3$ the algebra is $3$-dimensional with basis $b_0,b_1,b_2$ and bracket
table
\[
[b_0,b_1]=b_0,\quad [b_0,b_2]=2b_1,\quad [b_1,b_2]=b_2,
\]
the other brackets following from antisymmetry ($[b_k,b_k]=0$,
$[b_l,b_k]=-[b_k,b_l]$).

A linear map is a $3\times 3$ matrix over $\mathbb{F}_3$; we enumerated
\emph{all} $3^9 = 19683$ of them:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l r}
\toprule
candidate matrices & $3^9 = 19683$ \\
invertible ($\det \ne 0$) & $|\GL(3,\mathbb{F}_3)| = 11232$ \\
invertible \emph{and} bracket-preserving & $\mathbf{24}$ \\
conjectured value $p(p-1) = 3\cdot 2$ & $6$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Hence
\[
\boxed{\;|\Aut(W(1;1))| = 24 \ne 6 = p(p-1) \quad (p=3)\;}
\]
and the conjecture is false at $p=3$. The count $24$ is machine-verified twice:
by the exhaustive enumeration in \texttt{reproduce.py} and by the core-Lean
theorem \texttt{aut\_count\_3} in \texttt{lean4/Main.lean}.

\subsection*{Structural confirmation: $W(1;1)\cong \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{F}_3)$}

At $p=3$ the algebra is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{F}_3)$ via
\[
h = 2b_1, \qquad e = b_2, \qquad f = 2b_0 ,
\]
for which one checks $[h,e]=2e$, $[h,f]=-2f$, $[e,f]=h$. Consequently
\[
|\Aut(W(1;1))| = |\Aut(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{F}_3))|
= |\operatorname{PGL}(2,3)| = |S_4| = 24 .
\]
This is an independent theoretical confirmation of the numerical value $24$.
The element-order distribution of the $24$ automorphisms is
\[
\{1:1,\; 2:9,\; 3:8,\; 4:6\},
\]
which is exactly the order distribution of $S_4$; it is computed in
\texttt{reproduce.py}. Note that the exceptional isomorphism with
$\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{F}_3)$ exists only at $p=3$.

\section{The value at $p=5$}

At $p=5$ the algebra is $5$-dimensional. A direct brute force over $5^{25}$
matrices is infeasible, so we use the following exact structural reduction: the
pair $\{b_0,b_4\}$ generates $W(1;1)$ as a Lie algebra, so an automorphism
$\varphi$ is determined by the pair
$\bigl(\varphi(b_0),\varphi(b_4)\bigr)$; and $\varphi(b_0)$ must be a
\emph{regular nilpotent} element, because
$\operatorname{ad}(\varphi(b_0)) = \varphi \circ \operatorname{ad}(b_0)\circ
\varphi^{-1}$ is conjugate to $\operatorname{ad}(b_0)$, a single Jordan block of
size $p$. There are exactly $500$ regular nilpotent elements. Enumerating this
pair for all of them, reconstructing
$\varphi(b_3),\varphi(b_2),\varphi(b_1)$ from the relations
$[b_0,b_k]=k\,b_{k-1}$ and testing bracket preservation and invertibility gives
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l r}
\toprule
regular nilpotent candidates for $\varphi(b_0)$ & $500$ \\
extensions to an automorphism (each candidate has exactly one) & $500$ \\
conjectured value $p(p-1) = 5\cdot 4$ & $20$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Hence
\[
\boxed{\;|\Aut(W(1;1))| = 500 = (p-1)p^{\,p-2}\big|_{p=5} \ne 20 = p(p-1)
\quad (p=5)\;}
\]
and the conjecture is false at $p=5$ as well. This computation is carried out
exactly in \texttt{reproduce.py}; it was validated against the $p=3$ brute
force (where it returns $24$).

\section{The exception $p=2$}

At $p=2$ the algebra is the $2$-dimensional non-abelian Lie algebra
$[b_0,b_1]=b_0$ (since $k+l=1\le 2$), and $|\Aut| = 2 = p(p-1)$. So the
conjectured formula is correct at $p=2$ and wrong for every $p\ge 3$; the
number $p(p-1)$ is therefore not the order of $\Aut(W(1;1))$ in general.

\section{Rescue attempts that fail}

We tried to save the conjecture by changing the reading of ``Aut''; none
succeeds.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.40\textwidth} p{0.52\textwidth}}
\toprule
Reading & Outcome \\
\midrule
Automorphisms of the \emph{restricted} Lie algebra (respecting the $p$-power
map $X\mapsto X^{[p]}$) & the same counts result: at $p=3$ all $24$ Lie
automorphisms preserve the $p$-power map, so the restricted automorphism group
is again $24$; likewise the count at $p=5$ is again $500$. No rescue. \\
A different (non-standard) $p$-power convention & gives $2$ at $p=3$ and $4$
at $p=5$, even further from $p(p-1)$. No rescue. \\
Inner automorphisms & $W(1;1)$ is finite-dimensional, so the inner
automorphism group is trivial and $\operatorname{Out}=\Aut$, with order $24$
(resp.\ $500$). No rescue. \\
Automorphisms of the restricted enveloping algebra & a different (larger,
associative-algebra) group; not $\Aut(W(1;1))$. No rescue. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{The Möbius subgroup: where $p(p-1)$ really comes from}

The number $p(p-1)$ does occur, but as the order of a \emph{proper} subgroup.
The Möbius/Witt transformations
\[
x \longmapsto \frac{a x}{1+b x}, \qquad a\in \mathbb{F}_p^{\times},\;
b\in \mathbb{F}_p ,
\]
act on vector fields by pullback, $\varphi^*(b_k) = \varphi(x)^k/\varphi'(x)
\cdot d/dx$, i.e.
\[
\varphi^*(b_k) = a^{\,k-1} x^k (1+b x)^{2-k}\,\frac{d}{dx},
\]
expanded as a power series in $x$ and truncated at degree $p-1$. This action
preserves the bracket \eqref{eq:bracket}, and the $p(p-1)$ maps $(a,b)$ form a
subgroup of $\Aut(W(1;1))$:
\[
|\{\,x\mapsto a x/(1+b x)\,\}| = p(p-1) = 6 \ (p=3), \qquad 20 \ (p=5).
\]
But these are \emph{proper} subgroups, since
\[
6 < 24 = |\Aut| \ (p=3), \qquad 20 < 500 = |\Aut| \ (p=5).
\]
Thus the conjecture is true exactly if ``$\Aut$'' is silently read as this
Möbius/Witt subgroup rather than the full automorphism group. Under the
literal reading of the conjecture as filed, it is false for all $p\ge 3$.

\section{Formalisation}

The $p=3$ refutation is formalised in the Lean~4 project under \texttt{lean4/}
(core Lean only, \texttt{import Std}, no Mathlib, no \texttt{sorry}). The main
statements are
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
theorem num\_mats : allMats.length = 3 \^{} 9\\
theorem aut\_count\_3 : auts3.length = 24\\
theorem claim\_false\_3 : auts3.length \(\neq\) 3 * (3 - 1)
\end{quote}
The algebra is defined by its nine structure constants only, and the number
$24$ is produced by exhaustively filtering the $3^9$ matrices over
$\mathbb{F}_3$. The enumeration is discharged by the core kernel
\texttt{decide} tactic, \emph{not} by \texttt{native\_decide}; the axiom audit
(\texttt{lake env lean Check.lean}) reports that the theorems depend on no
axioms at all. The measured \texttt{lake build} wall time is about $50$~seconds
($47.5$~s on the final clean build).
The case $p=5$ is out of scope for Lean (a faithful analogue would need
$5^{25}$ matrices) and is covered by \texttt{reproduce.py}. See
\texttt{lean4/README.md} for details.
