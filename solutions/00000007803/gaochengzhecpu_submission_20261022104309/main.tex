\begin{abstract}
Take independent samples uniform on $[-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]$.
Their common law is centered, isotropic and log-concave.
The maximum absolute value is bounded by $\sqrt3$ for
every sample size, almost surely. Dividing by the square
root of the sample size therefore gives zero in the
limit, as do the centering and correction terms in the
source. The actual normalized laws cannot converge to
any nondegenerate Gumbel distribution. The construction
uses a genuine infinite independent process and its
actual maximum and pushforward laws.
\end{abstract}

\section*{An actual isotropic log-concave law}
Let $r=\sqrt3$ and let $\mu$ be the uniform probability
measure on $[-r,r]$. Its density with respect to
Lebesgue measure is
\[
       f(x)=\frac1{2r}\boldsymbol1_{[-r,r]}(x).
\]
The interval has length $2r>0$, so this density
integrates to one. It is log-concave in the usual
extended sense:
\[
 f(tx+(1-t)y)\geq f(x)^t f(y)^{1-t},
                         \qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\]
For $0<t<1$, if $x,y$ lie in the interval, their convex
combination does too and the inequality is equality.
If either lies outside, the right side is zero.
At $t=0,1$ the endpoint identity gives the result.

The identity and square functions are integrable on
this compact support. Direct integration yields
\[
 \int x\,d\mu=0,\qquad
 \int x^2\,d\mu=\frac1{2r}\int_{-r}^{r}x^2\,dx
                =\frac{r^2}{3}=1.
\]
Thus the real one-dimensional random variable is
isotropic: it has mean zero and covariance equal to
the one-dimensional identity. The source does not
exclude fixed dimension or compactly supported laws.
Independence below also meets any additional usual
independent-sample reading of its sequence hypothesis.

\section*{A genuine infinite independent sequence}
Use the probability space
\[
 (\Omega,\mathbb P)=
       (\mathbb R^{\mathbb N},\mu^{\otimes\mathbb N}),
             \qquad X_i(\omega)=\omega_i.
\]
For every finite index set $S$ and measurable sets $B_i$,
its actual cylinder probabilities are
\[
 \mathbb P\{X_i\in B_i\text{ for all }i\in S\}
                         =\prod_{i\in S}\mu(B_i).
\]
Each marginal law is $\mu$, and the coordinates are
independent. Consequently every $X_i$ has the density,
log-concavity and isotropic moments established above.
This is an actual product probability measure, rather
than a bounded deterministic sequence used as a proxy.

Each event $\{|X_i|\leq r\}$ has probability one.
Their countable intersection also has probability one.
On that single common event, for every $N\geq1$ the
actual maximum
\[
             M_N=\max_{0\leq i<N}|X_i|
                         \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
                         0\leq M_N\leq r.
\]
In particular $M_N/\sqrt N\to0$ almost surely. This
analytic conclusion needs only boundedness, although
the actual independent log-concave model above supplies
all the conjecture's stated probabilistic hypotheses.

\section*{The stated centering and correction also vanish}
For sufficiently large $N$, define the source's terms
\[
 c_N=\frac{\sqrt{2\log N}}{\sqrt N},\qquad
 d_N=\frac{\log\log N}{\sqrt{2\log N}\sqrt N}.
\]
Here $\log$ is the natural logarithm. Any fixed change
of base leaves the vanishing conclusions unchanged.
The standard elementary limits
\[
 \frac{\log N}{N}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \frac{\log t}{\sqrt t}\longrightarrow0
                                      \quad(t\to\infty)
\]
give $c_N\to0$ and $d_N\to0$. For the latter, put
$t=\log N$ and write
$d_N=2^{-1/2}(\log t/\sqrt t)N^{-1/2}$.
All expressions are ordinary real expressions for
$N\geq3$; arbitrary initial values do not affect a limit.

\section*{The normalized law cannot be Gumbel}
For any fixed real coefficient $a$, put
\[
           Z_N=\frac{M_N}{\sqrt N}-c_N-a d_N.
\]
This includes the displayed centering without a
correction by taking $a=0$, and either sign of any
fixed multiple of the stated correction. On the common
probability-one event above,
\[
       -c_N-a d_N\leq Z_N
          \leq\frac r{\sqrt N}-c_N-a d_N.
\]
Both endpoints tend to zero, so $Z_N\to0$ almost surely.
More specifically, the deterministic upper bound is
less than one for all sufficiently large $N$. Therefore
the actual pushforward distribution obeys
\[
                        \Pr(Z_N\leq1)=1
                         \quad\text{eventually}.
\]

A nondegenerate Gumbel law with any location
$\ell\in\mathbb R$ and scale $s>0$ has the continuous CDF
\[
       F_{\ell,s}(x)=
          \exp\!\left[-\exp\!\left(-\frac{x-\ell}{s}\right)\right].
\]
In particular $F_{\ell,s}(1)<1$, since the inner
exponential is strictly positive. Convergence in law
to this distribution would require convergence of the
actual CDF at its continuity point $1$, contradicting
the eventual identity above.

For completeness, that necessary CDF condition follows
directly from the bounded-continuous-function definition
of convergence in law. Bound the indicator of
$(-\infty,1]$ above and below by continuous functions
that interpolate linearly across intervals of width
$\varepsilon$ around one. Convergence of their integrals
bounds the lower and upper limits of the CDF between
$F(1-\varepsilon)$ and $F(1+\varepsilon)$.
Continuity at one and $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ give
the required equality.

\begin{theorem}
The supplied normalization of maxima of isotropic
log-concave sequences need not converge to a
nondegenerate Gumbel law, even for independent
identically distributed samples.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The independent uniform sequence above satisfies the
hypotheses. Its actual normalized CDF is eventually
one at the finite continuity point $1$, whereas every
nondegenerate Gumbel CDF is strictly less than one
there. This holds with or without the stated correction.
\end{proof}
The first distributional assertion is already false;
the additional proposed relation involving $L_K$,
an extreme exponent and a thin-shell constant is not
needed. No definition of those extra constants is chosen.

\section*{Formalization and reproducibility}
The Lean project defines $\mu$ as normalized Lebesgue
measure restricted to $[-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]$. It proves the
actual density representation, its multiplicative
log-concavity inequality, integrability and the exact
first and second moments. The infinite sample space
uses Mathlib's genuine infinite product measure.
Actual finite-cylinder factorization, marginal
pushforwards and independence are proved, as are
the centered and unit-second-moment coordinate integrals.

The running maximum is defined recursively, and proved
equal to the genuine maximum of the first $N$ absolute
coordinate values. It is measurable and uniformly
bounded on a single probability-one event. Formal
little-o logarithm limits prove that both original
normalization terms vanish. The resulting almost-sure
zero limits hold both without correction and with
every fixed real multiple of the displayed correction.

The event CDF is proved equal to Mathlib's CDF of the
actual pushforward law. Its value at one is eventually
one, and the continuous Gumbel formula is strictly
below one. Lean refutes the necessary CDF convergence
condition. It does not construct a separate Gumbel
measure or formalize the full weak-convergence equivalence;
the preceding page proves the required necessity.

The formal index is $n\geq0$ with sample size $N=n+1$.
The maximum, denominator, center and correction all use
that same sample size. This is only a reindexing of the
source's first-$N$-samples statement, with no change
to the asymptotic scale.
