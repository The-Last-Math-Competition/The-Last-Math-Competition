1. Definitions and the actual integration predicate

E is EuclideanSpace R (Fin 3), E2 is EuclideanSpace R (Fin 2), and S2 is the
actual unit-sphere subtype of E. This avoids the raw-Pi sup-norm error.
surface is two-dimensional Hausdorff measure on that metric subtype; sigma
is its inverse-total-mass scaling. SphericalDesign t X requires nonemptiness
and equal-weight integration of EVERY real multivariate polynomial with
totalDegree<=t. X is a Finset, so membership has no multiplicities. The
generalized internal DesignFor predicate is definitionally the same at sigma.

The sphereIso construction restricts an actual ambient linear isometry
equivalence and supplies the inverse and isometry proof. Norm/squared-norm/
inner-product coordinate lemmas expose the correct Euclidean metric.

2. SurfaceMeasure: actual finite positive Hausdorff surface measure

The rational stereographic charts are defined for both hemisphere signs.
The source proves their unit norm, exact chordal identity, and global
2-Lipschitz bound. The inverse-chart construction covers the closed positive
and negative hemispheres, including poles/equator, with positive denominators.
The coordinate projection is proved 1-Lipschitz, and its image of the sphere
is proved equal to the closed two-dimensional unit disk using a square-root
height for the reverse inclusion.

The only Hausdorff-Haar instance introduced is dimension two on E2. Compact
disk mass is finite there. Two chart images bound ambient H2 sphere mass
above; projection onto the disk and positivity of its open ball bound it
strictly below. The implementation does not incorrectly infer H2 local
finiteness on E3 or infer finite sphere measure merely from compactness.

map_surface explicitly identifies the intrinsic surface pushforward with
ambient H2 restricted to the sphere. surface_mass_eq transports the positive
finite mass bounds. The sigma probability instance is constructed only after
those facts, so inverse normalization is justified. Orthogonal invariance
comes from the actual sphere isometries preserving Hausdorff measure and
scaling the measure. Polynomial continuity and compact-sphere integrability
are proved with respect to sigma. No moments are assumed in this construction.

3. SphereMoments: moment derivation and polynomial support bridge

Coordinate sign reversals and permutations are actual Euclidean linear
isometries. Invariance proves zero for any monomial with any odd exponent.
The constant integral uses probability normalization. Coordinate square
integrals follow from permutation invariance and the sphere equation. The
fourth/mixed moments follow from the explicit orthogonal coordinate change,
odd mixed integrals, and the integrated identity
  x0^4 + x0^2*x1^2 + x0^2*x2^2 = x0^2.
All uses of additive integral splitting have supplied integrability proofs.

sphereMoment is an internal table that has arbitrary out-of-range behavior,
but its integral theorem explicitly requires sum_i d_i<=5. Under that bound,
the odd case is exhaustive; otherwise omega forces each exponent into
{0,2,4}, and the Cartesian case split eliminates sums exceeding five. The
remaining ten nonzero cases use the proved constant/quadratic/quartic/mixed
integrals. Thus all 56 degree-at-most-five monomials are covered.

evalOnSphere_as_sum expands the actual MvPolynomial finite support.
designFor_of_monomial_exact bounds every supported exponent sum using
MvPolynomial.le_totalDegree, converts Finsupp sums to the coordinate sum,
and distributes finite sums and integrals with integrability proofs. This
is the required backward bridge from a monomial certificate to full
polynomial exactness. A two-way named equivalence is unnecessary when the
needed forward applications test explicit polynomials directly, as in
DesignExactness. The implementation does not redefine 'design' as moments.

Non-substantive wording observation

The source's plane45 map is
  ((x+y)/sqrt(2), (x-y)/sqrt(2), z).
It is an orthogonal involution with determinant -1 (a reflection), rather
than a plane rotation. Its proof and use only require orthogonal invariance,
so there is no mathematical defect. Any report describing the exact map
should call it an 'orthogonal coordinate change' or reflection instead of
claiming it is a 45-degree rotation.
