\section*{Statement and verdict}
The conjecture asserts a one-to-one correspondence between Hermitian metrics
and holomorphic structures through their Chern connections.  As stated, it is
false: distinct metrics can induce the same Chern connection.

\section*{Counterexample}
Let $E=\mathbb C$ be the trivial holomorphic line bundle over a point.  Define
two Hermitian metrics by
\[
 h_1(z,w)=z\overline w,\qquad h_2(z,w)=2z\overline w.
\]
They are different because $h_1(1,1)=1$ and $h_2(1,1)=2$.  A connection has
no directional data on a point, so there is exactly one connection and both
metrics induce it.  Consequently the metric-to-Chern-connection map is not
injective and cannot be bijective.

The witness also works on a trivial holomorphic line bundle over any connected
complex manifold.  In the standard holomorphic frame the Chern connection
form is $A=h^{-1}\partial h$.  Both $h=1$ and $h=2$ are constant, hence
$\partial h=0$ and $A=0$ for both.

\begin{proposition}
The claimed one-to-one correspondence is false.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two metrics above are unequal and have equal Chern connection.  Therefore
the correspondence is not injective.  Every bijection is injective, so it is
not a bijection.
\end{proof}

\section*{Scope}
The familiar uniqueness of the Chern connection says that, for a \emph{fixed}
holomorphic structure and a \emph{fixed} Hermitian metric, there is a unique
compatible connection.  The counterexample does not dispute that theorem; it
refutes the stronger injectivity assertion actually filed.

The accompanying Lean project formalizes the finite core of this witness:
positive scales $1$ and $2$ are distinct, the connection type on a point is a
singleton, and the induced map is not injective.
