Follow-up source qualification: robust disproof of the upper-bound clause

Scope and relationship to the original assessment
This follow-up reconsiders only the logical/source qualification requested by
the parent: whether a complete disproof through the universal upper bound can
faithfully settle the source without resolving its separate sharpness wording.
The original assessment.txt is preserved unchanged. Its concerns remain relevant
to proving the entire asserted upper-bound-and-sharpness statement. They should
not be applied as a blanket prohibition on the robust disproof scope specified
below. This follow-up supersedes that broader gate decision for this scope only.

Decision
Yes. The generic upper-bound assertion has a sufficiently conventional meaning
to be falsifiable by an ordinary finite pure integer linear program with integral
data, attained unique integer and real-relaxation optima, and the stated robust
treatment of dimension and data. A verified violation of that assertion would
refute the conjecture as written, regardless of how its additional sharpness
claim is completed. No numerical instance or method for obtaining one is
asserted, considered, or supplied by this qualification.

Explicit conventions required for this qualification
1. The candidate is an ordinary finite pure integer linear program, with its
   objective, constraints, integer-variable domains, and real relaxation fully
   written out. All numerical input is integral. Both optima are attained and
   unique; the relaxation optimizer is also a vertex.
2. n is the number of decision coordinates in the displayed representation.
   Whenever a representation introduces variables, its new dimension is used.
3. Delta is explicitly computed as the maximum absolute value over all finite
   numerical input coefficients, right-hand sides, objective coefficients, and
   finite bounds. The inventory is comprehensive for that representation, and
   any objective constant or other finite scalar included in its model is
   accounted for. Dimension/count metadata are not optimization coefficients.
4. The infinity distance uses the standard coordinate infinity norm between the
   two displayed optimizers in the same representation.
5. The asserted violation is checked in the ordinary standard forms needed to
   resolve equality/inequality and variable-domain conventions. Each such form
   is assessed using its own complete data, dimension, and optimal solutions.
   Equivalence, uniqueness, or a bound violation in a changed representation
   must be demonstrated where it is relied upon, not presumed.

Why the original ambiguities no longer bar this disproof scope

Optimization class: A finite pure integer linear program with integral data is
an ordinary instance of integer programming. It remains within the ordinary
class even if the source's generic wording is read more broadly, for example to
permit mixed-integer or other integer-programming models. Neither language
states a special structural restriction that would exclude such ordinary
instances. The named Ben-Tal-Nemirovski-type constant supplies no written
restriction. It would be inappropriate to import an unstated narrow theorem
class solely to immunize the upper bound against such an instance.

Data: Reading data as the finite numerical input is faithful to the explicit
maximum-absolute-value wording. A complete inventory avoids silently choosing
only a favorable subset of the coefficients. At a fixed representation, an
interpretation that counts only a subset of those numerical coefficients cannot
make a violation established using their full maximum disappear. Representation
changes still require their own accounting as stipulated above. A determinant
or another derived parameter would need a source definition; it is not an
equally supported reading of this stated maximum of absolute data values.

Selection: Unique optima remove the disagreement among arbitrary, nearest, or
existentially selected optimal solutions. Including a relaxation vertex also
covers the common vertex-qualified convention. Existence and attainment are
explicit in this scope rather than left to implication.

Sharpness: Both versions assert an upper bound and additionally assert
optimality. A violation of the universal upper-bound clause is sufficient to
disprove the combined conjecture. A disproof need not first decide whether the
additional sharpness wording means an exact or an asymptotic constant, and it
need not disprove that additional clause separately. A report must nevertheless
identify clearly which clause it falsifies and avoid claiming that every possible
interpretation of the sharpness sentence has independently been disproved.

Remaining boundary
I identify no remaining material, source-supported interpretation that excludes
the specified robust scope. A future clarification could introduce a restricted
optimization class or a different defined parameter, but those would add content
absent from the current source and would warrant a new qualification.

The phrase standard representations here should mean the explicit ordinary
forms relevant to the convention ambiguity. It does not demand survival under
every arbitrarily padded or otherwise altered equivalent encoding: neither the
source nor this qualification makes such an infinite representation-invariance
claim. The reported checks must state exactly which forms they establish.

Rules fit
The reviewer requirements in both RULES files demand correspondence with the
conjecture and a complete proof/disproof. A complete verified falsification of a
conjunct is a disproof of the conjunction and can satisfy that requirement. This
qualification grants no exception to completeness, source fidelity, or the
required report/formal-project correspondence. It does not authorize editing
the conjecture to add assumptions or omitting validation of the asserted input,
optima, distance, dimension, or data maximum.
