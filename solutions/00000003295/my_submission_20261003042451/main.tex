\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} the Dawson asymptotic series (odd series of the
reciprocal) has the whole real axis as its convergence domain.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

The Dawson coefficients $a_n = (2n-1)!!/2^n$ give the term ratio
$a_{n+1}/a_n = \frac{2n+1}{2}\cdot x^{-2}$, which exceeds 1 for all
large $n$ at every fixed $x \ne 0$: the terms grow without bound and
the series diverges by the term test. At $x = 3$ the ratio exceeds 1
from $n = 10$ (kernel-certified: $21 \cdot 9 > 4$). The series is
correctly an asymptotic expansion --- divergent, not convergent.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} confirms the ratio exceeds 1 from $n = 10$ at
$x = 3$ (exact fractions) and that partial sums oscillate with growing
amplitude, while the true Dawson function $F(3) \approx 0.886$ stays
bounded. The Lean package (core Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies the exact
anchors; all three audited theorems report \texttt{does not depend on
any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

Only the all-of-$\mathbb{R}$ convergence claim is refuted.
