\section*{Dawson's reciprocal expansion}
Dawson's integral has the familiar asymptotic expansion
\[
 F(x)\sim\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{2^{n+1}x^{2n+1}}
 =\frac1{2x}+\frac1{4x^3}+\frac3{8x^5}+\frac{15}{16x^7}+\cdots.
\]
The conjecture incorrectly promotes this asymptotic expansion to a series
converging throughout the real axis.

\begin{theorem}
The displayed series diverges at $x=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $a_n=(2n-1)!!/2^{n+1}$.  Then
\[
 \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{2n+1}{2}.
\]
For $n\ge1$ this ratio is at least $3/2$.  In particular $a_4=105/32>1$
and all subsequent terms increase, so $a_n$ does not tend to zero.  A
necessary condition for convergence of a series is that its terms tend to
zero.  Therefore the series diverges at $x=1$ and cannot have the whole real
axis as its convergence domain.
\end{proof}

Indeed the same ratio tends to infinity for every fixed nonzero $x$, after
division by the additional factor $x^2$, so the expansion is divergent for
every fixed finite nonzero $x$.
