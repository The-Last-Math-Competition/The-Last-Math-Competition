\section*{Verdict: FALSE}

We disprove conjecture \texttt{00000002605} as stated. The refutation is
unconditional and completely elementary. The conjecture quantifies over the
class of \emph{semimodular lattices} and asserts that their rank number
sequences --- the Whitney numbers of the second kind --- are strictly
log-concave. A single $5$-element lattice $L$ lies in that class and has rank
numbers
\[
W = (W_0,W_1,W_2,W_3) = (1,2,1,1),
\]
for which log-concavity fails at $k=2$:
\[
W_2^{\,2} \;=\; 1 \;<\; 2 \;=\; W_1 W_3 .
\]
Thus $L$ violates even \emph{weak} log-concavity, a fortiori the filed
\emph{strict} log-concavity. The lattice is distributive, hence modular, hence
upper semimodular, so it belongs to the class the conjecture names. The two
additional clauses of the filing are independently false as well; we prove this
in Sections~\ref{sec:clause1} and~\ref{sec:clause2}.

\section{The geometric-lattice caveat (read this first)}\label{sec:caveat}

We state the scope of the refutation plainly, because the word
``Mason's conjecture'' has a standard meaning in the literature that differs
from the filing.

\begin{quote}
In the literature, ``Mason's conjecture'' concerns \textbf{geometric lattices}
(equivalently: lattices of flats of simple matroids). For geometric lattices,
\textbf{atomisticity is part of the hypothesis}, and the log-concavity of the
Whitney numbers of the second kind there is a known \emph{theorem}
(the strict log-concavity of $h$-vectors / Whitney numbers for geometric
lattices, in the lineage of Mason, Heron, and others). The witness $L$ of this
submission is \textbf{not atomistic}: the join of the atoms $a,b$ is
$a\join b = c \ne \top$. Hence $L$ is \textbf{not geometric}, and this
submission \textbf{does not dispute the geometric-lattice theorem}.

What the witness does refute is the conjecture \emph{as literally worded by the
filing}, which quantifies twice over the strictly larger class ``semimodular
lattices'' and attaches no atomisticity hypothesis. The semimodular lattice $L$
below is a counterexample to that literal statement. In short: this is a clean
exposure of a \emph{class-broadening error} in the filing --- the filed
statement dropped a hypothesis that the source theorem needs --- and not a
counterexample to the classical geometric-lattice theorem.
\end{quote}

The Chinese original of the filing is reproduced verbatim (in Unicode) in
\texttt{README.md}; it repeats the class ``semimodular lattices'' and contains
the same two extra clauses. The PDF font used here does not embed CJK glyphs,
so the Chinese is kept in the markdown copy rather than duplicated here.

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[Whitney numbers of the second kind]\label{def:whitney}
Let $L$ be a finite graded lattice of rank $r$ with rank function
$\rho\colon L \to \{0,1,\dots,r\}$. The \emph{Whitney numbers of the second
kind} of $L$ are
\[
W_k \;=\; \#\{\, x \in L \;:\; \rho(x) = k \,\},
\]
that is, the number of elements of rank $k$. Equivalently, $W_k$ is the
coefficient of $t^k$ in the rank generating function
$\sum_{x\in L} t^{\rho(x)} = \sum_{k} W_k\, t^k$. The \emph{rank number
sequence} of the conjecture is exactly the sequence $(W_0,W_1,\dots,W_r)$ of
these coefficients.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The name ``Whitney numbers'' is also used for the coefficients of the
characteristic polynomial (the \emph{first} kind). The filing specifies
``the coefficients of the rank generating function'', i.e. the number of
elements of each rank: this is the \emph{second} kind, and it is the reading we
use. The verdict is unaffected by the reading, because the first-kind Whitney
numbers are the M\"obius transform of the second-kind numbers up to sign, and the
witness's failure occurs already in the counting sequence
$(1,2,1,1)$ (see \texttt{README.md} for the alternative reading).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Log-concavity and deficit]
A finite sequence $(W_0,\dots,W_r)$ of non-negative reals is \emph{weakly
log-concave} if $W_k^{\,2} \ge W_{k-1} W_{k+1}$ for all $1\le k\le r-1$, and
\emph{strictly log-concave} if all these inequalities are strict. Its
\emph{deficit} is
\[
\operatorname{def}(W)
\;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{r-1} \max\bigl(0,\; W_{k-1}W_{k+1} - W_k^{\,2}\bigr).
\]
For the witness below only the term $k=2$ contributes, and
$\operatorname{def}(W) = W_1W_3 - W_2^{\,2} = 1 > 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Semimodularity]\label{def:semimod}
A lattice $L$ is \emph{upper semimodular} if for all $x,y\in L$,
\[
x\meet y \covers x \;\Longrightarrow\; y \covers x\join y ,
\]
where $u \covers v$ means that $v$ covers $u$ ($u < v$ and no element lies
strictly between). A lattice is \emph{semimodular} when it is upper
semimodular; every modular lattice and every distributive lattice is
semimodular, and every semimodular lattice is graded. We use ``semimodular''
in the filing's sense, i.e.\ upper semimodular.
\end{definition}

\section{The witness}\label{sec:witness}

Let $L = \{0,\,a,\,b,\,c,\,\top\}$ be the lattice whose cover relations are
\[
0 \covers a, \qquad 0 \covers b, \qquad a \covers c, \qquad b \covers c,
\qquad c \covers \top ,
\]
so that $a$ and $b$ are incomparable, $c = a \join b$, and $0,\top$ are the
bottom and top. Equivalently, $L$ is the lattice $J(P)$ of order ideals of the
poset $P$ with two incomparable minimal elements and a top. Concretely the
order relation is
\[
0 < a < c < \top, \qquad 0 < b < c < \top, \qquad a \parallel b ,
\]
and the rank function (longest chain length) is
\[
\rho(0)=0,\quad \rho(a)=\rho(b)=1,\quad \rho(c)=2,\quad \rho(\top)=3 .
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lattice}
$L$ is a distributive lattice, hence modular, hence upper semimodular. In
particular $L$ belongs to the class ``semimodular lattices'' named by the
conjecture.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$L = J(P)$ is the lattice of order ideals of a poset, with meet $=$ intersection
and join $=$ union; every such lattice is distributive (Birkhoff's
representation theorem). A distributive lattice is modular, and a modular
lattice is upper semimodular: if $x\meet y \covers x$ then
$x = x \join (x \meet y) = x \meet (x \join y)$ by modularity, so
$x \le x\join y$ with no intermediate element, i.e.\ $y \covers x \join y$
(checking directly that $y$ is a maximal element strictly below $x\join y$).
The script \texttt{reproduce.py} verifies upper semimodularity for all $25$
ordered pairs and distributivity for all $125$ triples.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Main]\label{thm:main}
$L$ is a semimodular lattice whose rank number sequence
$(W_0,W_1,W_2,W_3) = (1,2,1,1)$ is not weakly log-concave, hence not strictly
log-concave. Explicitly,
\[
W_2^{\,2} \;=\; 1 \;<\; 2 \;=\; W_1 W_3 ,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\neg\,\bigl(W_2^{\,2} \ge W_1 W_3\bigr).
\]
Consequently conjecture \texttt{00000002605} is false as stated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lattice}, $L$ is semimodular, and by the displayed ranks
above, $W_0 = 1$, $W_1 = 2$ (the elements $a,b$), $W_2 = 1$ (the element $c$),
and $W_3 = 1$ (the element $\top$). Hence
$W_2^{\,2} = 1$ while $W_1 W_3 = 2\cdot 1 = 2$, so
$W_2^{\,2} < W_1 W_3$. The inequality $W_2^{\,2} \ge W_1 W_3$ therefore fails,
and so does the strict version. This is a counterexample to the assertion that
\emph{the rank number sequences of semimodular lattices are strictly
log-concave}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The violation is as severe as possible: it occurs at the lowest nontrivial
index $k=2$, and the sequence is not even weakly log-concave, let alone
strictly. The deficit is $\operatorname{def}(W) = W_1W_3 - W_2^{\,2} = 1$.
\end{remark}

\section{The first extra clause is false}\label{sec:clause1}

The filing asserts: \emph{``the deficit of concavity is controlled by the
number of embedded N\textsubscript{5} sublattices''}. By this we take the
filing to mean that the deficit is governed by --- in particular, is a function
of, and vanishes with --- the count of embedded $N_5$ sublattices.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:clause1}
$L$ contains \textbf{zero} $N_5$ sublattices, yet has deficit $1$.
Hence the deficit is not controlled by the number of embedded $N_5$
sublattices.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$N_5$ is the $5$-element non-modular lattice (the ``pentagon''), and a lattice
is distributive if and only if it contains no sublattice isomorphic to $N_5$ or
to the Boolean lattice $B_2$ on four elements (the classical forbidden-subposet
characterisation). By Lemma~\ref{lem:lattice}, $L$ is distributive; since $L$
is a diamond-free pentagon-free poset, it contains no $N_5$ sublattice at all.
(For completeness, \texttt{reproduce.py} enumerates every $5$-element subset of
$L$, keeps those closed under meet and join, and tests order-isomorphism with
$N_5$: the count is $0$; the count of $N_5$ induced subposets is also $0$.)
On the other hand, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives deficit
$W_1W_3 - W_2^{\,2} = 2 - 1 = 1 > 0$. A positive deficit with zero $N_5$
sublattices refutes the asserted control.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is the cleanest failure of the clause: the clause predicts zero deficit
whenever the $N_5$ count is zero, but the deficit is $1$. Note also that the
witness is so small ($5$ elements) that the deficit cannot be attributed to any
large-scale structure.
\end{remark}

\section{The second extra clause is false}\label{sec:clause2}

The filing asserts: \emph{``the minimal positive deficit example is exactly the
fifth-layer embedding in the free Boolean lattice''}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:clause2}
No free Boolean lattice has any positive deficit, so it cannot contain the
minimal positive deficit example; and the minimal positive deficit example is
the $5$-element lattice $L$ of Section~\ref{sec:witness}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A free Boolean lattice $B_n$ is the lattice of subsets of an $n$-element set
under inclusion; it is distributive (hence semimodular) and graded, and its
rank-$k$ elements are the $k$-subsets, so its rank numbers are the binomial
coefficients $\binom{n}{k}$. Binomial rows are strictly log-concave:
$\binom{n}{k}^2 > \binom{n}{k-1}\binom{n}{k+1}$ for $0<k<n$ (the ratio
$\binom{n}{k}/\binom{n}{k-1} = (n-k+1)/k$ is strictly decreasing in $k$).
Hence $\operatorname{def}(B_n) = 0$ for every $n$: no free Boolean lattice
contains a positive deficit at all, and in particular no ``fifth-layer
embedding'' in one does. The script verifies $\operatorname{def}(B_n)=0$ and
strict log-concavity for all $n \le 20$ directly from the binomial rows.

Meanwhile the $5$-element lattice $L$ is semimodular with
$\operatorname{def}(L)=1 > 0$, as proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; and no
semimodular lattice with a positive deficit can have fewer than $5$ elements
(a lattice of $\le 4$ elements is a chain or a product of a chain with a
$2$-element chain, hence distributive and log-concave). So $L$ is a minimal
positive-deficit example, and it is not a Boolean layer. The clause is false.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The four-element chain]
The $4$-element chain has rank numbers $(1,1,1,1)$, for which
$W_1W_3 = W_2^{\,2} = 1$: weak log-concavity holds with equality, so it is a
degenerate violation of \emph{strict} log-concavity but has deficit $0$. It is
recorded for completeness; the genuine positive-deficit witness is $L$.
\end{remark}

\section{Formalisation}

The refutation is formalised in the Lean~4 project under \texttt{lean4/}. It is
\emph{core Lean only}: no Mathlib, and in fact \emph{no imports at all} (only
\texttt{Init}), and no \texttt{sorry}. Starting from the cover relations
$(0,1),(0,2),(1,3),(2,3),(3,4)$ on $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, the file \texttt{Main.lean}
\emph{derives} the order as the reflexive-transitive closure of the covers,
derives meet and join by search, and derives the rank as the longest
cover-chain length. It then proves, all by \texttt{decide}:

\begin{quote}\ttfamily\raggedright
theorem le\_refl / le\_antisymm / le\_trans : ... (the order is a partial order)\\
theorem le\_decomp : ... (every relation decomposes through a cover)\\
theorem meet\_is\_glb / join\_is\_lub : ... (the lattice axioms)\\
theorem rank\_cov : ... (rank increases by one along every cover)\\
theorem logConcavity\_fails : W 2 * W 2 < W 1 * W 3\\
theorem strictLogConcavity\_fails : \(\neg\) (W 1 * W 3 \(\le\) W 2 * W 2)\\
theorem upperSemimodular\_true : upperSemimodular = true\\
theorem distributive\_true : distributive = true\\
theorem not\_atomistic : joinAtoms \(\ne\) top\\
theorem conjecture\_00000002605\_false : (conjunction of the above)
\end{quote}

The script runs \texttt{lake build} (exits $0$) and \texttt{Check.lean}
(\texttt{\#print axioms}) which reports that every theorem depends only on the
core axiom \texttt{propext}; in particular no \texttt{sorryAx} appears. See
\texttt{lean4/README.md} for the statement table and the scope note.
