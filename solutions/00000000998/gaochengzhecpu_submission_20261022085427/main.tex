\begin{abstract}
The observable algebra of three qubits is $M_8(\mathbb C)$.
Its transpose map $\tau$ has ordinary induced norm one,
while its completely bounded norm is at least eight.
We give an explicit matrix amplification witnessing this
bound and a uniform upper bound of $64$, ensuring that
the cb norm is a genuine finite supremum. Thus $\sqrt2$
is not even an upper bound for the stated ratios.
Lean verifies actual Euclidean operator norms, all
amplification sizes and the concrete witness.
\end{abstract}

\section*{The norms and an actual completely bounded map}
Equip $M_8(\mathbb C)=B(\mathbb C^8)$ with the matrix
operator norm induced by the Euclidean norm. For a
complex-linear map $T:M_8\to M_8$, its ordinary norm is
\[
 \|T\|=\sup_{A\ne0}\frac{\|T(A)\|}{\|A\|}.
\]
Its amplification $T^{(k)}=\operatorname{id}_{M_k}\otimes T$
acts on each $8$ by $8$ block of $M_{8k}(\mathbb C)$.
All amplified matrix norms are also Euclidean operator
norms. The completely bounded norm is
\[
 \|T\|_{\cb}=\sup_{k\geq1}\|T^{(k)}\|
 =\sup_{k\geq1,\ X\ne0}\frac{\|T^{(k)}(X)\|}{\|X\|}.
\]
These are the usual operator-space definitions
\cite[p.~22]{paulsen}. The source imposes no restriction
to completely positive maps.

Consider $\tau(A)=A^{\mathsf T}$. Transposition preserves
the matrix operator norm: if $J$ denotes coordinatewise
complex conjugation, then $A^{\mathsf T}=JA^*J$, and $J$
is a conjugate-linear isometry. Hence $\|\tau\|=1$.

Let $E_{ij}$ be the matrix units, $1\leq i,j\leq8$.
For every $k$ and every $X\in M_{8k}$, put
$B_{ij}=I_k\otimes E_{ij}$. Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \tau^{(k)}(X)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{8}B_{ij}XB_{ij},
 \qquad \|B_{ij}\|\leq1.
\]
The factors on the two sides are the same matrix unit.
The triangle inequality and submultiplicativity imply
\[
 \|\tau^{(k)}(X)\|\leq64\|X\|\quad\text{for every }k,X.
\]
Thus $\tau$ is completely bounded and $\|\tau\|_{\cb}\leq64$.
This rough bound suffices; no sharp upper bound is needed.

\section*{A concrete amplification with norm at least eight}
Index $\mathbb C^{64}$ by pairs $(a,b)\in\{1,\ldots,8\}^2$.
Define the swap matrix and diagonal vector by
\[
 F_{(a,b),(c,d)}=\delta_{a,d}\delta_{b,c},\qquad
 \Omega_{(a,b)}=\delta_{a,b}.
\]
The matrix $F$ interchanges the two tensor factors.
It satisfies $F^*=F$ and $F^2=I_{64}$, so $\|F\|=1$.
Also $\Omega\ne0$ and $\|\Omega\|^2=8$.

Applying transposition within each block gives
\[
 Q:=\tau^{(8)}(F),\qquad
 Q_{(a,b),(c,d)}=\delta_{a,b}\delta_{c,d}.
\]
In particular $Q=\Omega\Omega^*$ and $Q\Omega=8\Omega$.
The operator norm inequality on this nonzero vector yields
\[
 8\|\Omega\|=\|Q\Omega\|\leq\|Q\|\|\Omega\|,
 \qquad \|Q\|\geq8.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{\|\tau\|_{\cb}}{\|\tau\|}
 =\|\tau\|_{\cb}\geq\frac{\|\tau^{(8)}(F)\|}{\|F\|}
 \geq8>\sqrt2.
\]

\begin{theorem}
Over the three-qubit observable algebra, $\sqrt2$ is not
an upper bound for the cb-norm to ordinary-norm ratios
of nonzero completely bounded complex-linear maps.
In particular it is not their supremum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The map $\tau$ is admissible: its ordinary norm is one
and its cb norm is finite. Its ratio is at least eight,
strictly larger than the claimed value. A supremum must
be an upper bound, so the conjecture is false.
\end{proof}

This is a standard transpose-amplification phenomenon,
not a novelty claim. The disproof needs only the certified
bounds $8\leq\|\tau\|_{\cb}\leq64$ and $\|\tau\|=1$.
It does not claim to formalize the sharp value of the
global supremum or the sharp cb norm of transposition.
The opposite algebra used in the ordinary-norm argument
does not change the target operator-space structure of
the amplified map: every amplification above acts into
the original $M_{8k}(\mathbb C)$.

\section*{Formalization and reproducibility}
The type \texttt{Obs} is the actual complex matrix algebra
on \texttt{Fin 8}.

The active Mathlib
\texttt{Matrix.L2OpNorm} structure is the operator norm
from the associated Euclidean continuous linear map.
The map \texttt{transposeMap} is a genuine complex
continuous linear map. Its ordinary norm-one theorem
uses transposition as a star-algebra equivalence to the
opposite algebra solely to prove its level-one isometry.

The function \texttt{amplify} explicitly applies that map
to each block, using matrix indices
\texttt{Fin k} times \texttt{Fin 8}. The all-$k$ bound
is proved by the matrix-unit identity above. The swap,
its unitarity, the nonzero Euclidean vector $\Omega$ and
the actual matrix-vector eigen-equation are checked in
Lean. They prove the lower bound using the actual
Euclidean matrix operator norm.

The definition \texttt{cbNorm} is the supremum of all
amplification norm ratios. The zero input and the
zero-dimensional amplification contribute only zero.
The witness makes the set nonempty and the $64$ bound
makes it bounded above, so no empty-set or unbounded-set
default value is used. The set \texttt{cbRatios} includes
only nonzero maps with bounded amplification ratios.
The theorem \texttt{not\_claimed\_supremum} directly
negates an \texttt{IsLUB} certificate at $\sqrt2$;
the stronger theorem \texttt{not\_upperBound} also holds.
