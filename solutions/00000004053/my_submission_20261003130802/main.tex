\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} the prime factors of the minimal period of a
periodic module always divide prime factors of the order of the
defect group, and every power of every admissible prime is realized
in some explicit block.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Take the group algebra $kC_3$ over a field of characteristic $3$:
by Jennings, $kC_3 \cong \mathbb{F}_3[x]/(x^3)$ with $x = g - 1$
(since $(g-1)^3 = g^3 - 1 = 0$).  The $\Omega$-resolution of the
trivial module $k$:
\[
\Omega(k) = \ker(kC_3 \to k) = (x), \quad \dim = 2;
\qquad
\Omega^2(k) = \ker(kC_3 \to (x)) = (x^2), \quad \dim = 1,
\]
and $g \cdot x^2 = x^2 + x^3 = x^2$ (trivial action, since
$x^3 = 0$): $\Omega^2(k) \cong k$.  So $k$ is periodic with minimal
period $2$ (not $1$: $\dim \Omega(k) = 2 \ne 1$).  The unique block
has defect group $C_3$ of order $3$: the period's prime factor
$\{2\}$ does not divide the defect order's prime factors $\{3\}$
($2 \nmid 3$) --- the correspondence fails at its core.  This is the
classical period-2 phenomenon: over $kC_p$ every non-projective
module has period dividing $2$, whatever the defect $p$.  The
powers clause collapses with it: $kC_3$ has radical cube zero, so
$\Omega^2 = \mathrm{id}$ on every non-projective module and every
period is $1$ or $2$ --- the powers $4, 8$ of the admissible prime
$2$ are realized in \emph{no} block.
