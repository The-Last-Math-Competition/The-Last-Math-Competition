# Disproof of conjecture `00000002051`

**Verdict: FALSE.**

The conjecture claims that the fine spectrum of the semigroup variety
`⟨x² = x⟩` is the closed form `f(n) = 2ⁿ·C_n`. The variety `⟨x² = x⟩` is the
variety of **bands** (idempotent semigroups), and `f(n)` is defined to be the
cardinality of the `n`-generated free algebra. The formula fails already at
`n = 1`: the free band on one generator is the singleton `{x}`, so `f(1) = 1`,
whereas the formula gives `2¹·C₁ = 2·1 = 2`. A second, independent failure at
`n = 2` (`f(2) = 6`, formula gives `8`) rules out any attempt to repair the
claim by shifting the range of `n`.

## The conjecture, quoted verbatim

From `conjectures/00000002051.md`:

> **English.** Definition: The fine spectrum f_V(n) of a variety V is the
> cardinality of its n-generated free algebra. Conjecture: The fine spectrum of
> the semigroup variety ⟨x² = x⟩ is the explicit closed form f(n) = 2^{n}·C_n
> (C_n the Catalan numbers); the spectrum is controlled by the
> connected-component count of band semigroups.
>
> **中文。** 定义：簇 V 的细谱 f_V(n) 指 n 元自由代数的基数。猜想：半群簇
> ⟨x²=x⟩ 的细谱为 f(n) = 2^{n}· C_n(C_n 为 Catalan 数)的显式闭式;谱由带状半群的
> 连通分量计数控制。

## What the terms mean here

- **Fine spectrum.** The file defines it explicitly: "the cardinality of its
  `n`-generated free algebra". So `f_V(n) = |F_V(n)|`, the number of elements of
  the free algebra of the variety `V` on `n` generators.
- **The variety `⟨x² = x⟩`.** A semigroup satisfying `x² = x` for every element
  is exactly an **idempotent semigroup**, i.e. a **band**. The file confirms the
  reading in its own last clause ("band semigroups"). Hence `F_V(n)` is the
  **free band on `n` generators**.
- **Range of `n`.** The definition speaks of the `n`-generated free algebra;
  the natural range is `n ≥ 1` (free algebras on `n` generators for
  `n = 1, 2, 3, …`). The cheapest witness, `n = 1`, already refutes the claim on
  this range.

## Failure at `n = 1` (the cheapest witness)

The free band on the one generator `x` is the free semigroup on `{x}` modulo
the band identities. Every word in the single letter `x` is `x`, `xx`, `xxx`,
…, and the band identity `x·x = x` collapses all of them:

```
x = x,   xx = x,   xxx = x,   …
```

So the free band on one generator is `{x}`, a singleton:

```
|F_⟨x²=x⟩(1)| = f(1) = 1 .
```

The conjecture's formula gives

```
2¹·C₁ = 2·1 = 2 ≠ 1 = f(1) .
```

This is an unconditional refutation of the formula as stated for `n ≥ 1`.
Formally (see `lean4/`), for **any** band `B` and **any** element `x ∈ B`, the
subsemigroup generated by `x` is the singleton `{x}`: a product of two elements
that both equal `x` is `x·x = x` (idempotency). Hence the free band on one
generator, which is generated by one element, has exactly one element.

## Failure at `n = 2` (independent verification)

The free band on the two generators `a, b` is the quotient of the free
semigroup `{a,b}⁺` by the congruence generated by `u·u = u` for all words `u`
(the general idempotency law, i.e. the free-band identities). Reducing words by
`u·u → u` repeatedly, the irreducible (reduced) words over `{a,b}` are exactly

```
a,   b,   ab,   ba,   aba,   bab          (6 words).
```

Why these are all: over a 2-letter alphabet **every word of length ≥ 4 contains
a square factor** `ss` (two adjacent equal blocks), so by the band identity it
reduces strictly; a finite case check confirms it for length 4 and induction
then handles all longer words. The square-free words of length ≤ 3 over `{a,b}`
are precisely the six words above (length 1: `a,b`; length 2: `ab,ba`; length 3:
`aba,bab`; words such as `aa`, `bb`, `aab`, `abb`, `aaa`, `bbb`, … all contain a
square). Hence **`f(2) = 6`**, a classical fact (Howie, *Fundamentals of
Semigroup Theory*, Oxford 1995, p. 123; OEIS A030449).

The induced multiplication table on the six canonical words (entry = reduce of
the concatenation) is:

| `·`   | `a`   | `b`   | `ab`  | `ba`  | `aba` | `bab` |
|:------|:------|:------|:------|:------|:------|:------|
| `a`   | `a`   | `ab`  | `ab`  | `aba` | `aba` | `ab`  |
| `b`   | `ba`  | `b`   | `bab` | `ba`  | `ba`  | `bab` |
| `ab`  | `aba` | `ab`  | `ab`  | `aba` | `aba` | `ab`  |
| `ba`  | `ba`  | `bab` | `bab` | `ba`  | `ba`  | `bab` |
| `aba` | `aba` | `ab`  | `ab`  | `aba` | `aba` | `ab`  |
| `bab` | `ba`  | `bab` | `bab` | `ba`  | `ba`  | `bab` |

`reproduce.py` verifies by exhaustive computation that this table is

- **closed** — every entry is one of the six words;
- **associative** — `u·(v·w) = (u·v)·w` for all 6³ = 216 triples;
- **idempotent** — all six diagonal entries are the word itself.

A semigroup that is associative and idempotent is a band, and this band is
generated by the two elements `a, b`; by the universal property of the free band
it is a quotient of the free band on two generators, so `f(2) ≥ 6`. Conversely
every word over `{a,b}` reduces (by band identities) to one of the six, so every
element of the free band is one of the six and `f(2) ≤ 6`. Therefore
`f(2) = 6`. The conjecture's formula gives

```
2²·C₂ = 4·2 = 8 ≠ 6 = f(2) .
```

This second failure is independent of the `n = 1` witness.

## True free-band values versus the formula

The number of elements of the free band on `n` generators is OEIS
**A030449** (author: Marcel Jackson), whose reference is J. Howie,
*Fundamentals of Semigroup Theory*, Oxford University Press 1995, p. 123. The
values are much larger than `2ⁿ·C_n` from `n = 3` on (and different at `n = 1,2`
as well):

| `n` | free band `f(n)` (A030449) | formula `2ⁿ·C_n` | equal? |
|----:|---------------------------:|-----------------:|:-------|
| 1   | 1                          | 2                | no |
| 2   | 6                          | 8                | no |
| 3   | 159                        | 40               | no |
| 4   | 332380                     | 224              | no |

(`C₁, C₂, C₃, C₄ = 1, 2, 5, 14`.) The formula `2ⁿ·C_n` is *not* the free-band
spectrum: it overcounts for `n = 1, 2` and drastically undercounts for `n ≥ 3`.
The true sequence is `1, 6, 159, 332380, 2751884514765, …`, not `2, 8, 40,
224`.

## Alternative readings tested

The refutation survives every reading we could construct:

- **(a) "`⟨x² = x⟩` means something else."** No: a semigroup with `x² = x` is an
  idempotent semigroup = a band, and the file itself says "band semigroups". The
  reading "free object written `⟨x | x² = x⟩`" is the *same* object (the free
  band), because a semigroup presentation has a single generator `x` there too:
  its free object on one generator is still `{x}`. So both readings agree and
  both are refuted by `f(1) = 1 ≠ 2`.
- **(b) "`f(n)` counts irreducible/maximal words, not algebra elements."** The
  definition explicitly says "cardinality of its `n`-generated free algebra".
  Even under this alternative reading the numbers are the same as the free-band
  counts for `n = 1, 2` (irreducible words: `1` and `6`), so the claim still
  fails (`1 ≠ 2`, `6 ≠ 8`).
- **(c) "`n` starts at 2."** Then `f(2) = 6 ≠ 8 = 2²·C₂`: still false. Shifting
  further does not help: `f(3) = 159 ≠ 40`, `f(4) = 332380 ≠ 224`, and in fact
  `2ⁿ·C_n` and A030449 never agree for `n ≥ 1` (A030449 grows far faster).
- **(d) "Adjoin an identity to the free band."** Under this non-standard reading
  `f(1) = 2 = 2¹·C₁` matches, but `f(2) = 6 + 1 = 7 ≠ 8 = 2²·C₂`, so the claim
  still fails. (The conjecture speaks of a *semigroup* variety, where no
  identity is adjoined, so reading (c) is not the intended one anyway.)
- **(e) `n = 0`.** If `n` is allowed to start at `0`, the free semigroup (hence
  free band) on the empty set is empty, `f(0) = 0`, while the formula gives
  `2⁰·C₀ = 1`. (If one instead declares the `0`-generated free band trivial,
  then `n = 0` happens to match; the failures at `n = 1` and `n = 2` are then
  unaffected.)

**No reading saves the claim.** The refutation is unconditional. The cheapest
formalisable witness is `n = 1`, which by itself suffices because the formula is
asserted as a closed form for all `n ≥ 1`; `reproduce.py` and `lean4/` certify
both `n = 1` and `n = 2`.

## Caveats

- The `n = 1` witness uses the standard definition of the free algebra of a
  **semigroup** variety: no identity element is adjoined. Under the
  (non-standard) "adjoined identity" reading, `n = 1` coincidentally matches, but
  `n = 2` fails (`7 ≠ 8`), so the conjecture is false under that reading too.
- We do **not** claim `2ⁿ·C_n` is meaningless. It is a genuine sequence
  (`2, 8, 40, 224, …`), but it is not the band fine spectrum. In particular it is
  not the classical free-band count.
- The `n ≥ 4` values in the table are taken from the cited literature (OEIS
  A030449); only `f(1) = 1` and `f(2) = 6` are re-derived here from first
  principles. The refutation needs only `n = 1` (and, for the independent second
  witness, `n = 2`).
- The conjunction "the spectrum is controlled by the connected-component count
  of band semigroups" is vague; nothing in the disproof depends on interpreting
  it, because the explicit closed form `f(n) = 2ⁿ·C_n` fails on its own.

## Reproducing

Python (dependency-free, runs in well under a second):

```sh
python3 reproduce.py
```

It enumerates all words over `{x}` and over `{a,b}` up to length 8, reduces them
by `u·u → u` to a fixed point, and asserts:

1. `n = 1` yields exactly one class, `{x}`; `f(1) = 1 ≠ 2 = 2¹·C₁`.
2. `n = 2` yields exactly six classes `{a, b, ab, ba, aba, bab}`; the induced
   multiplication table is closed, associative and idempotent; `f(2) = 6 ≠ 8 =
   2²·C₂`.
3. every word of length `4…12` over two letters has a square factor (the
   reduction mechanism).
4. the literature values `1, 6, 159, 332380` (A030449) differ from
   `2ⁿ·C_n = 2, 8, 40, 224` for `n = 1, 2, 3, 4`.
5. the alternative readings (irreducible-word counts, adjoined identity) also
   fail to match `2ⁿ·C_n`.

A `FAIL` (non-zero exit status) means a claimed fact did not verify.

LaTeX document:

```sh
tectonic -o build main.tex
```

Lean 4 project:

```sh
cd lean4
lake build
lake env lean Check.lean
```
