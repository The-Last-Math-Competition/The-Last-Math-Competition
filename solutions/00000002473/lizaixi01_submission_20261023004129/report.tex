\section{The necessary clause being refuted}
The English statement includes the explicit assertion that
``the two statistics agree outside the exceptional classes.''
In its stated Ramanujan-congruence context, the exceptional sizes are
\[
 n\equiv4\pmod5,\qquad n\equiv5\pmod7,\qquad n\equiv6\pmod{11}.
\]
The integer $n=2$ lies in none of these classes. We prove disagreement
both for individual partitions and for the complete distributions,
including distributions reduced modulo each of $5,7,11$. Thus no
choice between these usual meanings of agreement repairs this clause.
The accompanying Chinese text is unclear; the original bilingual
source is preserved without alteration, and this counterexample
addresses the clear English necessary clause. The further,
unspecified near-bijective refinement cannot make a false necessary
clause true and is not needed for the contradiction.

\section{Standard definitions and a complete example}
For a partition $\lambda$, let $L(\lambda)$ be its largest part,
$\ell(\lambda)$ its number of parts, $o(\lambda)$ its number of ones,
and $\mu(\lambda)$ the number of parts strictly greater than
$o(\lambda)$. The usual statistics are
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(\lambda)=L(\lambda)-\ell(\lambda),\qquad
 \operatorname{crank}(\lambda)=
 \begin{cases}
  L(\lambda),&o(\lambda)=0,\\
  \mu(\lambda)-o(\lambda),&o(\lambda)>0.
 \end{cases}
\]
There are exactly two partitions of two, with the following values:
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r|r}
\lambda &L&\ell&\operatorname{rank}&\operatorname{crank}\\\hline
(2)&2&1&1&2\\
(1,1)&1&2&-1&-2
\end{array}
\]
The rank distribution therefore has values $-1,1$, once each,
whereas the crank distribution has values $-2,2$, once each.
In particular, exactly one partition has rank one and none has
crank one. Modulo $5$, $7$ or $11$, exactly one partition has
rank congruent to one and none has crank congruent to one.

These definitions and the three Ramanujan classes are recorded in
\href{https://www.mi.uni-koeln.de/Bringmann/BMRFinalPublication.pdf}
{Bringmann--Mahlburg--Rhoades, \emph{Asymptotics for rank and crank
moments}}, introduction, equations (1.2)--(1.3).
The familiar exceptional generating-function convention at $n=1$
is irrelevant: this example uses actual partitions of $n=2$.

\section{Formal objects and exhaustive classification}
Lean uses Mathlib's \texttt{Nat.Partition n}, whose parts are a
multiset of positive natural numbers with sum $n$. The largest
part is the maximum of the actual parts; rank subtracts the
multiset cardinality in $\mathbf Z$. The crank counts actual ones
and actual parts greater than that count, also subtracting in
$\mathbf Z$. Negative values are not truncated by natural-number
subtraction.

The definitions \texttt{two} and \texttt{oneOne} are genuine members
of \texttt{Nat.Partition 2}. The theorem
\texttt{partition\_two\_classification} proves they exhaust that
type. If a part two occurs, its remaining parts have sum zero and
positivity forces them to be absent. Otherwise every positive
part is one; the sum forces exactly two such parts. Extensionality
of the partition type identifies the two cases with the named
objects. Their distinctness is proved from their different part
counts. The theorem \texttt{univ\_partition\_two} consequently
identifies the full finite universe with these two objects.

The definitions \texttt{distribution} and
\texttt{residueDistribution} count filtered subsets of this full
universe, for arbitrary sizes and integer statistics. The four
rank/crank value theorems are computed from the definitions.
The integer and modular count theorems then use the exhaustive
universe equality. These are counts over all partitions, not over
an assumed or incomplete test list.

\section{Formal negations}
The predicate \texttt{RamanujanExceptional} is exactly the union
of the three size residue classes above.
\texttt{two\_nonexceptional} proves that two avoids all of them.
The final theorems negate each of the following universal claims:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Outside the exceptional sizes, rank equals crank on every
individual partition.
\item Outside those sizes, the full integer-valued rank and crank
distributions are equal.
\item Outside those sizes, their residue distributions are equal
for all three moduli $5,7,11$.
\end{enumerate}
The explicit value-one discrepancy supplies the latter two
contradictions. Nothing here contradicts the actual Ramanujan
congruences or claims that rank and crank should be interchangeable
in the established theory.
