1. Exact mathematical target

Both source languages assert the best L1 polynomial approximation rate of |x| on [-1,1] is E_n^* ~ B/n, with additional claims concerning closed form and transcendence of B. Approximation.lean:10 defines
  l1Error(p) = integral_-1^1 |abs(x) - p.eval(x)| dx.
The outer absolute value is present. This is the ordinary unweighted real Lebesgue L1 error on the specified interval, not a signed error, a uniform norm, a coefficient norm, a weighted norm, or a numerical surrogate. The fixed integration endpoints are ordered. For every real polynomial, the residual and its absolute value are continuous, so the integral is the usual finite integral; no nonintegrability convention is being exploited.

Approximation.lean:13 defines bestError(n) as the real infimum of exactly the set of l1Error(p) over all real polynomials with p.natDegree<=n. It does not restrict the approximating class to even polynomials, the constructed family, nonnegative polynomials, sampled data, or a caller-provided family. The particular even family is used only to upper-bound this full infimum. The zero polynomial remains admissible even at n=0 under the usual natDegree convention.

Main.lean:73 defines HasInverseLinearRate(B) by B!=0 and convergence of bestError(n)/(B/n) to 1 as n tends to infinity through the naturals. This is the standard ratio meaning of the displayed asymptotic equivalence. The final theorem at Main.lean:77 is
  not exists B : Real, HasInverseLinearRate(B).
It therefore rejects every nonzero real comparison constant, not just a particular value or positive constants. Exclusion of B=0 is faithful to ratio asymptotic equivalence, which requires a nonzero comparison sequence eventually. Lean's total division assigns a value at n=0, but changing or defining that single initial term does not affect a limit at infinity. The proof explicitly handles n=0 and does not confuse it with the positive large-degree regime.

Refuting this necessary rate clause disproves the conjunction as written in both languages. The source review does not assert a decision about the ancillary transcendence/closed-form claims for a differently defined constant. No change of conjecture, approximation class, or norm is needed for the displayed disproof.
