\section*{Statement}

Conjecture 00000000013 claims that for every integer $n\geq 3$ there is a
prime $p$ in the half-open interval
\[
  2^n \leq p < 2^{n+1}
\]
whose binary expansion contains exactly three $1$'s.

We disprove the conjecture by taking $n=8$.

\section*{The counterexample}

Every integer in $[2^8,2^9)$ with exactly three $1$'s in its binary
expansion has the form
\[
  2^8+2^a+2^b,
  \qquad 0\leq b<a<8.
\]
There are $\binom{8}{2}=28$ such numbers. They are listed below, together
with a proper divisor. Thus every one is composite.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrrrrr}
\toprule
$n$ & divisor & $n$ & divisor & $n$ & divisor & $n$ & divisor\\
\midrule
259 & 7  & 261 & 3  & 262 & 2  & 265 & 5\\
266 & 2  & 268 & 2  & 273 & 3  & 274 & 2\\
276 & 2  & 280 & 2  & 289 & 17 & 290 & 2\\
292 & 2  & 296 & 2  & 304 & 2  & 321 & 3\\
322 & 2  & 324 & 2  & 328 & 2  & 336 & 2\\
352 & 2  & 385 & 5  & 386 & 2  & 388 & 2\\
392 & 2  & 400 & 2  & 416 & 2  & 448 & 2\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For completeness, the corresponding factorizations include
\[
\begin{aligned}
259&=7\cdot37, & 261&=3\cdot87, & 262&=2\cdot131, & 265&=5\cdot53,\\
266&=2\cdot133, & 268&=2\cdot134, & 273&=3\cdot91, & 274&=2\cdot137,\\
276&=2\cdot138, & 280&=2\cdot140, & 289&=17^2, & 290&=2\cdot145,\\
292&=2\cdot146, & 296&=2\cdot148, & 304&=2\cdot152, & 321&=3\cdot107,\\
322&=2\cdot161, & 324&=2\cdot162, & 328&=2\cdot164, & 336&=2\cdot168,\\
352&=2\cdot176, & 385&=5\cdot77, & 386&=2\cdot193, & 388&=2\cdot194,\\
392&=2\cdot196, & 400&=2\cdot200, & 416&=2\cdot208, & 448&=2\cdot224.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence there is no prime in $[2^8,2^9)$ whose binary expansion has exactly
three $1$'s. Since the conjecture asserts this for every $n\geq3$, the
single counterexample $n=8$ disproves it.

\section*{Formal verification}

The accompanying Lean 4 project formalizes the same finite verification.
It defines $\Composite(N)$ to mean that there is a divisor $d$ with
$1<d<N$ and $d\mid N$. A finite computation verifies that every pair of
exponents $0\leq b<a<8$ gives a composite number
$2^8+2^a+2^b$. The final Lean theorem derives the negation of the
conjecture's assertion at $n=8$. The project uses Lean 4.33.1 and only
its standard library.
