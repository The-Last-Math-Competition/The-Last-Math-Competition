\section*{The conjecture}
\begin{quote}
The spectral distribution of an exchangeable random graph limit is the
spectral measure of its graphon integral operator.  \emph{Conjecture:} The
characteristic function of the expected spectral measure is given explicitly
by the \emph{logarithm} of the Fredholm determinant of the kernel, and the
determinant series converges \emph{if and only if} the kernel is square
integrable.
\end{quote}

Write the claim as the conjunction
\[
\mathrm{(a)}\quad \varphi(t)=\log\det(I-tT_K)\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathrm{(b)}\quad \text{Fredholm series converges}\iff K\in L^2 .
\]
We refute the conjunction: conjunct (a) fails for \emph{every} kernel
(already at $t=0$), and the ``only if'' direction of (b) also fails on a
rank-one kernel.  The Lean development formalizes (a) in full.

\section*{The model}
An exchangeable random-graph limit is a graphon $W$, whose integral operator
$T_K f(x)=\int K(x,y)f(y)\,dy$ is a self-adjoint Hilbert--Schmidt operator
with eigenvalues $(\lambda_j)$.  The \emph{expected spectral measure} is the
empirical eigenvalue law, and its characteristic function is
\[
\varphi(t)=\int e^{it\lambda}\,d\mu(\lambda)
=\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{(it)^n}{n!}\,m_n,\qquad m_n=\operatorname{Tr}T^n .
\]
We discretize on a finite probability space $\mathrm{Fin}\,m$ with uniform
measure: the kernel is a symmetric matrix $K$ and $T=(1/m)K$.

\section*{Refutation of (a)}
The conjectured identity $\varphi(t)=\log\det(I-tT)$ is evaluated at $t=0$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\varphi(0)=\mu(\mathbb R)=1$: \emph{every} characteristic function of
a probability measure equals $1$ at $0$.  For the finite model
$\varphi(0)=(1/m)\operatorname{Tr}I=1$.
\item $\det(I-0\cdot T)=\det I=1$, hence $\log\det(I-0\,T)=0$ for \emph{every}
kernel.
\end{itemize}
Thus $\varphi(0)=1\ne 0=\log\det(I)$ on the complete graphon
($m=1$, $K\equiv 1$): the identity fails already at the expansion point, for
any kernel.  No normalizing convention repairs it --- a characteristic
function is $1$ at $0$, a log-determinant is $0$ there.

Even at nonzero $t$ the two sides cannot coincide: $|\varphi(t)|\le 1$ for
all real $t$ (it is a characteristic function), whereas
$\operatorname{Re}\log\det(I-tT)=\log\prod_j|1-t\lambda_j|$ is unbounded
above as $|t|\to\infty$ whenever $T$ has a nonzero eigenvalue.
Numerically, for a random $4\times4$ symmetric kernel,
$\varphi(20)\approx 0.118-0.071i$ (modulus $<1$) while
$\operatorname{Re}\log\det(I-20T)\approx +6.74$.

\section*{Refutation of (b), ``only if''}
The Fredholm determinant series needs only the coefficient integrals
$a_n=\frac1{n!}\int\det[K(x_i,x_j)]\,dx_1\cdots dx_n$.  For a rank-one kernel
$K(x,y)=u(x)v(y)$ every $n\times n$ determinant with $n\ge 2$ vanishes, so
\[
\det(I-zT)=1-z\!\int\! u(x)v(x)\,dx,
\]
a polynomial, hence convergent for all $z$.  Taking $u=1$,
$v(y)=y^{-3/4}$ on $[0,1]$ gives $\int v=4$ but
$\int\!\!\int K^2=\int v^2=\int_0^1 y^{-3/2}dy=+\infty$: the kernel is
\emph{not} square integrable, yet its Fredholm series converges (it equals
$1-4z$).  ``Converges $\Rightarrow$ square integrable'' is therefore false.
