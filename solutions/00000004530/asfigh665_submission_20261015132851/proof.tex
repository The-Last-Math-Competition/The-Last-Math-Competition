\begin{abstract}
Conjecture 00000004530 is true. We give an explicit family of synchronous
message-passing algorithms on a graph consisting of one edge. The damping
schedule has one averaging step every $p$ updates, so its averaging frequency
is $1/p$. Every member has the same fixed-point set and reaches consensus
in at most $p$ updates. The cases $p=1$ and $p=2$ have different trajectories
from the same initial messages. All claims needed for the construction are
verified in a standalone Lean 4 project.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and interpretation}
The English statement of Conjecture 00000004530 is:
\begin{quote}
There exist two algorithms with the same fixed-point set but different
convergence trajectories, and the separation is constructed by an explicit
frequency-sweep family of damping parameters.
\end{quote}
The Chinese statement has the same existential content. Neither version
specifies a particular message alphabet, graph, or continuous frequency
range. We supply a discrete frequency sweep: the period $p$ runs over all
positive integers, and the averaging frequency runs over $1,1/2,1/3,\ldots$.
For a time-dependent algorithm, a fixed state means a state left unchanged
by every scheduled update. In the construction below, this also equals the
fixed-point set of each individual update and of the one-period update.
No uniqueness of the limit, or different limiting values, is asserted by
the conjecture: it asks for different convergence trajectories.

\section{The message-passing family}
Take two vertices joined by an edge, and let $a,b$ be their two directed
messages. The undamped synchronous update copies the incoming message:
\[
  F(a,b)=(b,a).
\]
Use the invariant message space
\[
 \mathcal M=\{(c+d,c-d):c,d\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Equivalently, $a,b$ are integers of equal parity. The coordinates $(c,d)$
are unique: $c=(a+b)/2$ and $d=(a-b)/2$.
Thus the two-node update is an ordinary local message-passing operation,
and the coordinate representation is injective.

For each integer $p\geq1$, define, for update indices $n\geq0$,
\[
 \alpha^{(p)}_n=
 \begin{cases}
  1/2,&p\mid n+1,\\
  1,&p\nmid n+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The algorithm $A_p$ performs the usual damped update
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iteration}
 (a_{n+1},b_{n+1})
 =(1-\alpha^{(p)}_n)(a_n,b_n)
       +\alpha^{(p)}_n(b_n,a_n).
\end{equation}
All coefficients are valid damping parameters in $(0,1]$. At a full update,
$(a,b)$ is swapped. At a half update, both messages become $(a+b)/2$,
which is an integer because the input lies in $\mathcal M$; the output
again lies in $\mathcal M$. There is no integer rounding.

The schedule is periodic with period $p$. Its half-damped updates occur
exactly at $n=p-1,2p-1,3p-1,\ldots$. For $p\geq2$ the period is fundamental:
a smaller positive period would have to send the half-damped index $p-1$
to a non-half-damped index. Consequently there is exactly one half update
per $p$ updates. Varying $p$ gives the promised explicit frequency sweep.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fixed}
For every $p\geq1$ the fixed-point set of $A_p$ is
\[
 \operatorname{Fix}(A_p)=\{(c,c):c\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Every individual scheduled update has this same fixed-point set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a swap, $(b,a)=(a,b)$ if and only if $a=b$. For an averaging step,
$((a+b)/2,(a+b)/2)=(a,b)$ if and only if $a=b$. Both directions hold on
$\mathcal M$. Therefore a message pair is unchanged by every scheduled
update if and only if it is a consensus pair $(c,c)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:convergence}
Starting from $(a_0,b_0)=(c+d,c-d)$, algorithm $A_p$ satisfies
\[
 (a_n,b_n)=
 \begin{cases}
 (c+(-1)^n d,c-(-1)^n d),&0\leq n<p,\\
 (c,c),&n\geq p.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular every trajectory converges, and it reaches its limit after
at most $p$ updates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In coordinates, the average $c$ is preserved. Each full update sends
$d$ to $-d$, and each half update sends $d$ to $0$. The first $p-1$
updates are full; update $p$ is half. Subsequent updates preserve $d=0$.
These statements prove the displayed formula by induction. Eventual
equality to $(c,c)$ implies convergence in the usual Euclidean metric,
as well as in the discrete topology on $\mathcal M$.
\end{proof}
The one-period update therefore maps every $(c+d,c-d)$ to $(c,c)$,
so its fixed points are also exactly those of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed}.

\begin{theorem}
The algorithms $A_1$ and $A_2$ prove Conjecture 00000004530.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
They are two members of the explicit damping-frequency family above.
Their fixed-point sets are identical by Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed}, and all
of their trajectories converge by Lemma~\ref{lem:convergence}.
From the same initial messages $(1,-1)$ their trajectories begin
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &n=0&n=1&n=2&n\geq3\\\hline
 A_1&(1,-1)&(0,0)&(0,0)&(0,0)\\
 A_2&(1,-1)&(-1,1)&(0,0)&(0,0).
\end{array}
\]
The states at $n=1$ are different, which proves the required separation
of convergence trajectories.
\end{proof}

\section{Lean verification and correspondence}
The project uses Lean 4.24.0 and imports only \texttt{Std}. The entry point
is \texttt{lean/Main.lean}; from the \texttt{lean} directory run
\texttt{lake build}. The proof contains no proof placeholders, externally
trusted computation, or additional axioms.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Mathematical object or assertion & Lean declaration\\\hline
$2\alpha^{(p)}_n$ & \texttt{twiceDamping}\\
Coordinate change $(c,d)\mapsto(c+d,c-d)$ & \texttt{messages}\\
Injectivity of coordinates & \texttt{messages\_injective}\\
Exact damped local update \eqref{eq:iteration} & \texttt{message\_passing\_identity}\\
Periodicity and reset locations & \texttt{damping\_periodic}, \texttt{first\_reset},\\
 & \texttt{before\_reset}\\
Fixed-point set & \texttt{state\_fixed\_iff}\\
Actual iteration recurrence & \texttt{trajectory\_recurrence}\\
Finite-time consensus for every initial state & \texttt{consensus}\\
Existential separation & \texttt{conjecture4530}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The damping identity is denominator-cleared by multiplication by $2$;
its right-hand side uses the physical messages themselves. It therefore
verifies genuine convex damping, not merely an unrelated scalar recurrence.
The main theorem quantifies over every integer coordinate state, proves
agreement of the fixed sets, proves eventual equality to consensus for
both algorithms, and proves inequality of their physical messages after
one update from the common initial state. The printed axiom audit lists
only Lean's standard foundational axioms
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
